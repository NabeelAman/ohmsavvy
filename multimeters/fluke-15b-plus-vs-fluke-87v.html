Fluke 15B+ vs 87V

Fluke 15B+ vs 87V spans Fluke's range from its plainest full-size meter to its industrial standard. Both read to 1000 V and both have microamp ranges, which is where the similarity ends. The 87V is True RMS, CAT IV 600V and CAT III 1000V, ten times tighter on DC volts, with frequency, temperature, a drive filter and peak capture. The 15B+ is a 4,000-count volts, amps and ohms meter with a CAT III 600V rating, no frequency or temperature at all and a one-year warranty.

Both say 1000 V. Only one is rated for it on mains

The 15B+ has a 1000 V DC range and Fluke gives it CAT II 1000V, so on paper both meters cover 1000 V. The category attached to that number is what differs. CAT II covers receptacle-level circuits and plug-in equipment. CAT III covers panels, feeders and fixed wiring, and CAT IV the service entrance.

In practice: on 120/240 V residential circuits and a 277 V lighting circuit, both meters are inside their CAT III 600V rating, and the 15B+ is a legitimate tool. On a 480 V three-phase panel, both are still within 600 V, but only the 87V is rated for the service-entrance side. Above 600 V, in industrial switchgear or equipment running DC buses above 600 V, the 15B+ is outside its CAT III rating, and only the 87V should go on the circuit.

Check the category, not just the dial

A meter's highest range tells you what it can display, not where it is safe to use. Before probing anything above 600 V or upstream of the main panel, confirm the meter and leads carry the right CAT rating, wear the PPE your site requires, and if the work is outside your training, hand it to a qualified electrician. See CAT III vs CAT IV for the details.

What the 15B+ can't measure at all

The 15B+ is the 17B+ with functions removed. It has no frequency, duty cycle, temperature, MIN MAX or relative mode, and Fluke doesn't claim True RMS. Every one of those is on the 87V. That turns into specific jobs the 15B+ can't do:

Where the 15B+ holds its own: microamps

Some Flukes skip small currents entirely: the 115 and 106 stop at 1 mA steps. The 15B+ doesn't: it has six current ranges from 400.0 µA to 10.00 A, with a separate µA/mA jack fused at 440 mA. Its 400.0 µA range steps in 0.1 µA, ten times coarser than the 87V's 0.01 µA but fine enough for a standby-current check on a battery gadget or a microcontroller board.

Worked on a 5.000 V reference: the 15B+ uses its 40.00 V range, because 5 V is above its 4.000 V range, so it shows 5.00 V and allows 0.025 V plus 3 × 0.01 V, or ±0.055 V. The 87V shows 5.000 V on its 6 V range and allows 0.0025 V plus 0.001 V, or ±0.0035 V. For checking that a 5 V rail is present, both answer the question. For checking a voltage reference or a regulator's tolerance, only the 87V has the resolution.

A good first Fluke, not a cheap 87V

The 15B+ suits a student, a hobbyist or an apprentice who needs volts, amps including microamps, ohms and capacitance on a meter that is CAT III 600V. It isn't a budget way into industrial work. For that, the 87V's ratings, filter and capture are the point of buying it.

The bottom line

The Fluke 87V is the meter for industrial and drive work: True RMS, CAT III 1000V / CAT IV 600V, ±(0.05% + 1) DC accuracy, frequency, temperature, a low-pass filter, Peak Capture and a lifetime warranty. The Fluke 15B+ is a basic CAT III 600V meter with µA to 10 A ranges and no frequency, temperature or True RMS claim, suited to students and home or bench use.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Fluke 15B+ good enough for an electrician?+

For residential and light commercial circuits within CAT III 600V it measures volts, amps and ohms correctly on sine-wave loads. It lacks True RMS, frequency, temperature, LoZ and non-contact voltage, which most working electricians use. Fluke's 115, 117 or 87V suit daily trade work better.

Can the Fluke 15B+ measure 1000 volts?+

It has a 1000 V DC range, but Fluke rates it CAT II 1000V and CAT III 600V. That means it is rated for receptacle-level circuits up to 1000 V but for panels and fixed wiring only to 600 V. The 87V is rated CAT III 1000V and CAT IV 600V.

Does the Fluke 15B+ measure microamps?+

Yes. It has 400.0 µA and 4000 µA ranges with 0.1 µA resolution, plus 40 mA, 400 mA, 4 A and 10 A ranges, all specified at ±(1.5% + 3). The 87V resolves 0.01 µA. Both suit standby-current and parasitic-drain checks.

Why is the Fluke 87V so much more expensive than the 15B+?+

It adds a higher CAT rating, True RMS, a 19,999-count mode, ten times tighter DC accuracy, frequency to 200 kHz, temperature with a probe, a drive filter, 250 µs Peak Capture and a lifetime warranty. The 15B+ has a one-year warranty and only basic functions. These figures are Fluke's specifications.

Is the Fluke 15B+ True RMS?+

Fluke doesn't describe it as True RMS on its product page or datasheet. Its AC readings are reliable on sine-wave mains but not on distorted waveforms from drives, LED drivers or switch-mode supplies. The 87V is True RMS and adds a low-pass filter for drive outputs.
