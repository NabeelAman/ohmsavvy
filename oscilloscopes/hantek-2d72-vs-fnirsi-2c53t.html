Hantek 2D72 vs FNIRSI 2C53T

The Hantek has the stronger scope and generator; the FNIRSI has the far better multimeter and weighs half as much.

Hantek 2D72 vs FNIRSI 2C53T spec by spec

These numbers show how differently the makers balanced their 3-in-1 designs. Hantek spent its budget on the scope and generator; FNIRSI on the meter and the battery. Both headline 250 MSa/s, but the Hantek halves to 125 MSa/s per channel with both channels on, and FNIRSI doesn't publish a two-channel figure.

Why record length limits both

A scope fills its record across the time on screen, so the real sample rate is record length divided by that time, capped at the headline rate. Short records mean the 250 MSa/s figure only applies on the fastest timebases. The table works it through for one channel.

Time on screenHantek 2D72 (6K)FNIRSI 2C53T (1K)
10 µs250 MSa/s (capped)100 MSa/s
1 ms6 MSa/s1 MSa/s

At 10 ms on screen, about half a cycle of 50 Hz mains, the FNIRSI samples at about 100 kSa/s, which still draws a 1 kHz tone with 100 points per cycle but can't show a 1 MHz switching edge. The Hantek is six times better there, yet both fall far behind a benchtop with millions of points.

Short records limit handhelds

With 1K to 6K samples, both scopes must slow their sampling on long timebases. For serious waveform work a benchtop is still better; see oscilloscope vs multimeter for when each tool earns its place.

Use cases in practice

If your day starts with a multimeter, the FNIRSI makes more sense. Its 19,999-count True RMS meter handles distorted waveforms from drives and switching supplies better than an average-responding meter, and its 3,000 mAh battery is built in, so there is nothing to source separately. The scope is a backup for a quick look at a signal rather than a debugging tool.

If your day starts with signals, the Hantek is the better kit. A generator that reaches 25 MHz can sweep a filter or drive an amplifier input, and 70 MHz with a 5 ns rise time shows faster edges than the FNIRSI's 50 MHz. Our guide to measuring an amplifier's bandwidth shows the job where a wide-range generator pays off.

For car work, see our best oscilloscopes for automotive diagnostics, and for another FNIRSI handheld matchup, see OWON HDS272S vs FNIRSI 2C53T.

Safety: check the input limits before probing mains

Hantek lists 150 Vrms as the 2D72's maximum scope input, and FNIRSI quotes ±400 V for the 2C53T, but neither spec page we checked gives a CAT rating. Mains peaks about 170 V on a 120 V circuit, which is beyond the Hantek's scope input. Use the multimeter inputs for mains voltage, never the scope probes, keep to the meter's stated limits, and call a licensed electrician for anything in a panel.

Choose the Hantek 2D72 if…

  • ✓You need a usable signal generator: sine output to 25 MHz
  • ✓70 MHz of bandwidth and a 6K record matter more than meter resolution
  • ✓You want a fold-out stand for bench use

Choose the FNIRSI 2C53T if…

  • ✓The multimeter is your main tool: 19,999 counts and True RMS
  • ✓You want the lightest kit at 300 g with a built-in 3,000 mAh battery
  • ✓Your scope work stays at audio and low-frequency signals

Neither if…

You need to probe mains voltage directly: the 2D72's scope input is rated only 150 Vrms, below a 120 V outlet's peak, and FNIRSI gives no CAT rating for the 2C53T's ±400 V scope input either — use a meter's rated inputs for mains, not either scope's probes.

The bottom line

Buy the Hantek 2D72 if the scope and signal generator are the point: more bandwidth, a longer record and a generator that reaches 25 MHz. Buy the FNIRSI 2C53T if you mainly want a high-resolution True RMS multimeter with a basic scope attached, in a lighter package.

Frequently asked questions

Which handheld has the better multimeter?+

The FNIRSI 2C53T. FNIRSI lists a 19,999-count True RMS meter that reads to 1000 V DC and 750 V AC, with current to 9.999 A, capacitance and temperature. Hantek lists a 4,000-count meter on the 2D72 with 600 V AC and DC and 10 A ranges, which covers basic checks but resolves about five times fewer steps.

Does the Hantek 2D72 come with batteries?+

Sources disagree. Hantek's site lists two 2,600 mAh lithium cells, while the Hantek-store Amazon listing says the package contains no lithium batteries. Check the listing's package contents before ordering, and buy matching cells if they aren't included. The FNIRSI has its battery built in.

Can either scope measure mains voltage on its scope input?+

Not the Hantek: its scope input is rated 150 Vrms, below the roughly 170 V peak of a 120 V outlet. FNIRSI lists ±400 V on the 2C53T's scope input but no CAT rating. Measure mains on the multimeter inputs instead; our mains frequency guide covers safer ways to view it.

Which is better for audio work?+

Both cover audio frequencies on the scope side, and both generators produce test tones across the audible range. The Hantek's generator goes far beyond audio, to 25 MHz on sine, and its 6K record holds more cycles of a low tone. The FNIRSI's generator reaches 50 kHz, which is still enough for speaker and amplifier checks.

Do they have the same screen?+

Yes, both use a 2.8-inch, 320 × 240 color display. That is small for waveform work, so neither shows fine detail the way a 7-inch benchtop does. The Hantek adds a fold-out stand for bench use, while the FNIRSI is the smaller body at 167 × 89 × 35 mm against 199 × 98 × 40 mm.
