Fluke 117 vs Fluke 117/323 Kit

This isn't a comparison between two different meters: the 117/323 kit includes the 117 itself, plus a 323 True RMS clamp meter, an extra set of TL175 test leads, a TPAK magnetic hanger and a carrying case. The real question is whether you'll use the 323's clamp current capability, which reaches 400A AC without opening the circuit, enough to justify the kit's higher price over the 117 on its own.

Fluke 117 vs 117/323 kit spec by spec

The 117 in the box is identical, so the only specs that differ are the ones the 323 brings. Two numbers sum it up: how much current you can read, and how you have to connect to read it.

The clamp is not a second multimeter. Fluke lists the 323 with 600 V AC and DC, resistance on 400 Ω and 4000 Ω ranges, and continuity, but no capacitance, frequency, diode test or DC current. Its AC current accuracy is ±(2% + 5 digits) at 45 to 65 Hz. Treat it as the current tool and the 117 as everything else.

Series meter vs clamp: what changes on the job

To read current with the 117 you switch off, open the circuit, wire the meter into the gap so the load current runs through its fused A input, then power up. That works on a lamp circuit or a small motor, but it's slow and it's capped at 10A continuous. The 323 reads the magnetic field around one conductor, so you close the jaw around a live wire and read the current without disturbing anything.

Clamp one conductor at a time. Around a whole cable the outgoing and returning currents cancel and the display reads near zero, which is the most common clamp-meter mistake. Our guide to measuring AC current with a clamp meter covers line splitters and reading small currents, and the no-break current explainer shows what a multimeter alone can and can't do.

Respect the ratings on each tool

The 117 is CAT III 600V; the 323 adds CAT IV 300V, which allows origin-of-installation work only on systems up to 300 V to earth. The 117 has no CAT IV rating at all. Never put the 117's A input across a voltage source, and keep series current measurements under 10A. If you aren't qualified to work inside a live panel, call a licensed electrician.

A useful test: think of the last ten electrical jobs you did. If two or more involved finding out how many amps a breaker, motor or appliance was drawing, the kit earns its place. If current only came up when you were checking a fuse or a battery, the 117 alone is the better spend.

The bottom line

There's no meaningful downside to the 117 itself in either option: the kit simply adds a 323 clamp meter, TL175 leads, a TPAK hanger and a case on top of it. Buy the kit if you have a real, regular need for AC current above 10A or without breaking the circuit. Buy the 117 alone if you don't, rather than paying for a clamp meter you won't use.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Fluke 117 inside the kit a different or lesser version?+

No. Fluke lists the same 117 in the kit, with its own silicone test leads and holster, so its True RMS, VoltAlert, LoZ and 3-year warranty are unchanged. The kit simply bundles the 323 clamp meter and accessories alongside it rather than selling it alone.

Does the kit save money compared to buying both tools separately?+

Often, but not always: street prices for the kit and both tools move independently. Compare the current Amazon price of the kit against the 117 and the 323 listed separately on the day you buy, since a promotion on either can change the answer.

Can I buy just the 323 clamp meter later if I start with the 117 alone?+

Yes. The 323 is sold on its own, so starting with the 117 and adding a 323 later is a sensible path if you're not sure you'll need clamp current. You give up the bundled case and TPAK, but you avoid paying for a tool that might sit unused.

What's the TPAK included in the kit?+

It's Fluke's ToolPak magnetic meter hanger. A strap and magnet let you hang the meter on a steel panel door or enclosure at eye level, leaving both hands free for the probes. It's a small accessory, but it makes panel work noticeably easier and safer.

Can the Fluke 323 measure DC current from a car or solar system?+

No. Fluke lists AC current only for the 323, so it reads nothing useful around a battery cable or solar string. For DC current under 10A you can wire the 117 in series; above that you'd need a clamp meter that specifically lists DC amps.
