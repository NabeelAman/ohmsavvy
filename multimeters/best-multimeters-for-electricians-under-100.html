What an electrician needs from a budget meter

An electrician's meter spends its life on energised equipment: receptacles, breakers, disconnects, motor terminals and sometimes the meter base. That puts the safety rating ahead of everything else, followed by features that save time on real calls: a low-impedance mode that sees through ghost voltage, a non-contact sensor for a quick first check, True RMS for the distorted current drawn by LED drivers and drives, and some way of reading load current. Our multimeter buying guide explains each of those terms if you are new to them, and testing electrical wires with a multimeter shows the basic routine.

Every meter below was listed under $100 on Amazon.com when we checked in October 2026; prices move, so the buy button shows today's figure. We chose the ten from our whole verified catalog by how well each one handles electrical work, not by brand or overall score, and two clamp meters made the cut because reading amps without opening a circuit matters so much in this trade. If your budget is open, our main best multimeter for electricians guide covers the Fluke models above this price, and the clamp meter buying guide helps if you are weighing a clamp against a handheld.

Panel and service-entrance safety

A meter is only as safe as its lowest-rated part. Use leads rated at or above the meter's category, keep fingers behind the probe guards, and never connect leads plugged into the current jacks across a voltage source. CAT III 600V is the minimum for distribution panels; at the service entrance, meter base or outdoor feeders, use CAT IV 600V. Test the meter on a known live source before and after confirming a circuit is dead, and follow your lockout and arc-flash procedures regardless of which meter you carry.

How the ten compare on paper

What to look for in an electrician's meter under $100

CAT rating on the meter, not the leads

CAT III 600V is the floor for panel work; CAT IV 600V is what you want at the meter base, service drop or outdoor feeds.

Low-impedance (LoZ) voltage

Loads the circuit so capacitively coupled ghost voltage on an open conductor collapses instead of reading like a live feed.

Non-contact voltage (NCV)

A quick go/no-go sense of a live conductor before the leads go anywhere. It supplements a proper test, it never replaces one.

True RMS

LED drivers, dimmers and variable-speed drives distort the waveform; an average-responding meter misreads that AC.

A way to read current

A 10 A jack covers small loads in series; a clamp reads motors, feeders and branch circuits without opening anything.

Toughness

Drop ratings, IP sealing and a display you can read in a dark basement matter as much as specs on a job site.

Why ghost voltage fools ordinary meters

A disconnected conductor pulled alongside live ones picks up voltage through capacitance. A standard meter with a high input impedance draws almost no current, so it can display a large phantom reading. A LoZ input draws enough to drain that coupled charge, and the reading drops toward zero, while a genuinely live conductor holds its voltage. For other puzzling readings, see unstable multimeter readings and measuring AC current with a clamp meter.

Before you buy

Buying advice

Read the rating printed on the meter

Listings for budget meters often quote the highest number they can find. Klein's MM450 listings show CAT IV, but that figure belongs to the supplied leads; the meter is CAT III 600V. KAIWEETS's KM602 manual and printed face say CAT III 600V while the listing claims CAT IV. We used the maker's manual or the marking on the meter in every case, and you should check the unit in your hand the same way.

Decide how you will measure current

Putting a meter in series on a branch circuit means opening the circuit, and a 10 A jack is the ceiling. For motor loads, feeders and tripping breakers a clamp is faster and keeps you out of the circuit. That is why two clamps rank in this top 10. If you already own a clamp, put the money into a CAT IV handheld with LoZ instead.

Prioritize LoZ if you troubleshoot more than you install

Ghost voltage shows up most on long runs, shared conduit and abandoned conductors, which is daily life on service calls. A meter without LoZ forces you to add a load or second-guess readings. The MM720 is the handheld with LoZ in this price range; the HT208D clamp also has it.

Leave room in the budget for leads and a case

Good leads with shrouded tips and a CAT rating that matches the meter are part of the safety system, and a worn lead can undo a well-rated meter. Some picks include extras: the MM720 ships with a pouch and thermocouple, and the HT118A has a kickstand and flashlight. Factor that in before comparing listed prices.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can a multimeter under $100 be safe for electrical panel work?+

Yes, provided the meter itself carries a genuine CAT III 600V or better rating and you use leads with at least the same rating. Several meters here, including the Klein MM720 and KAIWEETS HT118A, are rated CAT IV 600V. Price says nothing about safety; the category marking printed on the meter, fused current inputs and leads in good condition are what count.

Do electricians need a low-impedance (LoZ) multimeter?+

It is one of the most useful features for troubleshooting. A normal high-impedance input can show tens of volts on a disconnected conductor that runs beside a live one, and that phantom reading wastes time or causes a wrong call. LoZ puts a small load across the leads so the ghost voltage collapses. Under $100, the Klein MM720 and KAIWEETS HT208D clamp both list it.

Is CAT III enough, or do I need CAT IV?+

CAT III covers distribution panels, feeders, fixed equipment and branch circuits inside a building. CAT IV covers the origin of the installation: the utility service, meter base, overhead drop and outdoor feeders where transients are largest. If you only work downstream of the main breaker, CAT III 600V suffices. If you ever measure at the service entrance, choose a CAT IV 600V meter.

Should an electrician buy a clamp meter or a multimeter first?+

Most electricians end up with both. A multimeter with fused current jacks is better for resistance, continuity, capacitance and low-current readings; a clamp reads load current on a live conductor without breaking the circuit, which is faster and safer for motors and feeders. If you can only buy one under $100, a clamp such as the KAIWEETS HT208D that also reads volts, ohms and LoZ covers more daily work.

Is non-contact voltage detection reliable enough to trust?+

Treat it as a warning light, not proof. NCV senses the electric field around an AC conductor, so shielded cable, DC circuits, a low battery or holding the meter at the wrong angle can all hide a live wire. Use it to spot a hot conductor quickly, then confirm with the test leads, and verify the meter on a known live source before and after testing dead.

Why is the Fluke 117 not on this list?+

It was listed well above $100 on Amazon when we checked in October 2026, so it falls outside this price limit. It remains our Best Overall pick and carries our Hands-on Tested badge. If your budget can stretch, compare it with the meters here; if not, the Klein MM720 matches its CAT III 600V rating and adds the CAT IV 600V rating that the 117 does not carry.
