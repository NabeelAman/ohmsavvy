Hantek 6022BE vs 1008C

Hantek 6022BE vs 1008C usually comes up when someone wants a cheap USB scope for the car. The answer is the 1008C for engine diagnostics: eight 12-bit inputs rated to 400 V, an ignition probe on Hantek's parts list, an eight-channel crank and cam signal generator and 78 to 80-plus guided vehicle tests. The 6022BE has two inputs, no stated voltage limit and no car software, but at 48 MS/s and 20 MHz it is the faster scope, and that matters for CAN bus and general electronics.

Hantek 6022BE vs 1008C on a running engine

A four-cylinder engine with coil-on-plug ignition has at least eight signals worth watching during a misfire hunt. The 1008C can wire up all of them in one session. The 6022BE takes two at a time, which is enough to check crank against cam timing but means moving probes and re-cranking for every injector.

Hantek's 1008C page lists the high-voltage ignition probe as included, quantity one. The Amazon listing for the kit we link doesn't name it in the box contents. If secondary ignition is the job you bought it for, ask the seller before ordering. Hantek publishes the same spec table and function count for the 1008B; our Hantek 1008B vs 6022BE comparison covers the 8-bit against 12-bit resolution gap in detail, so this page doesn't repeat it.

Spec by spec

CAN bus: the one car job the 6022BE does better

High-speed CAN commonly runs at 500 kbit/s, so each bit lasts 2 µs. At 2.4 MSa/s the 1008C takes 4.8 samples per bit with one channel on, enough to see that the bus is toggling but not enough to judge edge shape or ringing. Hantek doesn't give the per-channel rate with more channels running, so a two-wire CAN-H and CAN-L view may be coarser still. The 6022BE at 48 MS/s takes about 96 samples per bit, and its 20 MHz bandwidth passes the edges.

Hantek lists bus detection among the 1008C's vehicle tests, so its software will tell you whether the bus is alive. To see why a node drops off, such as slow edges from a damaged terminating resistor, the faster 6022BE shows more. Neither decodes CAN frames according to the pages we used.

Slow but deep versus fast but narrow

Most engine sensor signals are slow. A 60-tooth crank wheel at 800 rpm passes 13.3 revolutions per second, or about 800 teeth per second, and injector pulses last milliseconds. That is why Hantek can build a car scope around 2.4 MSa/s and 100 kHz. The 1008C trades speed for channel count and 12-bit detail. The 6022BE makes the opposite trade, which suits CAN edges and hobby electronics.

Voltage headroom on injectors and coils

When an injector or coil primary switches off, the collapsing magnetic field produces a voltage spike that can reach tens of volts on an injector and several hundred volts on a coil primary. Hantek rates the 1008C's inputs to 400 V DC plus AC peak, so a coil primary spike can come close to that limit. For the 6022BE it lists diode-clamp protection and no voltage figure, so we wouldn't connect it to a coil primary at all, and only through a x10 probe on injectors.

Ignition secondary and hybrid systems

Never clip either scope onto a spark plug lead or coil output. Secondary ignition runs at many kilovolts and needs a capacitive ignition probe, which is what Hantek lists with the 1008C. Keep both away from hybrid and EV high-voltage circuits, marked with orange cabling; that work needs rated equipment and training. Because both scopes share ground with your laptop through USB, run the laptop on battery when probing the car.

The bottom line

For car diagnostics, buy the Hantek 1008C: eight 12-bit inputs rated to 400 V, an ignition probe on Hantek's parts list, a crank and cam simulator and dozens of guided tests. Buy the Hantek 6022BE if cars are a side job and you mostly work on electronics, or if you need to see CAN bus edges, where its 48 MS/s is 20 times faster.

Frequently asked questions

Can the Hantek 6022BE be used for automotive diagnostics?+

For simple jobs, yes: two channels can show a crank sensor against a cam sensor, or one injector pulse through a x10 probe. It has no vehicle test library, no ignition probe and no stated input voltage rating, so coil primaries and secondary ignition are off the table. The 1008C is built for that work.

What is the difference between the Hantek 1008B and 1008C?+

Hantek uses the same eight-channel, 12-bit, 2.4 MSa/s hardware for the 1008A, 1008B and 1008C. Hantek's 1008B and 1008C pages publish identical spec tables and the same automotive function count, so check the kit contents instead: Hantek's 1008C page lists a high-voltage ignition probe. Our 1008B vs 6022BE page compares that hardware with the 6022BE in more depth.

How many automotive tests does the Hantek 1008C have?+

Hantek's own page says both "over 80 types" in its overview and "78 types of vehicle diagnosis function" in the function list. The Amazon listing says over 80, covering ignition, sensors, bus detection, starting and charging circuits. Expect roughly 80; the exact count depends on which part of Hantek's page you read.

Do these Hantek scopes work on a Mac?+

Not according to Hantek. The 6022BE software lists Windows 10, 8, 7, NT, XP and Vista, and the 1008C lists Windows XP, 7, 8 and 10. Neither page mentions macOS or Linux. Hantek does publish a 6022BE SDK, which some users build on, but that isn't official support.

Does the 1008C ignition probe come in the box?+

Hantek's 1008C product page lists one high-voltage ignition probe as included. The included-components list on the Amazon listing we link doesn't name one. If you need it for secondary ignition tests, confirm with the seller before you buy, or budget for a separate capacitive ignition probe.

Which is better for learning how an oscilloscope works?+

The 6022BE. It behaves like a normal two-channel scope with edge triggering, X-Y mode, FFT and cursors, and its 20 MHz covers most hobby circuits. The 1008C's software is organized around vehicle tests, and its 100 kHz bandwidth is too slow for many microcontroller signals.
