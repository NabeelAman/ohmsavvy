Rigol DS1054Z vs DS1202Z-E

Rigol DS1054Z vs DS1202Z-E puts two scopes from the same Rigol family side by side: same 313 × 161 × 122 mm case, same 7-inch 800 × 480 screen, same 1 GSa/s and 30,000 waveforms per second. The DS1054Z spreads that over four 50 MHz channels; the DS1202Z-E puts it into two 200 MHz channels. The DS1054Z is one of the scopes our owner has tested hands-on. We use power electronics to show the trade, because a motor drive wants many channels while a half-bridge's dead time wants fast ones.

Spec by spec

Both scopes have the same total sampling budget of 1 GSa/s. What changes is how it's divided and how fast each channel's front end is.

Two power-electronics jobs, two different winners

A three-phase motor drive and a half-bridge sit on the same kind of bench, but they ask opposite things of a scope. One needs breadth, the other needs speed.

Three phases and a current: the DS1054Z

Checking that a drive's three phases are 120° apart and that current tracks them needs four traces on one timebase. Motor PWM runs in the kHz to tens of kHz, far inside 50 MHz, and 250 MSa/s per channel is plenty. The DS1202Z-E can only show two of those at once, so you would compare pairs and lose the full picture.

Dead time in a half-bridge: the DS1202Z-E

Dead time is the gap where both switches are off, often tens to a few hundred nanoseconds. Two things limit how well you can read it. Sample spacing: 2 ns per sample on the DS1202Z-E with both channels on, 2 ns on the DS1054Z with two on (4 ns with three or four). And edge speed: the DS1054Z's 7 ns rise time blurs each end of the gap. For an illustrative 50 ns dead time, two 7 ns edges are a large fraction of the interval; two 1.75 ns edges barely register.

The samples-per-cycle surprise

With every channel on, the DS1054Z actually has the healthier ratio at its own limit: 250 MSa/s against 50 MHz is 5 samples per cycle. The DS1202Z-E's 500 MSa/s against 200 MHz is 2.5. The DS1202Z-E still sees far more, but near 200 MHz with both channels on, single-shot captures are thin. Turn one channel off for 1 GSa/s when you need detail at the top of the band.

What each one can and can't measure

Rigol DS1054Z: wide and slow

The DS1054Z suits multi-signal timing below about 10 MHz: buses, motor drives, sequencing, audio. Its 7 ns rise time sets an accurate-edge floor near 20 to 35 ns. It can't properly show ringing on fast gates or the shape of clocks above about 10 MHz, and with four channels on each one gets 6 Mpts per Rigol's datasheet.

Rigol DS1202Z-E: narrow and fast

The DS1202Z-E reads edges down to roughly 5 to 9 ns, clocks to about 40 MHz and millivolt ripple at 500 µV/div. Its 12 Mpts per channel with both on is twice the DS1054Z's four-channel figure. It can't show a third or fourth signal, which rules it out for multi-phase and full-bus work.

Power stages bite back

Half-bridges and motor drives often run from rectified mains or high-voltage DC. Both Rigols have earth-referenced inputs rated CAT I 300 Vrms and CAT II 100 Vrms, so a ground clip on a switching node shorts it to earth. Use high-voltage differential probes for anything not referenced to the low-voltage ground, and discharge bus capacitors before touching the board.

The trust question: the DS1054Z's two spec sheets

Rigol's DS1000Z datasheet lists 24 Mpts standard and decoding with no option label. Its product page lists 12 Mpts standard, 24 Mpts optional, and calls bus decoding an option. The DS1202Z-E's DS1000Z-E datasheet lists 24 Mpts and decoding as standard, and we found no conflicting page. If the DS1054Z's memory or decoding sways you, check what the listing includes.

Choose the Rigol DS1054Z if…

  • ✓You need three or four signals on one timebase: motor phases, SPI, sequenced rails
  • ✓Your edges are slower than about 25 ns and your clocks under 10 MHz
  • ✓You want the scope our owner has tested hands-on
  • ✓You want 250 MSa/s and 6 Mpts on each of four channels

Choose the Rigol DS1202Z-E if…

  • ✓You measure dead time, gate edges or ringing in nanoseconds
  • ✓You look at clocks from 10 to 40 MHz
  • ✓You want a 500 µV/div range for small ripple and noise
  • ✓Two channels at a time are enough for what you probe

Neither if…

You need four channels and fast edges together, for example two half-bridge legs at once. A four-channel 100 MHz or faster scope such as the Siglent SDS1104X-E covers both better.

The bottom line

Choose the Rigol DS1054Z if your work involves three or four signals at once and stays under about 10 MHz: motor phases, buses, rail sequencing. It's also the one our owner has tested. Choose the Rigol DS1202Z-E if you measure fast edges, dead time, ringing or 10 to 40 MHz clocks and two channels at a time are enough; it's four times faster on every channel it has.

Frequently asked questions

Is the DS1202Z-E an upgrade from the DS1054Z?+

It's a different trade rather than a straight upgrade. You gain four times the bandwidth (200 MHz against 50 MHz), a 1.75 ns rise time and a 500 µV/div range, but you give up two channels. The case, screen, 1 GSa/s total, capture rate, decoder list and warranty are the same in Rigol's datasheets.

Can the DS1054Z measure MOSFET dead time?+

It can show it, but not precisely when the gap is short. Its 7 ns calculated rise time smears each edge, and with three or four channels on, samples are 4 ns apart. For dead times of a few hundred nanoseconds it's workable; for tens of nanoseconds, the DS1202Z-E's 1.75 ns rise time is far better suited.

Do both Rigol scopes decode serial buses?+

Both datasheets list parallel, RS232/UART, I2C and SPI decoding on two buses. For the DS1054Z, Rigol's DS1000Z product page describes bus trigger and decode as an option, which conflicts with its datasheet. The DS1202Z-E's DS1000Z-E datasheet lists them without an option label.

Which is better for a three-phase motor controller?+

The DS1054Z. Seeing phases A, B and C together, plus a current probe on the fourth channel, is the normal way to check phase balance and commutation. Motor PWM frequencies sit far below 50 MHz. The DS1202Z-E would make you compare phases two at a time.

Are the two scopes the same size?+

Rigol lists both at 313.1 × 160.8 × 122.4 mm. The DS1054Z weighs 3.2 kg and the DS1202Z-E 2.9 kg without packaging. Both run from 100 to 240 V mains at up to 50 W, and Rigol gives both a three-year warranty on the mainframe.
