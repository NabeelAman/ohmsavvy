Fluke 17B+ vs Fluke T5-600

Fluke 17B+ vs Fluke T5-600 is a bench-versus-panel choice. The 17B+ is a full multimeter built for electronics, with temperature, frequency, capacitance to 1,000 µF and MIN MAX recording. The T5-600 is an electrical tester that trades those functions for speed: automatic AC/DC voltage, quick continuity and up to 100A AC through an open jaw.

Fluke 17B+ vs T5-600 spec by spec

The two overlap on voltage but diverge sharply elsewhere. These first two charts use a logarithmic scale because the ranges differ by orders of magnitude.

Neither error matters for the T5-600's intended job. Knowing a motor draws 9.4 A rather than 10.0 A, or that a circuit carries 120 V rather than 118 V, is plenty to tell a healthy load from a dead or overloaded one. The 17B+'s tighter figures pay off on electronics, where a reading of 4.85 V against 5.00 V on a logic supply is the difference between a working board and a fault.

Resolution: what whole-volt steps hide

The T5-600 shows voltage in 1 V steps. That is a deliberate choice for a tester meant to answer "is it live, and is it about 120 V or 240 V?" in a second. The 17B+ shows the same voltage to 0.01 V on its 40 V range and to 0.001 V on its 4 V range, which is what you need for batteries, sensors and logic rails.

A resting 12 V lead-acid battery near 12.7 V is roughly full, and one near 12.2 V is roughly half discharged; those are typical rule-of-thumb values that vary with battery type and temperature. The 17B+ separates them easily. The T5-600 can't, which is why a pocket multimeter belongs beside it for any battery, automotive or electronics job. Who should buy which

Buy the 17B+ if your work happens on a bench or inside appliances. Its µA and mA ranges cover standby draw and control loops, its thermocouple input covers ovens and HVAC air, and frequency to 100 kHz with duty cycle covers PWM circuits. It is also the only one of the pair that can log extremes with MIN MAX while you wait for an intermittent fault.

Buy the T5-600 if your work happens at panels and motors. The open jaw takes a single conductor up to 1/0 THHN in its 12.9 mm opening, so you read a compressor or heater current without shutting anything down. The long, narrow body stores its probes in the back and is rated for a 3 m drop. Many electricians carry both kinds of tool; if you want one meter that does both jobs, compare the Fluke 117 and T5-600 or read our clamp meter buying guide.

Safety: current checks on live circuits

To read current, the 17B+ must be wired in series: switch off, open the circuit, connect the leads to the A or mA jack, then power up. Never leave the leads in a current jack and probe a voltage source, which puts a near short across it. The T5-600 avoids that by clamping one conductor, but Fluke warns it won't read a whole cord, because hot and neutral cancel. Both are CAT III 600V; for service-entrance work or a panel that is hot, buzzing or burnt, call a licensed electrician.

The bottom line

For electronics, choose the Fluke 17B+: capacitance, frequency, temperature and MIN MAX are things the T5-600 simply can't do. For electrical panels and motors, the T5-600 is the faster, tougher tool and reads far more current. Many electricians carry a tester like the T5 and a multimeter like the 17B+ side by side.

Frequently asked questions

Can the Fluke T5-600 replace a 17B+ multimeter?+

Not for electronics. It has no capacitance, frequency, duty cycle or temperature function, resistance stops at 1,000 Ω, and voltage reads in whole-volt steps. It covers what an electrician checks most: AC and DC voltage, continuity and AC current to 100A. For circuit boards, batteries and sensors you still need a multimeter such as the 17B+.

Which reads more current, the 17B+ or the T5-600?+

The T5-600, up to 100A AC through its open jaw with 0.1 A resolution. The 17B+ reads up to 10A, but on both AC and DC and down to its 400.0 µA range at 0.1 µA resolution. So the T5-600 wins on big motor and heater loads, and the 17B+ wins on anything small or DC.

Does the Fluke 17B+ measure temperature?+

Yes, from -55 °C to 400 °C with the Type K thermocouple probe Fluke includes. Accuracy is ±2 °C from 0 to 50 °C and ±(2% + 1 °C) from 50 to 400 °C, which is fine for HVAC air, ovens and heatsinks. The T5-600 has no temperature function, so you would need a separate thermometer.

Are the 17B+ and T5-600 the same safety rating?+

Both are CAT III 600V, which covers distribution panels, feeders and branch circuits. Fluke's datasheet also gives the 17B+ a CAT II 1000V rating for lower-energy, plug-connected equipment, and it measures up to 1000V. Neither tool is rated CAT IV, so neither should be used at the service entrance or meter base.

Is the Fluke T5-600 True RMS?+

No. Fluke describes the T5-600's AC response as averaging, and it makes no True RMS claim for the 17B+ either. Both read clean 60 Hz sine waves correctly. On variable-speed drives, LED drivers and other distorted loads their AC readings can be off, so choose a True RMS meter such as the Fluke 117 for that work.
