HOW-TO GUIDE

How to Connect an Oscilloscope Probe Correctly

To connect an oscilloscope probe correctly, push its BNC onto the channel input and twist until it locks, set the probe's 1x/10x switch and the channel's probe setting to the same value, clip the ground lead to the circuit's ground, then touch the tip to the point you want to measure. Most bad first traces come from one of those four steps. This guide explains each one, plus the two mistakes that are easy to miss: long ground leads and accidental ground connections.

By the OhmSavvy Research Team · Updated October 22, 2026

Bench scopes such as the 100MHz Siglent SDS1104X-E ship with passive probes that connect the same way. See our SDS1104X-E review.

The ground clip is not a second probe

  • On a mains-powered bench scope the ground clip is connected to earth through the power lead. Clip it only to the circuit's ground or 0 V.
  • Never clip it to mains live, neutral, or the primary side of a mains power supply. Use a differential probe or isolated method for those.
  • Never remove the scope's earth to make it "float". Its metal parts can then sit at a lethal voltage.

The parts of a passive probe

BNC connector

Bayonet plug: push on, then twist to lock onto the channel input.

1x / 10x switch

Selects direct or divide-by-ten. Many probes have it on the body.

Hook tip

Spring-loaded hook that grips a lead or test pin hands-free.

Ground clip

Short crocodile-clip lead to circuit ground.

Ground spring

Tiny spring that grounds right beside the tip, for fast edges.

Trimmer

Compensation adjuster, on the probe body or BNC end.

1Attach the BNC

Line up the two slots on the probe's BNC with the pins on the channel input, push it home and turn it clockwise until it clicks. A half-seated BNC gives intermittent traces that come and go as the cable moves. If you have a four-channel scope, note which channel each probe is on; the colored rings that come with most probes help.

2Match 1x/10x on the probe and the scope

A 10x probe divides the signal by ten before it reaches the scope. The scope cannot see the switch, so you tell it in the channel menu (often labeled Probe or Attenuation). If the two disagree, every voltage reading is out by a factor of ten.

3Connect the ground clip

Find the circuit's 0 V point: a GND pin, the negative terminal of a battery-powered board or the ground plane near the part you are probing. Clip the ground lead there before the tip touches anything. Grounding first means the probe never sits at an unknown voltage, and it stops the tip from picking up stray hum while you position it.

4Place the tip

Extend the hook and grip a component lead, test loop or header pin. Avoid holding the tip against a fine-pitch IC pin by hand: a slip can short two pins. Solder a short wire or use a fine probe tip instead.

Why ground lead length matters

The standard ground clip lead is a loop of wire, and a loop has inductance. Together with the probe's capacitance it forms a resonant circuit that rings on every fast edge. On slow signals you will not notice, but on a logic clock or switching circuit the ringing can look like a real fault.

Rule of thumb

The faster the edge you are looking at, the shorter the ground connection must be. If you see ringing, swap the clip lead for the ground spring before you blame the circuit.

Earthed scopes and ground mistakes

  • All channel grounds are joined. Clipping CH1 ground and CH2 ground to two different voltages shorts those points together through the scope.
  • Earthed circuits create a loop. If the device under test is also earthed, the ground clip and the mains earth form a loop that can add 50 Hz hum. Keep leads short and route them together.
  • Floating signals need another method. To measure between two points neither of which is ground, use two channels and the scope's math subtract function, or a differential probe.

Quick check before you measure

Touch the tip to the scope's probe compensation terminal and its ground to the terminal's ground. You should see a square wave of the amplitude printed beside the terminal. Correct amplitude confirms the 1x/10x match; square corners confirm compensation. If the corners are rounded or spiked, follow our guide to compensating an oscilloscope probe.

Frequently asked questions

How do you ground an oscilloscope probe?

Clip the probe's ground lead directly to the circuit's ground or 0 V reference, as close to the test point as the lead reaches — a GND pin or the negative supply rail on most circuits. Keep the lead short, since a long ground return picks up noise and causes ringing on fast edges. Never clip it to a point that sits at a voltage relative to earth; the scope's chassis ground is typically tied to earth through the mains plug, and doing so creates a short.

Where should I connect the oscilloscope ground clip?

To the ground or 0 V reference of the circuit you are measuring, as close as practical to the test point. On a microcontroller board that is a GND pin or the negative supply rail. Never clip it to a point that sits at a voltage relative to earth, because the scope's ground is earthed and you will create a short.

Why is my oscilloscope reading 10 times too high or too low?

The probe's 1x/10x switch and the channel's probe attenuation setting disagree. If the probe is on 10x but the channel thinks it is 1x, every voltage reads ten times too small; the reverse reads ten times too large. Set both to the same value and the numbers will be right.

Do I need a ground clip on every probe?

Every channel shares the same ground inside a bench scope, so electrically one ground connection serves them all. For clean high-speed measurements, though, each probe should still have its own short ground near its tip, because a long shared return path picks up noise and causes ringing.

Should I use 1x or 10x?

Use 10x for most work. It loads the circuit far less and gives the full bandwidth of the probe. Switch to 1x only for very small signals, a few millivolts, at low frequency, where the extra sensitivity matters more than bandwidth.

Do I need to compensate the probe after connecting it?

Yes, whenever you move a 10x probe to a different channel or scope. Compensation matches the probe to that input so square edges look square. It takes a minute using the scope's calibration output, and it is covered step by step in our separate probe compensation guide.
