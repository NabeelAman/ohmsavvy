HOW TO CHOOSE

How to pick the right comparison for your shortlist

Most people land here with two irons in mind and one nagging doubt. Work out which kind of choice you are really making first; the right comparison is the one built around that question, not the one with the longest spec table.

Bench station vs pen iron

If you are torn between a mains-powered station and a compact USB-C iron, the real trade is a fixed bench setup against portability. Pairs such as the Pinecil V2 vs Hakko FX-888DX are built around that split.

Two stations at a similar price

Between two bench stations, the deciding points are usually temperature control, the tip series each takes and the stand. The Weller WE1010 vs Hakko FX-888DX comparison is a good example.

Two portable irons

For pocket irons, look at the power input each accepts, the tips available and the screen and controls. See the Pinecil V2 vs TS101 and TS101 vs FNIRSI HS-02A.

Iron only or iron plus hot air

If you rework surface-mount parts, a 2-in-1 station with a hot air gun may suit you better than a second iron. The Weller WE1010NA vs YIHUA 862BD+ comparison covers that choice.

Budget kit vs better iron

A low-cost kit gets you started with everything in one box; a better iron costs more but holds temperature properly. The Pinecil V2 vs Q-MING 60W kit comparison weighs exactly that.

Total cost, not sticker price

Add up what each option needs before you can solder: a stand, a USB-C power supply for a pen iron, spare tips, solder and flux. A comparison that looks close on price can split clearly once the extras are counted.

OUR PROCESS

How OhmSavvy compares soldering irons

Each comparison puts both irons' specs side by side, taken from the maker's datasheet, manual or product page or the official Amazon listing. Where a figure isn't published, the table says "Not stated" rather than guessing, and where two sources disagree, we point that out.

We then pick the one or two differences that actually decide the pair and explain who should buy which iron. Any iron in a comparison that we have bought and used on our bench carries a Hands-on Tested badge; for the others we work from published data and verified owner feedback, and we never present that as our own testing.

If one of the two irons stops being sold on Amazon, the comparison stays online with a notice and its buy button points to the closest current alternative. How we fund the site and rate products is set out on our about page.

FAQ

Common questions

01

How do you decide which soldering irons to compare?

We pair irons that buyers genuinely weigh against each other: similar price, similar use, or a common upgrade path, such as a budget kit against a better pen iron. Every iron in a comparison has to be on sale on Amazon when we publish it.

02

Are the specs in a soldering iron comparison measured by OhmSavvy?

No. Spec tables come from the maker's own data or the official Amazon listing, so you can check them yourself. Hands-on notes only appear for irons we have bought and used on our bench, and those carry a Hands-on Tested badge.

03

What if neither iron in a comparison suits me?

Our ranked best soldering irons list covers the main kinds of buyer, from first-time hobbyists to repair benches, with a pick for each. If you're still unsure, the buying guide walks through how to choose from scratch, starting from the kind of soldering you do.
