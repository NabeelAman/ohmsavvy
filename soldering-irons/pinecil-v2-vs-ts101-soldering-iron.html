Pinecil V2 vs TS101 Soldering Iron

Pinecil V2 vs TS101 soldering iron is the classic portable-iron decision: both are smart, OLED-equipped irons that run from USB-C Power Delivery or a DC supply. The Pinecil has a clearly published power ceiling of 88W at 24V; the Miniware TS101 brings motion-sensing sleep, a Boost button and a 65W adapter in its kit, but Miniware's wattage figures conflict.

Power input: what each iron runs from

Portable irons live or die by their power source. A USB-C PD charger is the most convenient supply, but it caps out at 20V, so the highest wattages need a DC supply at 24V. The chart shows the voltage window each maker publishes.

Pine64 adds two cautions worth knowing before you buy a supply. Use only one power port at a time, and use a DC5525 barrel plug: a DC5521 plug, the more common size on cheap adapters, will damage the socket. Pine64 also lists 28V USB PD EPR chargers as theoretically supported, but marks it tentative, so don't plan around it.

Pinecil V2 vs TS101 spec by spec

With Miniware's wattage unclear, the fairest power comparison is what each supply can deliver. A supply's ceiling is its voltage times its current limit, and the iron can never draw more than that, whatever its own rating says.

The arithmetic shows why both irons feel sluggish on a weak supply: 12 V at 3 A is 36 W at most, a little over half of what a 20 V charger allows. Pine64's 88 W figure is for 24 V at the barrel jack; 24 × 3 is 72, so that rating implies the 24 V supply can provide more than the 3 A minimum Pine64 lists. If your work is mostly small through-hole and SMD joints, 60 W on a good PD charger is plenty for either.

How USB-C PD decides your wattage

A USB-C port starts at 5 V, which is below the minimum of both irons. With Power Delivery, the charger first advertises the voltages it can supply, the iron asks for the highest one it accepts, and only then does the full voltage arrive. A phone charger that tops out at 9 V will run the TS101 but not the Pinecil; one that offers 20 V at 3 A lets either iron work at its best on USB.

Use cases: where each iron fits

The Pinecil V2 suits makers who already have a capable USB-C laptop charger or a 24 V bench supply. It is tiny, 28 g with its tip, and its open IronOS firmware is community maintained. Pine64's published 88 W ceiling gives it real headroom on ground planes and heavier wire when fed from 24 V DC. The trade-offs are that nothing else comes in the box, not even a stand, and Pine64's Amazon listing still describes the original Pinecil, so check which version you're getting.

The TS101 suits someone starting from scratch or packing a field kit. The 65 W adapter means no charger hunt, motion-sensing sleep lets the iron idle when it stops moving, and the Boost button is there for the odd stubborn joint. Owners of a TS100 can move their tips straight across. Its weak spot is documentation: Miniware's wattage figures conflict, so power claims are hard to pin down.

A note on wattage

Wattage is only as high as your supply allows: a 20V, 3A charger delivers about 60W to either iron. If you plan to solder heavy connectors, see our soldering iron buying guide for how power affects thermal recovery, and why a soldering iron takes too long to heat up if yours feels slow.

Safety: a 400 °C tip in a pocket-sized body

Both irons reach soldering temperature in seconds and stay hot after you unplug them. Use a stand (the Pinecil ships without one), unplug and let the tip cool before packing it, and never run either from a damaged cable or an unknown barrel adapter. Work with ventilation for flux fumes, and if a lithium power bank swells or gets hot while powering an iron, disconnect it and move it away from anything flammable.

Choose the Pinecil V2 if…

  • ✓You want a clearly stated power ceiling: up to 88W from a 24V DC supply
  • ✓You already own a USB-C PD charger or power bank and want the iron alone
  • ✓You value fast heat-up, about 12 seconds on a 20V or higher supply

Choose the Miniware TS101 if…

  • ✓You want a power adapter in the box: this kit includes a 65W USB-C charger
  • ✓You want motion-sensing sleep, an overheat warning and a Boost button
  • ✓You already own TS100 tips, which fit the TS101

The bottom line

Buy the Pinecil V2 if you already have a good USB-C PD charger or a 24V supply and want the clearest published power ceiling. Buy the Miniware TS101 if you want a complete kit with its own 65W adapter, motion-sensing sleep, or you already own TS100 tips.

Frequently asked questions

Is the TS101 more powerful than the Pinecil V2?+

We can't say. Pine64 states up to 88W at 24V for the Pinecil V2, while Miniware's TS101 listing gives conflicting figures, so we don't quote a TS101 wattage. On a 20V, 3A USB-C PD charger both are limited to about 60W by the charger itself, which makes them broadly comparable on USB power.

Do TS100 tips fit the TS101?+

Yes. Miniware's listing says TS100 accessories, including tips, are compatible with the TS101, so a TS100 owner can upgrade without rebuying a tip set. The Pinecil V2 uses its own short tips; Pine64's spec page lists the supplied ST-B2 tip but doesn't name TS100 compatibility, so check before buying tips for it.

Can I run either iron from a power bank?+

Yes, if the power bank supports USB-C Power Delivery at a suitable voltage. The TS101 accepts PD from 9V and the Pinecil from 12V, and higher voltages give more heat. A bank that offers 20V at 3A supplies up to 60W, while a 12V-only bank caps either iron at about 36W.

Does either iron come with a stand?+

The Pinecil V2 doesn't include a stand by default, so budget for one. The TS101 kit on Amazon includes the iron, a B2 tip and a 65W adapter; check the current listing photos and contents for a stand. A stable stand matters with both, because a tiny iron can roll off a bench.

Which is better for a beginner?+

The TS101 kit is simpler to start with because the charger is included and motion-sensing sleep protects the tip. If you already have a capable USB-C laptop charger, the Pinecil V2 is the more economical way in. Either needs a stand, solder and flux; our best soldering iron for beginners guide covers the rest.
