The best budget multimeter, in brief

The best budget multimeter for most people is the KAIWEETS HT118A: it is True RMS, rated CAT III 1000V / CAT IV 600V in its manual, and adds NCV, temperature and microamp current, so it covers the house, the car and hobby electronics. Choose the Klein Tools MM420 if you prefer a major US tool brand with a thermocouple in the box, or the UNI-T UT61E+ if you mostly work on circuit boards and want 22,000 counts. The other seven each win for a narrower buyer, from rechargeable Smart-mode meters to a basic manual-ranging Klein.

Budget meters differ far more from each other than premium ones do. Some publish a full manual with accuracy tables and a CAT rating printed on the case; others make claims on the listing that the manual doesn't support. We only considered meters whose safety rating is documented by the maker or marked on the case, and where sources disagree we used the lower figure. This guide ranks for the general buyer; if you need True RMS above all, our True RMS budget ranking weighs that first, and trade users should read the electricians' budget meters guide. For a head-to-head between two of the cheapest picks, see KAIWEETS KM200s vs UNI-T UT139C.

A low-cost meter still has to be safe

The CAT rating, fused current inputs and lead insulation are what stand between you and a mains fault. Use a CAT III 600V meter or better on anything wired into the wall, keep CAT II meters to plug-in appliances and electronics, and never measure voltage with the red lead in an amps jack. Replace damaged leads straight away, and call a licensed electrician for panel or service work.

What to check before you buy a budget meter

A real, documented safety rating

Low-cost meters vary most here. Trust the manual and the marking on the case; where a listing claims more, we use the lower figure.

True RMS where you need it

Most of this list is True RMS. It matters for dimmers, inverters and motor drives, and barely at all for batteries and fuses.

Counts and resolution

A 20,000-count meter shows a 5V rail to the millivolt and resolves 0.01 Ω on its lowest range; a 4,000-count meter cannot.

Current ranges that fit your work

A µA range matters for sleep current in battery projects. Household users rarely need more than the 10 A input.

Warranty and support

KAIWEETS gives a 3-year warranty on the HT118A and KM602, and Klein is a major US tool brand with an established warranty process.

What's in the box

A thermocouple, a USB cable or a rechargeable cell adds real value; check the listing for exactly which accessories ship.

Cheap meters fail at the edges, not in the middle

Nearly every meter here will read a 12V battery or a 120V outlet correctly. The differences show up at the extremes: microamps, fractions of an ohm, distorted AC and high-energy transients. Decide which of those edges you will actually reach. Our guides to measuring microamps, multimeter accuracy for electronics repair and the multimeter buying guide explain each one, and our KAIWEETS KM200s review looks closely at the entry-level end.

Before you buy

Buying advice

Spend on the safety rating before the feature list

A low-cost meter with a documented CAT III 600V rating, fused current inputs and sound leads is a better buy than a feature-packed one with no stated category. Meters with no stated rating, such as the AstroAI DM6000AR, were left out of this list on purpose. If a listing promises CAT IV but the manual or case says CAT III, believe the manual.

Choose resolution only if you work on electronics

Counts and microamp resolution are what separate the UT61E+, B41T+ and HT118E from the rest. They matter for measuring a microcontroller's sleep current or a 0.5 Ω shunt. For household and automotive checks, a 4,000- or 6,000-count meter already shows more digits than the job needs, and its simpler dial is easier to use.

Factor in the running costs

A budget meter's real costs are batteries, fuses and leads. The KM602 avoids batteries with its rechargeable cell; the others use AA or 9V cells. Check that replacement fuses with the rating printed in the manual are easy to buy, and plan on a better set of leads if the included ones feel stiff or thin.

Know when to step up

If you start working inside distribution panels every week, need an industrial warranty or want LoZ for ghost voltage, a budget meter is the wrong tool. Our guides for electricians and HVAC technicians cover meters built for daily trade use. Until then, any of these ten will cover the vast majority of home, car and hobby jobs.

Frequently asked questions

Is a budget multimeter accurate enough for real work?+

For most household, automotive and hobby measurements, yes. The UNI-T UT61E+ quotes ±(0.05% + 5) on DC volts and the OWON B41T+ ±(0.1% + 2), both far tighter than a typical 12V or 120V check needs. What you give up on low-cost meters is usually support, documentation consistency and ruggedness, not basic accuracy. Use a calibrated professional meter when a job demands a certificate.

Which budget multimeter is safest for household outlets?+

Pick one whose manual and case both state CAT III 600V or higher. On this list the HT118A, HT118E and UT61E+ add CAT IV 600V, and the Klein MM420, MM460S and MM325, UNI-T UT139C and KAIWEETS KM602 are CAT III 600V. The KM200s is CAT II, so keep it to plug-in appliances, batteries and electronics rather than fixed wiring or panels.

What does 6,000 count mean on a multimeter?+

It is the largest number the display can show before switching range, roughly 5,999 plus a sign. On a 6V range a 6,000-count meter resolves 0.001V, while a 4,000-count meter switches up a range sooner and loses a digit around 4V. Higher counts, like the 22,000 on the UT61E+, give finer readings on low voltages and resistances, which helps electronics work more than household checks.

Are KAIWEETS and UNI-T multimeters good brands?+

Both publish manuals with full specification tables, which is what we rely on, and the CAT rating is printed on the case of each meter we list. Where their listings and manuals disagree, as on the KAIWEETS KM602's CAT rating and the HT118A's maximum voltage, we use the lower figure from the manual or the meter face. They lack the dealer network of Klein or Fluke, so check warranty terms before buying.

Do I need Bluetooth on a budget multimeter?+

Only if you want to record readings over time. The OWON B41T+ sends data to a phone over Bluetooth and stores up to 10,000 points by itself, which helps when watching a battery drain overnight or a temperature over an afternoon. For checking a fuse or an outlet, Bluetooth adds pairing steps and nothing else, so most buyers are better served by a plainer meter.
