Siglent SDS1104X-U vs SDS1104X-E: What the Cheaper Model Drops

Siglent SDS1104X-U vs SDS1104X-E is the rare comparison the maker has written up itself. Siglent's application note says both four-channel scopes share a similar platform: 100 MHz, 1 GSa/s, 100,000 wfm/s and a 7-inch 800 × 480 screen. The X-U drops the second converter and memory module, which halves its four-channel sample rate, and loses Bode plot, the logic option, web control and the 500 µV/div scale.

Spec by spec

Every chart here is a number Siglent itself put in its X-E against X-U application note.

With one channel on, the two are effectively the same scope: 1 GSa/s and 14 Mpts each. The gap opens with the second channel on the X-U and with the third and fourth on both. At 250 MSa/s the X-U gets 2.5 samples per cycle at its 100 MHz bandwidth with four channels on; the X-E gets 5.

What each one can and can't measure

Siglent SDS1104X-U: the same scope for one channel

Most of the time on a hobby bench is spent on one or two channels, and with one channel on the X-U matches the X-E. It keeps the 100,000 wfm/s update rate, the 80,000-frame history, a 3.5 ns typical rise time and standard decode of I2C, SPI, UART, CAN and LIN. What it can't do is grow: there is no logic option, no Bode plot and no web control, and its 128 kpts FFT gives coarser spectra than the X-E's 1 Mpt.

Siglent SDS1104X-E: the version with room to expand

The X-E's second converter is what keeps 500 MSa/s per channel with four channels on, and its second memory module keeps 14 Mpts per channel on two channels from different pairs. Siglent lists an on-screen Bode plot that drives an external Siglent SDG or SAG generator, an optional 16-channel logic upgrade and web browser control. Its 500 µV/div scale helps a little with small signals, though both are 8-bit scopes.

Buy the X-U only if you're sure about the extras

The X-U saves money by removing things you can't add later: a converter, a memory module, the logic option and Bode plot. If there is a reasonable chance you'll want a logic analyzer or frequency-response plots within a couple of years, the X-E is the cheaper path in the end.

Earthed probes on both

Both are mains-powered with probe grounds tied to earth. A ground clip on a live conductor causes a short through the scope and the probe. Use a differential probe for mains circuits, and an electrician for building wiring.

The question with the SDS1104X-U: the second channel

The X-U's quietest limit is memory, not sample rate. As soon as a second channel is on, it drops to 7 Mpts per channel, while the X-E keeps 14 Mpts if the two channels sit in different pairs. For input-against-output work on long timebases, that halves the X-U's full-rate record. The X-E is the scope our owner tested; this comparison uses Siglent's published figures only.

The bottom line

Buy the Siglent SDS1104X-E if you will run four channels on fast signals, want Bode plots or may add logic channels: two converters keep 500 MSa/s per channel with four on, and it is the one our owner has tested. Buy the Siglent SDS1104X-U if you mostly use one or two channels and won't need those extras: with one channel on it matches the X-E, with the same 100 MHz, update rate, screen and standard decode.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Siglent's X-U and X-E?+

Per Siglent's own application note: the X-E has two converters and two 14 Mpt memory modules against one of each, 500 µV/div against 1 mV/div sensitivity, an on-screen Bode plot, external generator control, an optional 16-channel logic upgrade, web control and a 1 Mpt FFT against 128 kpts. Bandwidth, update rate and screen are shared.

Can I add logic channels to the SDS1104X-U?+

No. Siglent's application note lists the optional 16-channel logic upgrade for the four-channel X-E models and none for the X-U. If you think you'll need a logic analyzer, buy the X-E or plan on a separate USB logic analyzer alongside the X-U.

Is the SDS1104X-U's 250 MSa/s with four channels a problem?+

Only for fast signals. At 250 MSa/s a 10 MHz clock still gets 25 samples per cycle, plenty for a clean trace. At 50 MHz it gets 5 and at 100 MHz only 2.5. If your four-channel work involves signals above about 25 MHz, the X-E's 500 MSa/s is worth paying for.

Do both include serial decoding?+

Yes, as standard. Siglent lists I2C, SPI, UART, CAN and LIN trigger and decode on the SDS1104X-U, and I2C, SPI, UART, RS232, CAN and LIN on the SDS1104X-E. RS232 is the same UART framing at RS232 levels, so in practice the decode sets match.

How do I get full memory on the SDS1104X-E with two channels?+

Use one channel from each pair, such as CH1 and CH3 or CH2 and CH4. Siglent's application note says that keeps 14 Mpts per channel; using both channels of one pair, such as CH1 and CH2, drops each to 7 Mpts. The X-U gives 7 Mpts with any two channels.
