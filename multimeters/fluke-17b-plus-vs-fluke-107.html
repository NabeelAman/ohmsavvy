Fluke 17B+ vs Fluke 107

Fluke 17B+ vs Fluke 107 is closer than it looks: unlike most pocket meters, the 107 reads frequency, duty cycle, capacitance and diodes, so it covers much of what the 17B+ does. What the bigger meter adds is temperature, microamp and milliamp ranges, MIN MAX, relative mode and a higher voltage ceiling, while the 107 counts to 6,000 in a 200 g case.

What the temperature function is worth

The 17B+ ships with a Type K probe and reads −55 °C to 400 °C. Fluke's accuracy is banded: ±2 °C from 0 to 50 °C, 2% ±1 °C above 50 °C, and 9% ±2 °C below freezing. Worked through, that is the spread below. It is plenty for a supply-air check, a heatsink or a freezer, though a dedicated thermometer is better for refrigerant superheat.

The 107 has no thermocouple input at all, so for temperature you'd carry a separate thermometer. Our guide to checking a thermocouple with a multimeter shows the millivolt side of the same job, which the 107 can only do on AC.

Use cases in practice

On a workbench the 17B+ earns its size. Relative mode zeros a reference reading so you see only the change, MIN MAX holds the dip when a motor starts, and the microamp range reads sleep current on a battery-powered board. Its two AA cells are rated for at least 500 hours, so it rarely dies mid-job.

Away from the bench the 107 makes more sense. It reads the frequency of a generator, the duty cycle of a fan controller and the capacitance of a start capacitor, which covers most quick field checks, and Fluke rates it to survive a 1 m drop. What it can't do is read a current below a few tens of milliamps with useful resolution: on its 4.000 A range one count is 1 mA.

A note on True RMS

Neither meter is True RMS, so both read clean sine-wave mains correctly but can misread the chopped waveforms of dimmers, drives and switch-mode supplies. If that is your work, step up to a True RMS meter such as the Fluke 117 or 115.

Safety: both are CAT III 600V meters

The 17B+ displays up to 1000 V, but both meters carry a CAT III 600V rating, so neither belongs at a service entrance or meter base. Check the leads are in the volts jack before probing an outlet, because a lead left in the A jack shorts the circuit through the fuse. On a hot heatsink or stove element, let the 17B+'s probe do the touching, not your hand, and call an electrician for anything inside a panel you aren't trained to open.

Choose the Fluke 17B+ if…

  • ✓You want temperature readings with a probe included
  • ✓You measure small currents in µA or mA, such as standby or sensor loops
  • ✓You log fluctuating readings with MIN MAX or need relative mode

Choose the Fluke 107 if…

  • ✓You want frequency and duty cycle in something that fits a pocket
  • ✓You value a 6,000-count display over extra functions
  • ✓You want a meter Fluke rates for a 1 m drop

The bottom line

Pick the 107 if you want frequency and duty cycle in a pocket-size meter with a finer display; it covers most everyday electronics checks. Pick the 17B+ if temperature, milliamp ranges, MIN MAX logging or readings above 600V matter, and you don't mind a full-size meter.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Fluke 107 measure frequency like the 17B+?+

Yes. Fluke lists frequency from 10 Hz to 100 kHz and duty cycle from 1% to 99% on both meters, with the same ±(0.1% + 3) frequency accuracy. Its sibling, the Fluke 106, lacks both functions, which is the main reason to choose the 107 over the 106.

Is the Fluke 107 True RMS?+

No. Fluke states plainly that the 107 is not a True RMS meter, and it makes no True RMS claim for the 17B+ either. Both read pure sine-wave mains accurately, but on variable-speed drives, LED drivers and dimmers their AC readings can be off. For that work, look at a True RMS Fluke such as the 115 or 117.

Can the Fluke 107 measure milliamps?+

Not usefully. The 107 has only 4.000 A and 10.00 A current ranges on a single 11 A fused input, so its smallest step is 1 mA and its accuracy band on a 50 mA load is several milliamps wide. The 17B+ has dedicated 40.00 mA and 400.0 mA ranges, plus microamp ranges.

Can the Fluke 107 measure temperature?+

No. Temperature is a 17B+ feature here, using its included Type K probe across −55 °C to 400 °C. Fluke rates it at ±2 °C between 0 and 50 °C and 2% ±1 °C above that, which suits supply-air, pipe and heatsink checks. The 107 has no thermocouple input.

Which has more resolution, the 17B+ or the 107?+

The 107, with a 6,000-count display against the 17B+'s 4,000 counts. In practice that matters between 4 V and 6 V, and between 400 V and 600 V, where the 107 stays on a finer range. A 5 V rail reads to 0.001 V on the 107 and 0.01 V on the 17B+.
