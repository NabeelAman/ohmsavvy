Why True RMS matters, and what it doesn't fix

A True RMS meter computes the real heating value of an AC signal from its shape, so it stays accurate when the waveform is clipped, peaky or chopped. That is the norm today: LED drivers, phone chargers, dimmers, inverters and variable-speed drives all distort voltage or current. An average-responding meter assumes a clean sine wave and can be badly off on those loads. True RMS doesn't make a meter accurate at any frequency, though; it only holds within the bandwidth the maker specifies, which is why we favored meters that publish one.

Every meter here is True RMS and was listed under $100 on Amazon.com when we checked in October 2026; prices change, so the buy button shows the live figure. This is a narrower list than our best multimeter under $100 guide, which also admits average-responding meters. For background, read the multimeter buying guide and multimeter accuracy for electronics repair; for a close look at a cheaper True RMS option, see our UNI-T UT139C review and the KAIWEETS KM200s vs UNI-T UT139C comparison.

True RMS is not a safety feature

Accurate AC readings don't make a meter safe on mains. What protects you is the CAT rating on the meter and leads, fused current inputs and correct lead placement. Use a CAT III 600V or better meter in distribution panels and CAT IV 600V at the service entrance, never put leads that sit in the current jacks across a voltage, and keep fingers behind the probe guards.

What to look for in a True RMS meter on a budget

True RMS on the functions you use

Some meters apply True RMS to AC volts only. Check that AC current is covered too if you measure loads.

Published AC bandwidth

True RMS accuracy holds only inside the meter's specified frequency range; outside it, readings drift.

Display counts

20,000-plus counts give an extra digit on the same range, useful for sensor signals and small AC voltages.

A low-pass filter

Strips the switching carrier from a variable-frequency drive's output so the meter reads the motor voltage.

Safety rating you can trust

Prefer a rating the maker documents; where only the case marking states it, treat it with a little caution.

Current ranges

A µA range helps with electronics and flame sensors; 10 A or 20 A covers small appliance loads in series.

Bandwidth is the fine print of True RMS

UNI-T specifies the UT61E+'s AC voltage accuracy from 40 Hz to 10 kHz, while Klein's MM420 manual specifies 50 Hz to 400 Hz. Both are True RMS, and both read a 60 Hz outlet correctly, but only the UNI-T is specified for the harmonics and higher-frequency AC you meet in electronics. Our verified data has no such figure for most of the others, so check the manual before trusting their high-frequency readings. Before you buy

Buying advice

Match the meter to the AC you measure

Mains voltage and motor current sit at 50 or 60 Hz, which every meter here handles. Audio signals, inverter outputs and switching-supply ripple run far higher, and a meter specified only to a few hundred hertz will under-read them. If you measure above mains frequency, choose a meter that publishes its AC bandwidth, like the UT61E+, and stay inside it.

Pick counts or toughness, then fill in the rest

The list splits into two camps. The UT61E+, B41T+ and OW18E give about 20,000 counts or more for bench work. The MM720, HT118A and the Klein 400-series give 4,000 to 6,000 counts in tougher, simpler cases with features aimed at electrical work. Decide which side you need first; it narrows the choice quickly.

Treat case markings as the weaker evidence

Both OWON meters carry CAT markings on the case, but OWON's own pages don't state a rating. KAIWEETS's KM602 listing claims more than its manual. We report the documented figure where one exists. If you will work in panels, prefer a meter whose maker publishes its CAT rating in the manual, such as the Klein or UNI-T models here.

Check stock before you rely on a pick

Prices and stock at this level move quickly. The MM720 was only a few cents below $100 when we checked, and the OW18E was down to its last unit. If a meter has crossed $100 or vanished, the next pick with the same feature you need (counts, CAT IV or LoZ) is the better substitute.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What does True RMS mean on a multimeter?+

RMS (root mean square) is the DC-equivalent heating value of an AC signal. A True RMS meter calculates it from the actual waveform, so it reads correctly on distorted AC. A cheaper average-responding meter measures the average and multiplies by a factor that is only right for a clean sine wave, so it can read well off on the output of a dimmer, LED driver, switching supply or motor drive.

Do I really need True RMS under $100?+

If you only check batteries, continuity and ordinary outlets, an average-responding meter will be fine. True RMS starts to matter as soon as you measure current drawn by electronic loads, a variable-speed motor, a dimmed circuit or a UPS output. Since several True RMS meters here cost about the same as basic ones, there is little reason to skip it on a new purchase.

Is a 20,000-count True RMS meter better than a 6,000-count one?+

For electronics, usually. More counts mean an extra digit on each range, so a 220.00 mV or 200.00 µA range resolves ten times finer than a 6,000-count range. For mains work, 6,000 counts is plenty and the safety rating, LoZ and toughness matter more. That is why the 6,000-count Klein MM720 ranks second despite its lower count.

Why does my True RMS meter read differently from a cheap one on the same outlet?+

Household voltage is rarely a perfect sine wave, especially with many electronic loads on the circuit, so the two methods disagree slightly. The gap is larger on current: a computer power supply or LED driver draws peaky current that an averaging meter can under-read noticeably. Within its bandwidth, the True RMS reading is the one to trust for heating and load calculations.

Can a True RMS multimeter measure a VFD output?+

It can, but the high-frequency switching carrier on a drive's output can confuse the reading. A low-pass filter helps: the UNI-T UT61E+ has one on its AC voltage function. Without a filter, readings may be erratic or high. Drive outputs carry hazardous voltage, so use a meter rated for the location (CAT III or better) and follow the drive maker's procedure.
