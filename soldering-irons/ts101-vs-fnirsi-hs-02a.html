TS101 vs FNIRSI HS-02A

TS101 vs FNIRSI HS-02A pits two portable irons that both ship with a USB-C charger against each other, and they differ in the details that shape daily use. Miniware's TS101 sets 50 to 400 °C, takes TS100 tips, runs from USB-C PD or a 9 to 24 V DC supply and has a Boost button. FNIRSI's HS-02A sets 100 to 450 °C, is rated at 100 W from USB-C, stores three presets and comes with six tips and a 100 W charger against the TS101's 65 W.

TS101 vs FNIRSI HS-02A: temperature reach

Both ranges cover normal electronics work. Eutectic Sn63/Pb37 solder melts at 183 °C and SAC305 lead-free at about 217 to 220 °C, and common starting tip settings sit around 300 to 350 °C for leaded and 350 to 400 °C for lead-free. The difference is at the top. Only the HS-02A can go past 400 °C.

Those last 50 °C help with heavy, lead-free joints, where the TS101 is already at its limit. The TS101's Boost button covers a related case from the other side: hold it, and Miniware says the tip rises from 300 °C to 400 °C within a short time. For the reasoning behind tip temperature, see soldering iron temperature by metal and leaded vs lead-free solder.

Spec by spec

Tips: an established range against a full kit

Miniware says TS100 tips fit the TS101. If you owned a TS100, your tips carry over, and you pick the shapes yourself. FNIRSI takes the opposite approach: its own tip design, with six shapes in the HS-02A kit (K, KU, JS, I, C2 and B). That covers knife, chisel and fine conical work on day one. The catch is that FNIRSI lists a different tip set for the HS-02B (I, IS and K), so check you buy HS-02A tips.

Count the tips you will actually use

Most electronics work runs on two or three tips: a fine conical or bevel for small pads, a chisel for general joints and a broad knife or chisel for wires and ground planes. The HS-02A kit covers that range. With the TS101 you choose your own TS100 tips, which suits someone who already knows the shapes they like.

The wattage question with the TS101

Miniware's site is unreachable, so the TS101's specs come from its Amazon listing, and that listing states power two ways. The Wattage field says 65 W, matching the adapter in the box. A bullet says USB-C "PD power up to 100W". We don't quote either as the iron's rating. In practice the 65 W adapter sets the ceiling until you supply something bigger. FNIRSI's 100 W rating is on its own product page, and its kit charger matches it.

The TS101 does have one power trick the HS-02A lacks: a DC input up to 24 V, so a laptop brick or a bench supply works as well as a USB-C charger. The HS-02A needs a USB-C PD or QC source every time.

Portable irons and safe handling

Both tips reach at least 400 °C. Use a stand every time you put the iron down, keep the cable away from the hot end, and unplug the iron when you leave the bench. The HS-02A's child lock and auto power-off and the TS101's motion-sensing sleep help, but don't replace unplugging. Work with ventilation or a fume extractor to keep flux smoke away from your face.

  • ✓You want to edit a parameter file for custom heating curves

  • ✓You want headroom to 450 °C for heavy joints
  • ✓You want the bigger charger in the box: 100 W against 65 W
  • ✓You switch between set temperatures and want three presets

You want a portable iron with a clearly published power figure and the lightest body. The Pinecil V2 lists 88 W at 24 V and weighs 28 g with its tip, though it ships without a charger, stand or extra tips.

The bottom line

Buy the FNIRSI HS-02A for the higher ceiling and the fuller kit: 100 to 450 °C, a 100 W rating stated on FNIRSI's site, a 100 W charger, six tips and three presets. Buy the Miniware TS101 if you already have TS100 tips, want a 9 to 24 V DC input or like a Boost button and editable firmware, and can live with power figures stated only on its Amazon listing.

Frequently asked questions

Is the FNIRSI HS-02A better than the Miniware TS101?+

On published figures the HS-02A has the higher temperature ceiling (450 °C against 400 °C), the bigger kit charger (100 W against 65 W), presets and calibration. The TS101 wins on tip availability, since TS100 tips fit, and on input flexibility, with a 9 to 24 V DC jack. Which is better depends on those needs.

Do TS100 tips fit the FNIRSI HS-02A?+

FNIRSI doesn't say they do. It lists its own tips for the HS-02A (I, JS, K, KU, B and C2) and a different set for the HS-02B. Miniware says TS100 tips fit the TS101. If you have a drawer of TS100 tips, the TS101 is the iron that uses them.

How many watts is the Miniware TS101?+

Its Amazon listing gives two answers: 65 W in the Wattage field and "PD power up to 100W" in a bullet. Miniware's own site is unreachable, so we can't settle it. The kit's 65 W USB-C adapter sets the practical ceiling unless you use a larger PD charger or a DC supply.

Can the TS101 run from a laptop power brick?+

Yes, if the brick's voltage is between 9 and 24 V and its plug fits the TS101's DC input. Miniware lists DC 9 to 24 V alongside USB-C PD 9 to 20 V. The HS-02A only accepts USB-C, so it needs a PD or QC charger or power bank.

Which is better for lead-free solder?+

Both cover typical lead-free work, which usually starts around 350 to 400 °C with SAC305 melting near 217 to 220 °C. The HS-02A has more headroom with a 450 °C ceiling. The TS101 tops out at 400 °C, which its Boost button reaches from 300 °C, so heavy lead-free joints push it to its limit.
