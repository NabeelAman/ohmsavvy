An 8-bit converter splits the vertical span into 2⁸ = 256 levels; a 12-bit one splits it into 2¹² = 4,096. If the screen spans eight vertical divisions at 1 V/div, that 8 V range gives steps of about 31 mV on the SDS1202X-E and roughly 2 mV on the DHO924S, assuming the converter covers exactly the visible screen. You notice the difference when you zoom into a small detail riding on a bigger signal: power-supply ripple on a 12 V rail, a sensor output that moves by a few millivolts, or the tail of a slow settling waveform. In those cases the coarser scope often forces you to change the volts-per-division setting just to see the detail; the finer one can keep the whole signal on screen and still resolve it.
