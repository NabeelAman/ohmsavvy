Fluke 17B+ vs Fluke 115: Which Offers Better Value?

In a Fluke 17B+ vs Fluke 115 comparison, value means what each meter gives you for its tier, and the two spend it differently. The 17B+ packs in functions: temperature with a supplied probe, six current ranges down to microamps, frequency to 100 kHz, duty cycle and relative mode. The 115 spends it on measurement quality: True RMS, a 6,000-count display with a fast bar graph, and three years of warranty instead of one.

What "better value" means for these two Flukes

We don't quote prices because they move every week. Value here is a question of what each model includes at its level of Fluke's range. Count the functions and the 17B+ wins easily: it measures temperature, duty cycle and four small-current ranges the 115 lacks, and adds relative mode and a hazardous-voltage warning LED. Weigh how good each measurement is, and the 115 pulls back: it is True RMS, its display resolves 50% more counts, and Fluke backs it for three times as long.

Two kinds of value

The 17B+ is value by breadth: one meter covers more jobs. The 115 is value by depth: fewer jobs, but readings you can trust on the AC loads found in modern buildings. Decide which kind your work needs before comparing anything else.

Accuracy worked through on everyday readings

Both meters list ±0.5% of reading for DC volts and ±1.0% for AC volts. The difference lives in the counts term and in which range a reading lands on. A 4,000-count meter tops out at 3.999 V on its lowest DC range, so a 5 V rail has to move up to the 40.00 V range, where one count is 10 mV. The 115 keeps the same 5 V rail on its 6.000 V range, where one count is 1 mV. Here is the worst-case arithmetic from Fluke's published figures:

Reading17B+ worst case115 worst case

12.60 V car battery±(0.063 + 0.03) = ±0.093 V±(0.063 + 0.02) = ±0.083 V

On a 120 V receptacle the two are identical on paper, as long as the waveform is a clean sine. On a car battery the gap is 10 mV, too small to change any diagnosis. The 5 V case is where the 115's extra counts show up: half the uncertainty, and a third decimal place on screen. These are spec limits, not readings from our bench; a calibrated meter normally sits well inside them.

Watching a reading move: bar graph vs digits

Digits are slow by design so you can read them. Fluke lists 3 display updates a second for the 17B+ and 4 for the 115, but the 115 also has a 33-segment analog bar graph that refreshes 32 times a second. That matters when a value won't sit still: a sagging battery under cranking load, a loose connection that flickers, or a capacitor charging on the resistance range.

The 17B+ answers with recording instead of speed. MIN MAX holds the highest and lowest values seen, and relative mode zeroes the display against a reference, which helps when you subtract test-lead resistance from a capacitance reading or compare cells in a pack. Fluke's manual notes relative mode isn't available on the resistance or continuity functions. The 115 records too, adding an average and elapsed time to its Min/Max, and its continuity beeper catches opens or shorts as brief as 500 µs.

Small currents and temperature: the 17B+'s edge

If any of your work happens below one amp, the 17B+ is the only option of the two. Its 400.0 µA range resolves 0.1 µA, and its 40.00 mA range reads a 4-20 mA transmitter loop to 0.01 mA. The 115's best resolution is 1 mA, so a 12 mA loop shows as 0.012 A and a parasitic drain of 30 mA is barely more than a rounding error. Our guides to measuring parasitic battery drain and 4-20 mA loop testing show why that resolution matters.

Temperature is the other gap. The 17B+ reads a Type K thermocouple from −55 °C to 400 °C with the bead probe in the box, enough for supply and return air, heatsinks and appliance elements. Fluke's datasheet puts thermocouple input on the 116, not the 115, so a 115 owner needs a separate thermometer.

The 17B+'s 1000V range isn't a higher mains rating

The 17B+ can display up to 1000V, but on building wiring it carries the same CAT III 600V rating as the 115; its CAT II 1000V rating applies to plug-connected equipment. Neither meter is rated CAT IV, so neither belongs on a service drop or meter base. Verify the meter on a known live source before and after any dead test, and call a licensed electrician for panel or service work.

Choose the Fluke 17B+ if…

  • ✓You want the most measurement functions in one Fluke body, including temperature
  • ✓You read sensor loops, standby drain or other currents below 1 A
  • ✓You mostly measure DC, resistance and clean mains rather than drive or LED-driver outputs

Choose the Fluke 115 if…

  • ✓You measure AC on electronic loads, where a True RMS reading is the trustworthy one
  • ✓You want a finer display and a bar graph that tracks drifting readings
  • ✓You'd rather have a three-year warranty than extra functions you rarely use

The bottom line

For breadth, the Fluke 17B+ is the better value: temperature, microamp-to-milliamp current ranges, duty cycle and relative mode in one body. For measurement quality on AC, the Fluke 115 is the better value: True RMS, 6,000 counts, a fast bar graph and a three-year warranty. Electronics, appliance and automotive users should lean 17B+; anyone measuring modern AC loads should pick the 115.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Fluke 17B+ better than the Fluke 115?+

Neither is better overall. The 17B+ does more things: temperature, duty cycle, relative mode and current ranges from 400 µA. The 115 does fewer things more reliably on AC, because it is True RMS with a 6,000-count display and bar graph. Choose by the job: electronics and appliances favor the 17B+, while LED lighting and motor-drive work favor the 115.

Does the Fluke 115 measure milliamps?+

Only coarsely. Fluke lists 6.000 A and 10.00 A ranges for the 115, so its smallest step is 1 mA and there is no microamp range. That is enough for checking a motor or heater current, but not for reading a 4-20 mA loop precisely or tracing a small parasitic drain. The 17B+ has dedicated 400.0 µA, 4000 µA, 40.00 mA and 400.0 mA ranges.

Why does the Fluke 115 have a longer warranty than the 17B+?+

Fluke simply assigns different terms to different ranges. Its 15B+/17B+/18B+ users manual gives one year from shipment, while the 110 Series, which includes the 115, carries three years. Fluke doesn't explain the reasoning. If long coverage is part of how you judge value, the 115 has a clear advantage here.

Can the Fluke 17B+ test a capacitor as well as the 115?+

For small and medium parts, yes. The 17B+ starts with a 40.00 nF range and reaches 1,000 µF, which covers HVAC run capacitors and most board-level electrolytics. The 115 reaches 9,999 µF, so it can also read the large filter capacitors found in amplifiers, inverters and power supplies. Always discharge a capacitor before you test it.

Which is lighter to carry, the 17B+ or the 115?+

The 17B+, at 455 g against 550 g, per Fluke. It is slightly larger, at 183 × 91 × 49.5 mm against 167 × 84 × 46 mm. Batteries differ too: the 17B+ runs on two AA cells for at least 500 hours, while the 115 uses one 9V battery rated for about 400 hours typical.

Is the 17B+ accurate enough for household electrical checks?+

On ordinary outlets and resistive loads, yes. Both meters share ±(1.0% + 3) AC volts accuracy and the same CAT III 600V rating, so a 120 V receptacle reads within the same ±1.5 V on either. The caution is distorted waveforms: dimmers, LED drivers and variable-speed motors can make a non-True-RMS meter read high or low.
