Fieldpiece SC260 vs Fieldpiece SC480

Fieldpiece SC260 vs SC480 compares the two ends of Fieldpiece's compact clamp-meter line. They share the same 1.2 in (30 mm) jaw and both read True RMS, which is easy to miss since the SC480 is often assumed to be the first True RMS model in the pair — it isn't. What actually separates them is current range (400 A vs 600 A), safety category (CAT III 600V vs CAT IV 600V / CAT III 1000V), a step up from a 4,000-count to a 10,000-count dual display, and a set of HVACR-specific extras the SC480 alone carries: DC microamps for flame sensors, inrush mode for motor starting current, active power and power factor, three-phase rotation, a VFD low-pass filter, and wireless reporting to Fieldpiece's Job Link app.

Spec by spec

The SC260 and SC480 look almost identical in the hand — same jaw, same backlight behavior, same NCV — and the gap between them is really about ceiling and instrumentation, not size. The charts below use Fieldpiece's own published numbers where the product pages give them, and this site's already verified catalog entries (sourced to each model's operator's manual) where they don't.

Two specs that sound like they should differ, don't. Both pages describe the current and voltage functions as True RMS, so the SC260 isn't a budget average-responding meter next to a True RMS SC480 — both read distorted HVAC loads (ECM blower motors, variable-speed drives) correctly. And neither fieldpiece.com product page states a jaw dimension, but retailer spec sheets for both models agree on the same 1.2 in (30 mm) opening, so the SC480's extra capability doesn't come with room for a bigger conductor or bus bar.

What each one can and can't measure or do

A compressor doesn't draw a flat current from the moment it starts. It pulls a brief, large locked-rotor spike as the motor comes up to speed, then settles to a much lower running current. Catching that spike is one of the clearest, most concrete gaps between these two meters.

Fieldpiece SC260: the basic compact clamp

Within its 400 A ceiling, the SC260 covers most single residential and light-commercial AC/R loads: window units, split-system condensers, blower motors, control transformers. It reads True RMS current and voltage, checks capacitance on run and start capacitors, reads Type K temperature, and its NCV catches a live branch circuit or a 24 VAC thermostat line. Fieldpiece's operator's manual rates it CAT III 600V, which covers panelboards and feeders inside a building but not service equipment or anything the SC480 is rated for above 600V. It has no microamp range, so flame-sensor checks aren't possible, no power or phase-rotation functions, and no wireless reporting.

Fieldpiece SC480: the instrumented power clamp

The SC480 does everything the SC260 does, at a higher current ceiling and a higher safety category (CAT IV 600V / CAT III 1000V), and adds the jobs a basic clamp can't touch: a DC microamp range for flame sensors, active power, VA, VAr and power factor for equipment efficiency checks, three-phase rotation (clip one lead to L1 and probe the others), a low-pass filter so a VFD's switching carrier doesn't inflate an output reading, and inrush mode for starting current. Readings can report wirelessly to the Job Link System app instead of being copied off the meter by hand. Neither meter clamps DC current — both are AC-only through the jaw — and Fieldpiece doesn't publish an exact low-ohm range or current-wattage limits for the SC480 on its product page.

True RMS on both, despite the common assumption

It's easy to assume the cheaper, simpler-looking SC260 is an average-responding meter and that True RMS is the SC480's upgrade. Fieldpiece's own SC260 product page states True RMS plainly, and this site's already-verified catalog entry agrees. The real upgrade in the SC480 is current range, safety category, display resolution and the HVACR-specific functions above — not the RMS math.

Neither clamps DC current

Both meters measure AC current only through the jaw; Fieldpiece lists no DC amps function for either model, through the clamp or otherwise. If you need to clamp DC current — a solar string, a battery bank, a DC bus — look at a meter built for it, such as the solar-rated clamps in our solar panel testing guide, or measure DC current with a shunt and a bench meter instead.

The bottom line

The Fieldpiece SC260 and SC480 share the same 1.2in jaw, the same backlight and NCV behavior, and both are True RMS — the common assumption that the SC480 is the first True RMS model in the pair doesn't hold up against Fieldpiece's own SC260 page. The real split is current range and instrumentation: the SC260 covers 400 A and CAT III 600V work in a lighter, longer-battery-life body; the SC480 covers 600 A at CAT IV 600V / CAT III 1000V and adds DC microamps, inrush mode, power/PF, phase rotation, a VFD filter and wireless Job Link reporting.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Fieldpiece SC260 True RMS?+

Yes. Fieldpiece's own SC260 product page states True RMS for the meter, and this site's already-verified catalog entry agrees. It's a common assumption that only the step-up SC480 is True RMS, but that isn't correct — both models read AC current and voltage as True RMS.

What's the real difference between the SC260 and SC480?+

Current ceiling (400 A against 600 A), safety category (CAT III 600V against CAT IV 600V / CAT III 1000V), display resolution (4,000 against 10,000 counts), and a set of HVACR extras only the SC480 has: DC microamps for flame sensors, inrush mode for starting current, active power/PF, three-phase rotation, a VFD low-pass filter, and wireless reporting to the Job Link app. The jaw opening, NCV behavior and backlight are the same on both.

Do the SC260 and SC480 have different jaw sizes?+

No, as far as published specs show. Neither fieldpiece.com product page states a jaw dimension, but retailer spec sheets for both models (Zoro for the SC260, TestEquipmentDepot for the SC480) independently list the same 1.2 in (30 mm) opening. The SC480's higher current rating comes from its electronics, not a bigger clamp.

Can either meter measure DC current?+

Not through the jaw. Fieldpiece lists AC current only for both the SC260 and SC480's clamp function. The SC480 does add a DC microamp range, but that's read through its test leads for flame-sensor checks, not through the clamp, and it isn't a DC amps range in the ordinary sense.

Which one should an HVAC technician buy?+

If your calls stay under 400 A and CAT III 600V — most residential and light-commercial split systems — the SC260 is lighter, runs longer on a battery and costs less. Choose the SC480 if you also chase flame-sensor microamp faults, diagnose hard-starting compressors with inrush mode, check power factor or phase rotation, or want readings logged wirelessly to the Job Link app.
