UNI-T UT61E+ vs Fluke 117: Bench Precision or Field Safety?

UNI-T UT61E+ vs Fluke 117 is a fair fight only if you know which bench you work at. The UT61E+ is the more precise instrument: 22,000 counts, ±(0.05% + 5) on its 2.2 V and 22 V ranges, and a 220 µA range that reads to 0.01 µA. The Fluke 117 reads to 6,000 counts with no current range below 6 A, but it carries the two tools an electrician uses every day, LoZ and non-contact voltage, plus a 1000 V-rated fuse and a 3-year warranty.

UNI-T UT61E+ vs Fluke 117 on the numbers

The UT61E+ wins every resolution and accuracy figure on the spec sheet. That is not a knock on the 117. Fluke built the 117 for electricians, where a reading of 120.4 V or 120 V makes no difference, and it never claims to be a bench meter. UNI-T built the E+ for exactly that bench.

The arithmetic behind that second chart: on the UT61E+'s 22.000 V range, 0.05% of 12.600 V is 0.0063 V and 5 digits at 1 mV is 0.005 V, so ±0.0113 V. On the 117's 60.00 V range, 0.5% is 0.063 V and 2 digits at 10 mV is 0.02 V, so ±0.083 V. Both are good enough to tell a charged lead-acid battery from a flat one. Only the UNI-T is good enough to check a 2.500 V reference or see a 5 mV drop across a connector. If you want to see why the last digit matters on a board, read multimeter accuracy for electronics repair.

Two jobs, two different winners

Take an illustrative ESP32 board drawing 150 µA in deep sleep. On the UT61E+'s 220.00 µA range that reads 150.00 µA, and ±(0.5% + 10) works out to 0.75 µA plus 0.10 µA, so ±0.85 µA. The 117 shows 0.000 A because its finest current step is 1 mA. You would need a second meter. Our guide to measuring microamps covers the hookup and the burden-voltage trap; UNI-T doesn't publish a burden figure for the E+.

Flip to a house with an abandoned run of cable sharing a box with a live circuit. Any 10 MΩ meter, the UT61E+ included, can show a few tens of volts on that dead wire because stray capacitance couples voltage onto it with almost no current behind it. The 117's low-impedance position loads the wire at about 3 kΩ and the phantom collapses, while a real supply holds up. UNI-T reserves LoZ for the UT61D+. On the E+ you are left guessing, and that is the wrong place to guess. The background is in why a meter shows voltage on a disconnected circuit.

The trust question: a 1000 V rating with 240 V fuses

UNI-T rates the UT61E+ CAT III 1000V / CAT IV 600V, higher on paper than the 117's CAT III 600V. Read further into UNI-T's own manual and the current inputs are protected by a 1 A 240 V and a 10 A 240 V fast-acting fuse, 6 × 25 mm. A fuse's voltage rating is the highest voltage it is specified to interrupt. Fluke puts an 11 A 1000 V fuse in the 117's A input. If you leave the leads in a current jack and touch a 480 V bus, the fuse is the only thing between that energy and you, and only Fluke's is rated for it.

That doesn't make the UT61E+ unsafe on a bench supply, a battery pack or a 120 V appliance cord with the leads in the volts jacks. It does mean the CAT IV 600V line on the box shouldn't be your reason to take it into a service panel. Two smaller paperwork gaps: UNI-T's web page lists the meter at 350 g and 190 × 90 × 50 mm while the manual says 400 g and 186 × 89 × 49 mm, and the warranty in the manual is one year against Fluke's three.

Precision and protection are different specs

Counts and accuracy tell you how well a meter reads. CAT rating, fuse rating and input design tell you what happens when something goes wrong. The UT61E+ leads the first list and the Fluke 117 leads the second, so choose by which failure costs you more: a wrong digit or a wrong assumption about a live wire.

Mains and panel work

Use a meter rated for the category you are working in, with its fuses rated to match, and test it on a known live source before and after you prove a circuit dead. If you are not trained for work inside a panel, call a licensed electrician. For more on categories, read CAT III vs CAT IV.

The bottom line

Buy the UNI-T UT61E+ if your work is on circuit boards: 22,000 counts, ±(0.05% + 5) DC on its middle ranges, a 220 µA range at 0.01 µA and a USB link. Buy the Fluke 117 if your work is in buildings: LoZ for ghost voltage, built-in non-contact detection, an 11 A 1000 V fuse and a 3-year warranty. Neither replaces the other.

Frequently asked questions

Is the UNI-T UT61E+ more accurate than the Fluke 117?+

On published specs, yes for DC volts. UNI-T lists ±(0.05% + 5) on the UT61E+'s 2.2 V and 22 V ranges, against ±(0.5% + 2) for the 117. At 12.6 V that is ±0.011 V against ±0.083 V. On AC volts the gap is smaller: ±(0.8% + 10) against ±(1.0% + 3). Accuracy on paper is not the same as safety rating or build.

Can the Fluke 117 measure microamps?+

No. The 117 has one current input with 6 A and 10 A ranges, and its smallest step is 1 mA. Fluke's 115 and 117 are electrician's meters and leave out µA and mA ranges. For sleep current, flame sensors or 4-20 mA loops at fine resolution, use a meter with a µA range, such as the UT61E+ with its 220.00 µA range.

Does the UNI-T UT61E+ have LoZ mode?+

No. UNI-T's UT61+ datasheet and manual list LoZ ACV only for the UT61D+, the 6,000-count sibling. The UT61E+ keeps an input impedance of about 10 MΩ on its voltage ranges, so it can show ghost voltage on disconnected wiring. The E+ does add a low-pass filter and AC+DC mode, which the D+ lacks.

Is the UT61E+ safe for household electrical work?+

For measuring outlet or appliance voltage with the leads in the volts jacks, its CAT III 1000V rating covers the job. Two cautions: it has no LoZ, so a dead wire can show a phantom voltage, and UNI-T's manual lists 240 V fuses on the current inputs. For proving circuits dead in a panel, an electrician's meter like the Fluke 117 is the better tool.

Can I connect either meter to a computer?+

The UT61E+ ships with a USB cable and UNI-T publishes PC software for it; Bluetooth needs the optional UT-D07B adaptor. The Fluke 117 has no data connection, and Fluke lists no logging or app link for it. If you need to record readings over time, the UNI-T is the only one of the two that can.

Which is better for a beginner learning electronics?+

The UT61E+. Beginners in electronics spend most of their time on low-voltage DC, small currents and component checks, and the E+ covers those with µA, hFE and capacitance ranges the 117 lacks. The 117 suits a beginner electrician, where LoZ and VoltAlert matter more than an extra digit.
