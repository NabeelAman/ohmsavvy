Rigol DHO804 vs Siglent SDS1104X-E

Rigol DHO804 vs Siglent SDS1104X-E is a rare even match on channels: both give you four. What separates them is the kind of headroom each buys. The Siglent has 100 MHz of bandwidth and keeps 500 MSa/s on every channel with all four running. The Rigol stops at 70 MHz but adds 12-bit resolution, 25 Mpts on one channel, a 1024 × 600 touchscreen, HDMI and USB-C power. Both include serial decoding as standard.

With four channels each, the numbers that differ are bandwidth, how the sample rate and memory are shared, resolution and the screen. Rigol figures come from our verified pick data; Siglent's come from its SDS1000X-E series page and data sheet.

Rigol DHO804 vs Siglent SDS1104X-E for a Home Lab

A home lab scope lives on a desk that also holds a soldering station, a bench supply and a laptop, and it gets used for whatever the current project is. Since channel count is tied here, the practical questions are how fast your signals get, how small the details you chase are, and how much the instrument itself should fit around your bench.

The DHO804 suits a bench built around microcontrollers and power. Its 12-bit converter gives 4,096 vertical steps, so the switching ripple on a buck converter's output or the slow drift of a temperature sensor stays legible after you zoom in, where the Siglent's 8-bit converter has 256. Decoding an ESP32's UART log or an I2C sensor read into bytes works on either scope, and with four inputs you can decode a bus while a fourth channel watches a reset or interrupt line. USB-C power and HDMI out make it easy to place: run it from a USB-C source and mirror the screen to the monitor you already have.

The SDS1104X-E suits a bench where signals keep getting faster. Its 100 MHz rating stretches the comfortable range to roughly 20 to 33 MHz, and it holds 500 MSa/s per channel when all four inputs are busy, which is the situation where the Rigol gives up the most. Siglent's interface is also widely regarded as easy to learn, which counts for a lot on a tool you reach for in short sessions after work. It decodes I2C, SPI, UART, CAN and LIN as standard and keeps 7 Mpts per channel with four on, more than the Rigol's 5 Mpts. The trade is 8-bit resolution, a non-touch 800 × 480 screen and mains power instead of USB-C.

What each scope can and can't measure

Bandwidth is defined at the frequency where a sine wave reads 3 dB low, about 70% of its real amplitude. Applying the familiar 3 to 5 times rule, the DHO804 handles signals up to roughly 14 to 23 MHz with good fidelity and the SDS1104X-E up to roughly 20 to 33 MHz. In hobby terms, both cover I2C at 400 kHz, UART, PWM, audio and the switching frequency of most small converters many times over. The gap shows on a 25 MHz SPI clock or a fast logic edge: inside the Siglent's comfortable band, past the Rigol's. More detail is in our explainer on what oscilloscope bandwidth means.

For edges, a widely used estimate puts a scope's own rise time at 0.35 divided by its bandwidth: about 5 ns for 70 MHz and 3.5 ns for 100 MHz. That figure varies with each front end's design, so use it as a guide. A logic edge faster than the scope's own rise time is shown slower and rounder than it really is, which can hide overshoot or make timing between two lines look different from reality.

Sample rate is shared across channels on both. With one channel on, the Rigol samples faster (1.25 GSa/s against 1 GSa/s). With four on, the Siglent keeps 500 MSa/s each against the Rigol's 312.5 MSa/s, so a four-signal capture of a fast bus favors the Siglent twice: more bandwidth and more samples. Our guide to what GSa/s means explains why sample rate per channel is the figure to check.

Neither has a built-in generator: Rigol lists no generator or logic channels for the DHO804, and Siglent offers a USB 25 MHz generator module and 16 digital channels for the four-channel SDS1104X-E only as paid options. If you test filters and amplifiers, budget for a source whichever you choose.

Speed or detail

The Siglent buys headroom for faster signals; the Rigol buys a closer look at slower ones. Ask which of those your next year of projects needs, not which spec sheet looks bigger.

Mains and high-voltage circuits

A scope probe's ground lead is normally earthed through the instrument's supply or whatever else it is plugged into, and USB-C power doesn't change that by itself. Never clip it to a mains-referenced node; use a differential probe for that work.

The bottom line

For a home lab centred on microcontrollers, sensors and power supplies, the Rigol DHO804 gives you more to work with: 12-bit resolution, 25 Mpts on one channel, a touchscreen, HDMI and USB-C power on four channels. Both decode serial buses as standard. If your signals are heading past 15 to 20 MHz, or you often run all four channels on fast signals, the Siglent SDS1104X-E's 100 MHz and 500 MSa/s per channel make it the better long-term scope.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Rigol DHO804 or the Siglent SDS1104X-E better for a home lab?+

For most home benches built around Arduino, ESP32, sensors and power supplies, the DHO804: 12-bit resolution, a 1024 × 600 touchscreen, HDMI and USB-C power. Both include UART, I2C, SPI and CAN decoding. The SDS1104X-E is the better pick when fast clocks and edges are the focus, thanks to 100 MHz and 500 MSa/s per channel with four on.

Is 70 MHz on the DHO804 enough, or do I need the Siglent's 100 MHz?+

By the 3 to 5 times rule, 70 MHz covers signals up to roughly 14 to 23 MHz, which includes most hobby buses, PWM, audio and converter switching. You need the Siglent's 100 MHz, good to roughly 20 to 33 MHz, mainly for faster SPI clocks and for viewing sharp logic edges accurately.

Which samples faster with all four channels on?+

The Siglent SDS1104X-E, at 500 MSa/s per channel against the DHO804's 312.5 MSa/s. With one channel on the order flips: the Rigol reaches 1.25 GSa/s and the Siglent 1 GSa/s. So the Rigol is quicker for single-signal work and the Siglent for four-signal work.

Does the Siglent SDS1104X-E have 12-bit resolution like the DHO804?+

No. Siglent's SDS1000X-E data sheet lists 8-bit vertical resolution, or 256 levels. Rigol specifies the DHO804 as 12-bit, which gives 4,096 vertical levels. If small-signal detail such as ripple decides your purchase, that favors the DHO804.

Can either scope generate a test signal?+

Not on their own. Rigol lists no built-in generator or logic channels for the DHO804. Siglent sells an optional USB 25 MHz generator module for the four-channel SDS1104X-E, which plugs into its USB host port. For filter, amplifier or sensor-input testing, plan on a separate function generator alongside whichever scope you choose.
