FNIRSI 1014D vs Rigol DS1202Z-E

FNIRSI 1014D vs Rigol DS1202Z-E is a choice between two kinds of value. The 1014D is a two-channel 100 MHz scope with a signal generator built in, a 5 V power brick and a 100X probe in the kit. The DS1202Z-E has no generator but doubles the bandwidth to 200 MHz, records up to 24 Mpts, triggers on and decodes UART, I2C and SPI, and carries Rigol's three-year warranty. The short answer: buy the FNIRSI for analog and audio work where a test signal matters; buy the Rigol for digital debugging and long captures.

Key differences

  • →Bandwidth: 200 MHz on the DS1202Z-E against 100 MHz on the 1014D. Rigol's 1.75 ns calculated rise time is the clearer figure; FNIRSI says under 3 ns.
  • →Memory: Rigol lists 24 Mpts on one channel and 12 Mpts on two. FNIRSI writes "240Kbit" without saying how many points each channel gets. Even read generously as 240K points, that is one hundredth of the Rigol's figure.
  • →Generator: the 1014D has a DDS output (sine to 10 MHz, other shapes to 2 MHz, 2.5 Vpp). The DS1202Z-E has none.
  • →Digital debugging: the DS1202Z-E triggers on and decodes RS232/UART, I2C and SPI and also has runt, window, setup/hold and pattern triggers. The 1014D lists edge triggering only.
  • →Sample rate: both reach 1 GSa/s on one channel. Rigol states 500 MSa/s per channel with both on; FNIRSI doesn't state the split.
  • →Power and build: the 1014D runs from a 5 V adapter; the DS1202Z-E takes 100–240 V mains, draws up to 50 W and has a fan.
  • →Paperwork: Rigol publishes a full datasheet with a three-year warranty. The DS1202Z-E Amazon listing is run by a reseller and its attribute table wrongly says battery powered.
  • →Neither carries our Hands-on Tested badge; both assessments are desk research from published specs.

Spec by spec

The bandwidth chart shows the Rigol at twice the FNIRSI. The memory chart is the one that changes how the scopes feel in use, and it needs a log scale because the gap is a factor of one hundred.

Both screens are 7 inches at 800 × 480 and both scopes are 8-bit, so the displays themselves are a draw. Rigol adds intensity-graded color and 30,000 waveforms per second, which means rare glitches show up as faint traces instead of vanishing between updates. FNIRSI doesn't publish an update rate for the 1014D. For background on how bandwidth and sampling interact, see what oscilloscope bandwidth means and sample rate explained.

FNIRSI 1014D vs Rigol DS1202Z-E: where the money goes

The FNIRSI puts its budget into a second instrument. The Rigol puts it into a better scope. That is the whole decision, and the rest of this page is about which of those matters for your bench.

Memory decides what one capture can hold

A scope can only record as much time as its memory allows at a given sample rate. Divide memory by sample rate and you get the window. At 1 GSa/s the DS1202Z-E's 24 Mpts hold 24 ms. With both channels on, Rigol halves both numbers (12 Mpts at 500 MSa/s), so the window stays 24 ms. If the 1014D's "240Kbit" means 240K points, it holds 0.24 ms at 1 GSa/s. To show a longer window it has to drop the sample rate: a 10 ms window on 240K points means no more than 24 MSa/s.

Why that matters in practice: catch a UART burst, a power-up sequence or a motor start in one shot, then zoom in on the edge that went wrong. On the Rigol the zoomed edge still has 1 ns between samples. On a short-memory scope the zoom reveals the coarse sampling it had to use to fit the window. For repetitive signals such as a 1 kHz square wave or 50 kHz PWM, short memory hardly matters because every sweep redraws the same shape.

The generator saves you a second box

The 1014D's DDS output gives 14 standard waveforms plus a custom one, sine to 10 MHz and the other shapes to 2 MHz, at 2.5 Vpp. That covers driving an audio amplifier, injecting a clock into a counter chip, or checking an RC filter's corner frequency with nothing else on the bench. The DS1202Z-E needs a separate generator for those jobs. If you would buy one anyway, that cost sits on the Rigol side of the ledger. If your work is mostly reading signals that a circuit already makes, such as microcontroller pins, supply rails and sensor outputs, you will rarely miss it.

Decoding turns waveforms into bytes

On the 1014D a UART line is a square-ish trace you count bits on by hand. The DS1202Z-E can trigger on a specific UART data value or an I2C address and print the decoded bytes under the trace, on up to two buses at once. Rigol's own sources disagree on whether that costs extra: the E-series datasheet lists the triggers and decoders in the spec table with no option code, while the rigolna.com series page lists serial decoding as an option. Check the seller's configuration before you rely on it. The DS1202Z-E vs SDS1202X-E comparison covers its decoding against a rival that adds CAN and LIN.

What each one can and can't measure

FNIRSI 1014D

With the usual three-to-five-times rule, 100 MHz shows signals up to roughly 20–30 MHz at close to true size. That covers audio, switch-mode supply ripple and switching nodes in the hundreds of kHz, PWM, I2C at 400 kHz and SPI clocks of a few MHz as shapes. It can't decode those buses, its edge trigger can't isolate one rare event in a stream, and FNIRSI doesn't say how the 1 GSa/s divides when both channels run.

Rigol DS1202Z-E

200 MHz stretches the comfortable range to roughly 40–65 MHz, so 25 MHz SPI clocks and fast logic edges keep their shape. The runt trigger catches a pulse that never reached full height, the setup/hold trigger flags a data line that changed too close to its clock, and 60,000-frame recording lets you scroll back through earlier triggers. It can't make a signal, it has two channels only, and at 2.9 kg with a fan it is a bench fixture, not a carry-around tool.

The 1014D's memory figure is the soft spot

"240Kbit" is a unit you rarely see on a scope datasheet. Points per channel is the useful number, and FNIRSI doesn't give it. We charted the most generous reading. If the real per-channel depth is lower, the capture-window gap in the diagram grows.

Both are earth-referenced bench scopes

The DS1202Z-E's mains lead grounds every probe's clip to earth, and the 1014D's adapter can do the same through anything else connected to it. Clip a ground lead onto a live mains point and you create a short circuit. Rigol rates the inputs CAT I 300 Vrms and CAT II 100 Vrms; FNIRSI's 400 V (10X) is an input range, not a safety category. For mains-side work use a differential probe, and if you are not sure, call an electrician.

The trust question: two kinds of paperwork gap

Rigol publishes a seven-page datasheet for the DS1202Z-E with every trigger type, input rating and accessory, but the Amazon listing we link is run by a reseller (GuliTech). Its attribute table says "Battery Powered", which contradicts Rigol's 100–240 V mains specification, and its in-the-box line names only the scope. Rigol lists two PVP2350 350 MHz probes as standard; confirm with the seller that they ship. The 1014D listing comes from FNIRSI's own store and itemizes three probes, but the maker's spec sheet leaves memory per channel and the two-channel sample rate unstated.

Choose the FNIRSI 1014D if…

  • ✓You need a test signal source and don't own a function generator yet
  • ✓Most of your work is audio, power supplies, sensors and filters under a few MHz
  • ✓You want a 100X probe in the box for higher-voltage measurements
  • ✓A light scope on a 5 V adapter suits your bench better than a fan-cooled mains unit

Choose the Rigol DS1202Z-E if…

  • ✓You debug microcontrollers and want UART, I2C and SPI triggers and decoding
  • ✓You need long single-shot captures at full sample rate (24 Mpts on one channel)
  • ✓Your signals have fast edges that a 100 MHz scope rounds off
  • ✓A three-year maker warranty and a full published datasheet matter to you

Neither if…

You need four channels to watch a full SPI bus or a three-phase signal at once, or you need CAN and LIN decoding. Both scopes have two channels and neither lists CAN.

The bottom line

Buy the FNIRSI 1014D if you want a scope and a signal source in one light box for analog, audio and power work under a few MHz. Buy the Rigol DS1202Z-E if you debug digital circuits: 200 MHz, 24 Mpts, UART/I2C/SPI decoding and a three-year warranty, but no generator, and check that the reseller ships Rigol's probes.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Rigol DS1202Z-E worth it over the FNIRSI 1014D?+

For digital work, yes. It has twice the bandwidth, about one hundred times the stated memory and serial triggering and decoding the 1014D lacks, plus a three-year warranty. For analog bench work where you need a test signal, the 1014D's built-in generator can be worth more than the Rigol's extra speed, because the Rigol needs a separate generator.

Does the Rigol DS1202Z-E have a signal generator?+

No. Rigol's DS1000Z-E datasheet lists no source output; the DS1202Z-E has two analog inputs, an external trigger input and an Aux output for trigger out or pass/fail. The FNIRSI 1014D has a DDS generator with sine to 10 MHz and other waveforms to 2 MHz at 2.5 Vpp.

How much memory does the FNIRSI 1014D have?+

FNIRSI writes it as "240Kbit" and doesn't say how many points each channel gets or how memory splits with both channels on. The Rigol DS1202Z-E has 24 Mpts on one channel and 12 Mpts on two, per Rigol's datasheet. Even at 240K points, the FNIRSI has about one hundredth of the Rigol's memory.

Can the FNIRSI 1014D decode I2C or UART?+

FNIRSI doesn't list serial triggering or decoding for the 1014D; its trigger is a rising or falling edge with Auto, Normal and Single modes. You can see I2C or UART as waveforms and read the bits by hand. The DS1202Z-E triggers on and decodes RS232/UART, I2C and SPI, plus parallel buses.

Which is easier to move around the bench?+

The FNIRSI 1014D. Amazon lists it at 3.46 lb, about 1.57 kg, and it is 70 mm deep and runs from a 5 V adapter. The DS1202Z-E weighs 2.9 kg, is 122.4 mm deep, needs a mains outlet and has a cooling fan. Both have 7-inch 800 × 480 screens.

Are the probes included with the DS1202Z-E?+

Rigol's datasheet lists two PVP2350 350 MHz passive probes as standard accessories for the DS1202Z-E. The Amazon listing we link is sold by a reseller and its in-the-box line names only the scope, so confirm with the seller before ordering. The FNIRSI 1014D listing itemizes two 1X/10X probes plus a P4100 100X probe.
