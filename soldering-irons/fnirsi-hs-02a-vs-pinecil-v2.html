FNIRSI HS-02A vs Pinecil V2

FNIRSI HS-02A vs Pinecil V2 comes down to where your power comes from. The HS-02A is rated at 100 W from USB-C alone and ships with a 100 W charger, six tips and a stand. The Pinecil V2 is rated at 88 W, but only from a 24 V DC supply on its barrel jack; on USB-C, Pine64 lists 20 V at 3 A, which caps it at 60 W. Pinecil sells the iron and one B2 tip, nothing else. If you only own USB-C chargers, the HS-02A has the higher ceiling.

FNIRSI HS-02A vs Pinecil V2: what each pulls from your charger

Rated watts only matter if your supply can deliver them. Pine64 lists the Pinecil V2 at 20 V and 3 A over USB-C, which is 60 W. FNIRSI rates the HS-02A at 100 W with a 20 V maximum, and 100 W at 20 V is 5 A. Under the USB PD rules, anything above 3 A needs a 5 A e-marked cable as well as a charger that offers it.

From a 65 W laptop charger the two are close: about 60 W against 65 W. The gap opens with a 100 W supply, which FNIRSI puts in the box. The Pinecil's answer is its barrel jack: give it a 24 V brick and it reaches its full 88 W, and the HS-02A has no barrel input to compete. Pine64 also lists 28 V USB PD EPR as theoretically supported, but calls it tentative, so we don't count it.

Spec by spec

When the extra watts matter

A 0603 resistor or a header pin needs very little power, and both irons hold temperature on those easily. Watts show up when a big copper mass pulls heat out of the tip faster than the heater can put it back: a ground plane, a 12 AWG wire into a battery tab, or a shield can. That is where 100 W against 60 W from the same USB-C port shortens the wait. Our soldering iron wattage guide and thick-wire troubleshooting page cover the physics.

The tip matters as much as the watts

A fine conical tip limits heat flow on any iron. The HS-02A kit includes six shapes (K, KU, JS, I, C2 and B), so you can fit a broad K or chisel-style tip for heavy joints. The Pinecil ships with one short B2 tip; plan on buying a wider one if you solder thick wire. Note that FNIRSI lists a different tip set for the HS-02B, so buy tips marked for the HS-02A.

Controls, firmware and support

The HS-02A has the friendlier controls on paper: a 0.96-inch color screen, three stored presets, calibration to match the display to the real tip temperature, sleep, auto power-off and a child lock. The Pinecil's 0.69-inch, 96 × 16 OLED shows less at once, but it runs Ralim's open-source IronOS, so features and fixes come from a public project you can update yourself.

Support terms differ too. FNIRSI says its 2-year warranty applies to orders placed directly with it. The Pinecil listing on Amazon states a 30-day warranty, and its description still lists the original Pinecil's 108 MHz processor and 12–21 V barrel input rather than the V2's figures. Check the version in the photos before you buy.

Hot tips and power banks

At 450 °C the HS-02A's tip can burn skin and melt cable insulation in a moment. Rest either iron in a stand, never on the bench; the Pinecil doesn't come with one. Neither iron has a battery, so a USB-C power bank must support PD at 12 V or more for the Pinecil, or 9 V or more for the HS-02A. Unplug both when you walk away, and solder with ventilation so flux smoke doesn't sit in your face.

Buy the FNIRSI HS-02A if you want a complete USB-C kit and the most power from USB-C: 100 W rated, with the charger, six tips and a stand in the box. Buy the Pinecil V2 if you already own supplies, want open-source firmware and the lightest iron, or have a 24 V brick to unlock its 88 W.

Frequently asked questions

Is the FNIRSI HS-02A more powerful than the Pinecil V2?+

From USB-C, yes on paper. FNIRSI rates the HS-02A at 100 W with up to 20 V, while Pine64 lists the Pinecil V2 at 20 V and 3 A over USB-C, which is 60 W. From a 24 V DC supply the Pinecil reaches 88 W, but the HS-02A has no barrel input, so it can't use that supply at all.

Do I need a special cable for 100 W on the HS-02A?+

Probably. 100 W at 20 V means 5 A, and the USB PD standard only allows more than 3 A through a cable with a 5 A e-marker chip. FNIRSI supplies a 100 W adapter in the kit but doesn't state the cable's rating. With an ordinary 3 A cable, expect at most 60 W from a 20 V supply.

Can I run either iron from a power bank?+

Yes, if the power bank supports USB-C PD at a high enough voltage. The HS-02A accepts 9 to 20 V and the Pinecil V2 12 to 20 V. A bank that only gives 5 V won't run either iron. Power will be limited by the bank's output, often well below the irons' ratings.

Do Pinecil tips fit the FNIRSI HS-02A?+

Neither maker says so. FNIRSI lists its own tip set for the HS-02A (I, JS, K, KU, B and C2) and a different set for the HS-02B, and Pine64 lists the ST-B2 short tip for the Pinecil V2. Buy tips sold for your exact model rather than assuming they interchange.

Which heats up faster?+

The makers measure different things, so we can't rank them. FNIRSI says the HS-02A can tin in 2 seconds on its site and 3 seconds on Amazon. The Pinecil listing says it reaches operating temperature in 12 seconds. Neither figure states the supply, starting temperature or target temperature used.

What is the maximum temperature of each iron?+

FNIRSI lists 100 to 450 °C for the HS-02A. Pine64's V2 specification page doesn't give a range; the Amazon listing says 100 to 400 °C, though that listing still describes the original Pinecil in places. Most leaded and lead-free electronics work happens well inside both ranges.
