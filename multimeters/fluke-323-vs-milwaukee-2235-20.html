Fluke 323 vs Milwaukee 2235-20

Fluke 323 vs Milwaukee 2235-20 is a close match: both clamp to 400 A AC with True RMS, read 600 V AC and DC, check resistance and continuity, run on two AAA cells and skip DC current. The differences are in the details. Fluke adds a CAT IV 300V rating, a slightly larger 30 mm jaw and a published accuracy spec; Milwaukee adds a backlit display, an LED work light and a 5-year warranty, but publishes no accuracy figure.

The numbers side by side

On the headline figures the two clamps tie: 400 A AC, 600 V, True RMS, two AAA cells. The charts below show the few places where the makers publish different numbers, and one where only Fluke publishes a number at all.

A spec you can calculate with matters more than it sounds. Clamp a 15.0 A load with the 323: 2% of 15.0 A is 0.3 A, and five digits at 0.1 A resolution add 0.5 A, so the true current sits within ±0.8 A of the display, anywhere from 14.2 A to 15.8 A. With the 2235-20 you can't do that sum, because Milwaukee doesn't publish the figure. Its readings may be just as good; you simply have no written spec to hold it to.

CAT IV 300V: what the extra rating covers

Measurement categories describe how much transient energy a meter can survive at a given point in the system. The closer you work to the utility transformer, the higher the category you need. On a US 120/240 V split-phase service, each leg sits at about 120 V to ground, so a CAT IV 300V rating covers the meter base and service entrance conductors, while CAT III 600V covers the panel and everything wired from it.

If your clamp work happens at the main panel, on feeders, or on HVAC and motor circuits, both meters are rated for it. The Fluke's extra category only pays off if you check current on service entrance conductors or at the meter socket, which is common for service upgrades and utility-side troubleshooting. Our CAT III vs CAT IV guide explains the categories in full.

Working at the service entrance

A CAT IV rating doesn't make live work at the meter base safe on its own. Service conductors have no overcurrent protection ahead of them, so a slip can release far more energy than a branch circuit fault. Wear arc-rated PPE suited to the job, keep test leads in their own CAT-rated condition, and leave meter-socket work to a licensed electrician or the utility if it isn't your trade.

Light, resistance and the small print

Fluke 323: a bare instrument with full paperwork

The 323 is the entry clamp in Fluke's 320 family, and it shows: no backlight, no capacitance, no frequency, no temperature and no min/max. What it does have is a complete spec sheet, with accuracy for every function, two resistance ranges to 4000 Ω, a continuity beeper that sounds at 70 Ω or less, and a stated weight of 265 g. If you need DC current, capacitance or temperature, Fluke puts those on the 324 and 325 instead.

Milwaukee 2235-20: built for the job site

Milwaukee pitches the 2235-20 at electricians who work in poor light, and the backlit black-on-white screen and LED work light make that case. The 5-year warranty is two and a half times Fluke's. The weak spot is documentation: no accuracy figures, no size or weight on the product page, and resistance listed as 400 Ω on one Milwaukee page and 4000 Ω on another. Either range covers fuses, switches, heating elements and motor windings, but not kilohm sensor circuits.

Same battery, same blind spot

Both clamps run on two AAA cells and both stop at AC current. Neither has a DC amps range, so neither can check a battery bank, a solar string or a vehicle's alternator output. If that work is coming, buy a DC-capable clamp now rather than a second clamp later; for a meter that pairs a clamp with full multimeter functions, compare the Fluke 117/323 kit.

For more clamp matchups, see Fluke T5-600 vs 323, Fieldpiece SC260 vs Fluke 323 and Fluke 117 vs Milwaukee 2235-20, or start with the clamp meter buying guide.

Choose the Fluke 323 if…

  • ✓You take readings at the meter base or service entrance, where a CAT IV rating applies
  • ✓You want a stated current accuracy you can quote in a report: ±(2% + 5) at 45–65 Hz
  • ✓You clamp conductors up to 600 MCM, beyond the 350 MCM Milwaukee quotes
  • ✓You work in lit spaces and don't need a backlight

Choose the Milwaukee 2235-20 if…

  • ✓You work in attics, crawlspaces and dark panels, where a backlight and work light help
  • ✓You want the longer warranty: Milwaukee lists 5 years against Fluke's 2
  • ✓Your work stays at CAT III: panels, feeders, motors and fixed equipment
  • ✓You want a thin jaw for picking single wires out of a crowded panel

Neither if…

You need DC current for solar, battery or automotive work. Both clamps read AC amps only, so look at a DC-capable clamp meter instead.

The bottom line

Buy the Fluke 323 if you work at the service entrance or need a published accuracy spec: it adds CAT IV 300V, a jaw stated to 600 MCM and ±(2% + 5) current accuracy. Buy the Milwaukee 2235-20 if your clamp work stays at the panel and beyond, often in poor light: its backlight, work light and 5-year warranty are worth more there than a spec sheet.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Fluke 323 more accurate than the Milwaukee 2235-20?+

Nobody can say from the specs, because Milwaukee doesn't publish an accuracy figure for the 2235-20. Fluke rates the 323 at ±(2% + 5 digits) for AC current between 45 and 65 Hz. That doesn't prove the Milwaukee is worse; it means only the Fluke gives you a written tolerance to calculate with and to cite.

Can either clamp meter measure DC amps?+

No. Fluke lists AC current only for the 323; its sources place DC current on the higher 320-series models. Milwaukee rates the 2235-20 for 400 A AC with no DC current range. Both read DC volts to 600 V, so you can check a battery's voltage, but not the current flowing from it.

Does the Fluke 323 have a backlight?+

Fluke doesn't list one, and the 323's spec sheet has no backlight entry. The Milwaukee 2235-20 has a backlit black-on-white display plus an LED work light aimed at the jaw. If you often read current inside dim cabinets, attics or crawlspaces, that difference matters more than any spec in the table.

Which clamp has the bigger jaw?+

The Fluke, on paper. Fluke states a 30 mm maximum conductor, given as 600 MCM. Milwaukee gives a 1.0 inch opening, about 25.4 mm, and its NIST-kit page for the same meter says it fits up to 350 MCM. Both take any residential branch-circuit wire and most service conductors.

Which one has the longer warranty?+

The Milwaukee 2235-20, with 5 years listed on Milwaukee's product page. Fluke gives the 323 a 2-year warranty. Both take two AAA cells, so running costs are the same, and Milwaukee includes test leads in the box. The warranty gap is worth weighing if the clamp will live in a work van.
