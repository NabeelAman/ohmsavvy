Fluke 17B+ vs Fluke 87V: Which Is Better for Professionals?

For professionals, the Fluke 87V is the better meter in the Fluke 17B+ vs Fluke 87V matchup, and the margin is wide. It carries a CAT IV 600V / CAT III 1000V rating, True RMS, DC accuracy ten times tighter, peak capture and a lifetime warranty. The 17B+ still has a place in a professional's kit: as an apprentice's first Fluke, a bench or appliance meter, or a backup with temperature and microamp ranges.

What professionals actually need from a meter

"Professional" covers a lot of trades, but most of them judge a meter on the same five things: where it is safe to use, whether its AC readings hold up on modern loads, how precise it is, whether it can catch a fault that comes and goes, and how long the maker stands behind it. Score the two Flukes against that list and the gap is clear.

Safety rating decides first

If your work includes the meter base, service drop or outdoor feeders, the 17B+ is out before accuracy enters the conversation: it isn't rated CAT IV. Our Fluke 117 vs Fluke 87V comparison maps where each category sits in a building.

The accuracy gap in volts and amps

A tenfold difference in the percentage term sounds abstract until you apply it. The table uses each meter's published basic accuracy and the resolution of the range the reading falls on. The 17B+'s 4,000-count ranges are 4, 40, 400 and 1000 V; the 87V's 6,000-count ranges keep more readings on a finer scale. This is worst-case spec arithmetic, not bench data.

The 48 V case shows the range penalty at its worst. The reading is just over the 17B+'s 40 V range, so it jumps to 400 V and loses a decimal place; the 87V keeps it on its 60 V range. For telecom, solar storage and forklift batteries, where cell balance is judged in tens of millivolts, that is the difference between a useful reading and a rough one. On a 277 V lighting circuit the gap shrinks to about a volt, and the 17B+'s figure assumes a clean sine, since Fluke doesn't call it True RMS.

Catching faults that don't wait for the display

Many service calls aren't about a steady wrong value. A controller resets, a contactor chatters or a drive trips, and the cause lasts a few milliseconds. A display that refreshes three times a second, as the 17B+'s does, almost always samples before or after such an event. The 87V's Peak Capture records events as short as 250 µs, and its 32-segment bar graph updates 40 times a second.

The 17B+ does have MIN MAX recording, which holds the extremes it sees over time, but Fluke doesn't publish a capture speed for it, so don't rely on it for millisecond events. For chasing these faults step by step, see how to find an intermittent electrical fault with a multimeter. The 87V's low-pass filter is the other industrial tool: it strips a drive's switching carrier so the meter reads the voltage the motor responds to, as explained in Fluke 87V vs Fluke 179.

Where the 17B+ holds its own

The 17B+ isn't a toy. It shares the 87V's 1000V display range, reads duty cycle and frequency to 100 kHz, and has separate microamp and milliamp ranges behind a 440 mA fuse. Its temperature input reads −55 °C to 400 °C with the Type K bead probe in the box, and its hazardous-voltage LED lights at 30 V or more. It also runs on two AA cells for at least 500 hours, which are easy to find on a job site.

On temperature the 87V reads a far wider span, −200 °C to 1090 °C, but that figure excludes the probe; the 80BK probe Fluke supplies is rated −40 °C to 260 °C. Furnace stacks or cryogenic lines need a different probe on either meter. For HVAC supply and return air, both cover the job.

Match the meter to the circuit, not the job title

A CAT rating covers both the voltage and the energy available at a point in the system. Using a CAT III 600V meter such as the 17B+ on a service entrance exposes you to fault energy it isn't rated for, whatever its display range. Wear the PPE your site requires, verify the meter on a known live source before and after testing, and follow lockout procedures before working on drives, whose DC bus can stay charged after power is removed.

The bottom line

For professional electrical and industrial work, the Fluke 87V is the better choice: CAT IV 600V, True RMS with a drive filter, ±(0.05% + 1) DC accuracy, 250 µs peak capture and a lifetime warranty. The Fluke 17B+ suits apprentices, appliance and bench technicians, and professionals who want a capable backup with temperature and microamp ranges.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Fluke 17B+ good enough for a professional electrician?+

For some tasks, not as a main meter. It is rated CAT III 600V, so it covers branch circuits and panels up to 600V but not the service entrance, and Fluke doesn't call it True RMS. Many electricians would rather carry a True RMS meter such as the 87V or 117 daily and keep the 17B+ for appliance or control work.

How much more accurate is the Fluke 87V than the 17B+?+

On DC volts, Fluke lists ±(0.05% + 1) for the 87V and ±(0.5% + 3) for the 17B+, so the percentage term is ten times tighter. On a 48 V battery bank that works out to about ±0.034 V against ±0.54 V worst case. On AC volts the gap is smaller: ±(0.7% + 2) against ±(1.0% + 3).

Does the Fluke 87V measure temperature like the 17B+?+

Yes, and over a wider span. Fluke lists −200 °C to 1090 °C for the 87V, excluding the probe, while the supplied 80BK probe is rated −40 °C to 260 °C. The 17B+ reads −55 °C to 400 °C and comes with a Type K bead probe. For HVAC air and pipe temperatures, either meter does the job.

Do the Fluke 17B+ and 87V have non-contact voltage detection?+

Neither lists it. Fluke publishes no non-contact voltage or low-impedance (LoZ) mode for the 87V or the 17B+. If you want those electrician conveniences, the Fluke 117 has both, though it is rated CAT III 600V rather than the 87V's CAT IV 600V. Many professionals carry a separate voltage pen alongside an 87V.

Which Fluke has the better warranty, the 17B+ or the 87V?+

The 87V by a wide margin. Fluke lists a lifetime warranty for it, while the 15B+/17B+/18B+ users manual gives the 17B+ one year from shipment. Read Fluke's warranty terms for what lifetime covers and keep your proof of purchase. For a meter used daily for years, that coverage is part of what separates the two.

Can the Fluke 17B+ read variable-frequency drive outputs?+

Not reliably. Drive outputs are trains of fast pulses, and Fluke makes no True RMS claim for the 17B+ and lists no low-pass filter. The 87V is True RMS and has a selectable low-pass filter, 3 dB down at 1 kHz, that removes the switching carrier so the reading reflects what the motor sees. Always follow lockout procedures around drives.
