Fluke 87V vs Fluke 87V MAX: What Is the Difference?

The Fluke 87V MAX measures like the 87V: Fluke lists the same ±(0.05% + 1) DC volts, the same 6,000-count display with a 19,999-count hi-res mode, the same low-pass filter, 250 µs peak capture, 10 A input and CAT IV 600V / CAT III 1000V rating. What changes is the shell around that core. The MAX is sealed to IP67 instead of IP30, rated for a 4 m drop, runs about 800 hours on three AA cells instead of about 400 on a 9V, and has a backlit keypad. It is also heavier, and its AC volts digit term is slightly looser.

Key differences

  • →Sealing: Fluke rates the 87V MAX IP67 (dust-tight and rated for temporary immersion). The standard 87V is IP30, which means no rated water protection at all.
  • →Drop: the MAX is rated for 4 m. Fluke's comparison table gives the 87V 3 m, while the 87V spec tab lists a 1 m drop per IEC 61010-1, so the honest gap is between 1 m and 3 m.
  • →Power: three AA cells and about 800 hours on the MAX, against a single 9V and about 400 hours on the 87V, both quoted without the backlight on.
  • →Keypad: both have a two-level display backlight, but only the MAX lights its buttons.
  • →Accuracy: DC volts, current, resistance, frequency and temperature specs match. AC volts differ in the digit term: ±(0.7% + 2) on the 87V and ±(0.7% + 4) on the MAX.
  • →Box: the MAX ships with TL175 TwistGuard leads and the 80BK-A probe; the 87V with TL75 leads and the 80BK probe. Both holsters are removable and hold a probe.

The arithmetic behind that last chart: 0.7% of 480 V is 3.36 V. The 87V adds two counts (0.2 V) for ±3.56 V, and the MAX adds four (0.4 V) for ±3.76 V. On a motor or feeder check that difference is smaller than the variation in the supply itself, so treat it as a footnote rather than a reason to choose.

Where Fluke's pages disagree

The 87V's drop rating appears twice on fluke.com: 3 m in the comparison table and 1 m per IEC 61010-1 in the spec tab's shock line. We show both. The comparison table on the MAX page also lists conductance and pulse width under the MAX only, but the 87V spec tab lists conductance to 60.00 nS, and we found no pulse-width range on the MAX spec tab, so we don't count either as a difference.

What IP30 and IP67 actually mean

An IP code has two digits. The first rates protection against solids, the second against water. IP30 says the 87V keeps out a probing finger or tool larger than about 2.5 mm and makes no water claim at all; a zero there means Fluke hasn't rated it, not that it dissolves in drizzle. IP67 says the MAX is dust-tight and survives temporary immersion, which the IEC 60529 test commonly defines as 1 m of water for 30 minutes.

Dust matters as much as water. Cement plants, grain handling, sawmills and mine sites push fine powder into every seam, where it can work into switches and the battery compartment. A dust-tight rating is the reason a meter like the MAX is pitched at those sites, not just at people who work in the rain.

Battery, keypad and holster: the daily-use changes

The power change is easy to underrate. A 9V cell is one more battery type to stock, and Fluke quotes about 400 hours for it. The MAX runs three AA cells for a quoted 800 hours. Put a technician at four hours of meter-on time a day, which is our illustrative assumption, and that is roughly 100 working days per 9V against roughly 200 per set of AAs, before the backlight takes its share.

The backlight is the second change. Both meters have a two-level display backlight; only the MAX lights the buttons as well. In a dark crawlspace or a panel at night, that means finding the HOLD or MIN MAX key without a flashlight in your teeth. It sounds small, and on a night-shift breakdown call it isn't.

Holsters are not a difference, despite how the MAX is sometimes marketed. Fluke describes both as removable and able to hold a test probe. The MAX ships with TL175 TwistGuard leads and the 80BK-A thermocouple; the 87V comes with TL75 leads and the 80BK. Both probes are K-type.

The cold-weather row needs care. The 87V's listed operating range starts lower, at -20 °C, than the MAX's -15 °C. The MAX's advantage is a short excursion: Fluke rates it down to -40 °C for 20 minutes when taken from 20 °C, which suits a meter carried from a warm truck to a frozen disconnect and back.

Is the MAX worth it for a field technician?

Think about how a meter dies in the field rather than how it measures. Indoors, an 87V mostly fails from abuse it can be protected from: a blown fuse from leads left in the A jack, or a fall from a ladder. Outdoors, it faces water in the battery bay, dust in the rotary switch, and drops onto concrete or into standing water. Those are exactly the risks the MAX's rating targets.

So the question is where you spend your hours. A technician who splits time between a dry MCC room and the bench gets nothing measurable from the MAX and carries an extra 74.5 g on the belt. A solar O&M tech, a utility contractor or anyone servicing pumps and lift stations is buying insurance against the most likely way their meter ends its life. For the solar case, our multimeters for solar panel testing guide explains which readings matter on a string.

If the MAX is more meter than your job needs, compare the 87V against its smaller siblings in Fluke 87V vs Fluke 179 and Fluke 117 vs Fluke 87V. If you want logging on top of the 87V's precision, see Fluke 87V vs Fluke 289.

Safety: a waterproof case is not a waterproof measurement

IP67 describes the closed meter, not the job. Once leads are plugged in, the jacks, probe tips and your hands are part of the circuit, and water on any of them can create a leakage path at the voltages both meters are rated for. Don't measure energized equipment while standing in water or with wet gloves. Dry the input jacks and battery door before use after a soaking. Both meters are rated CAT IV 600V / CAT III 1000V, but only with leads of an equal rating; check the cap on your probe tips before metering at a service entrance, and follow your site's lockout and PPE rules.

Choose the Fluke 87V MAX if…

  • ✓You want twice the published battery life and AA cells you can buy anywhere
  • ✓You work in dim spaces and want the keypad, not just the display, to light up

The bottom line

Measurement-wise, the Fluke 87V and 87V MAX are the same meter, with the MAX two counts looser on AC volts. Buy the MAX for the conditions: IP67 sealing, a 4 m drop rating, 800-hour AA battery life and a backlit keypad make sense outdoors, in wet or dusty plants and on night calls. Indoors and on the bench, the standard 87V, our hands-on tested pick, is lighter and gives you the same readings for less.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Fluke 87V MAX more accurate than the 87V?+

No. Fluke lists the same ±(0.05% + 1) DC volts, ±(0.2% + 2) DC amps, ±(1.0% + 2) AC amps and ±(0.2% + 1) resistance for both. The only measuring line that differs is AC volts, where the MAX is ±(0.7% + 4) against the 87V's ±(0.7% + 2), so the MAX is very slightly looser there.

Is the Fluke 87V waterproof?+

No. Fluke lists the standard 87V as IP30, and the zero means it has no rated water protection. It will usually shrug off a splash, but that is not a rating. If your meter gets rained on, used in wash-down areas or might be dropped in water, the IP67 87V MAX is the model built for it.

Does the Fluke 87V MAX have LoZ?+

Fluke doesn't list a low-impedance (LoZ) function for the 87V MAX, and none is listed for the 87V either. If ghost-voltage checks on building wiring are a priority, the Fluke 117 has a LoZ AutoVolt position, and the Fluke 289 also lists LoZ. Otherwise, add a known load across the leads to unmask a phantom reading.

What batteries does the Fluke 87V MAX use?+

Three AA cells, which Fluke rates at about 800 hours of typical use without the backlight. The standard 87V uses a single 9V alkaline battery rated about 400 hours. AA cells are easier to find on a job site, and the doubled life means fewer mid-job battery swaps on a meter that is on all shift.

Can the Fluke 87V MAX be used in freezing weather?+

Fluke lists its operating range as -15 °C to 55 °C, and says it can go to -40 °C for 20 minutes when taken from 20 °C. That suits short trips from a heated vehicle to an outdoor panel. Oddly, the standard 87V lists a lower continuous minimum, -20 °C, but no short-term cold rating.

Do the Fluke 87V and 87V MAX use the same test leads?+

No, they ship with different sets. Fluke lists TL75 test leads in the 87V box and TL175 TwistGuard leads in the 87V MAX box. Check the CAT rating printed on any replacement leads, since a meter is only as safe as its leads. Both include AC175 alligator clips and a K-type temperature probe: the 80BK with the 87V and the 80BK-A with the MAX.

Is the 87V MAX heavier than the 87V?+

Yes. Fluke lists the 87V MAX at 698.5 g with its holster and the 87V at 624 g with holster, so the MAX is about 75 g heavier. Its holstered case is also larger, 215 × 101 × 60 mm against 201 × 98 × 52 mm. Sealing gaskets, a thicker shell and three AA cells account for the extra mass.
