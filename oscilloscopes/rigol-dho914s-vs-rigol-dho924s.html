Rigol DHO914S vs DHO924S: Do You Need 250 MHz?

Rigol DHO914S vs DHO924S comes down to one line of Rigol's DHO900 data sheet: 125 MHz against 250 MHz. Both have four 12-bit channels, 16 digital inputs with the PLA2216 probe, 50 Mpts of memory, the same single-channel 25 MHz signal generator and the same Bode plot. The DHO924S also ships with 350 MHz probes instead of 150 MHz ones. The real question is how fast the edges on your bench are.

Spec by spec

With one difference on paper, the charts show what it does to real edges and where it stops helping.

The estimates use the standard root-sum-of-squares rule for cascaded rise times. On a 2 ns edge the DHO914S overstates rise time by about 70%, the DHO924S by about 20%. On a 5 ns edge the errors are about 15% and 4%, and both scopes tell you the same story. For the theory, see how to measure rise time with cursors.

What each one can and can't measure

Rigol DHO914S: the generator is the point

Most buyers of an S model want the built-in source. Rigol lists a single channel with sine to 25 MHz, square to 15 MHz, ramp to 150 kHz and arbitrary waveforms to 10 MHz at 156 MSa/s and 14 bits, plus a Bode plot from 10 Hz to 25 MHz with up to 300 points per octave. All of that works the same on the DHO914S. Its 125 MHz input is comfortable to roughly 25 to 42 MHz by the three-to-five-times rule, which covers almost everything the generator can produce.

Rigol DHO924S: bandwidth that needs free channels

250 MHz pays off on fast edges, overshoot and ringing. It has a catch: the sample rate is the same as the DHO914S. With one channel on, 1.25 GSa/s gives five samples per 250 MHz cycle. With two on, 625 MSa/s gives 2.5. With three or four on, 312.5 MSa/s falls below twice 250 MHz, so the top of the bandwidth can't be sampled properly. Use the DHO924S as a one- or two-channel fast scope and a four-channel slower one.

Bandwidth that the probe can't pass is wasted

Rigol ships 350 MHz probes with the DHO924S for a reason. If you move 150 MHz probes from an older scope onto it, the probe limits the system and much of the extra bandwidth disappears. Use a probe rated above the scope, and a short ground spring instead of the long clip for fast edges.

Generator output and circuit voltages

Rigol lists a generator output range up to 10 V from a 50 Ω output, and the scope inputs are rated CAT I, 300 Vrms. Don't connect the generator output to a powered node of a circuit, and keep both away from mains; use a differential probe for mains-referenced measurements.

The question with these two: buying them on Amazon

On 2026-11-08 the DHO914S listing showed no buy box at a New York ZIP, only offers under "See All Buying Options", and the DHO924S listing was in stock from a reseller, GuliTech, whose title adds "with AWG". Rigol's data sheet backs that generator claim for the DHO924S. Check who the seller is and how warranty claims go before you order either one; neither is the RIGOL Official Store listing today.

The DHO914S against an 8-bit rival is on Rigol DHO914S vs Siglent SDS1104X-E; the DHO924S against Siglent is on Siglent SDS814X HD vs Rigol DHO924S and Rigol DHO924S vs Siglent SDS1202X-E. If you don't need the generator, see Rigol DHO814 vs DHO914.

Choose the Rigol DHO914S if…

  • ✓Your fastest clocks are under about 25 MHz
  • ✓Most of your work is power, audio, sensors and slow buses
  • ✓You mainly bought for the generator and Bode plot, which are identical
  • ✓You'd rather put the price difference toward the PLA2216 logic probe

Choose the Rigol DHO924S if…

  • ✓You look at edges of 2 ns or faster, such as modern logic or fast SPI
  • ✓You run one or two channels most of the time, where 250 MHz has sample rate behind it
  • ✓You want 350 MHz probes in the box
  • ✓You'd rather not outgrow the scope's bandwidth in a few years

Neither if…

You need full bandwidth on all four channels at once. Both drop to 312.5 MSa/s with three or four channels on, which can't properly sample signals near 250 MHz.

The bottom line

Buy the Rigol DHO914S if you want the scope, logic inputs, 25 MHz generator and Bode plot in one box and your clocks stay under about 25 MHz: every feature except bandwidth is identical. Buy the Rigol DHO924S if you measure edges near 2 ns or fast clocks on one or two channels at a time; its 250 MHz bandwidth, 1.4 ns rise time and 350 MHz probes show those edges far more faithfully.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Rigol DHO924S have a built-in signal generator?+

Yes, per Rigol's DHO900 data sheet, which states that only the DHO914S and DHO924S have the arbitrary function generator and Bode plot. The Amazon listing title also says "with AWG". Rigol's series web page doesn't break this out per model, which is why some product summaries, including our own card, call it unconfirmed.

Is the generator better on the DHO924S?+

No. Rigol lists one generator specification for both S models: one channel at 156 MSa/s and 14 bits, sine to 25 MHz, square to 15 MHz, ramp to 150 kHz and arbitrary waveforms to 10 MHz. The Bode plot also runs to 25 MHz on both. The DHO924S's extra bandwidth applies only to the scope inputs.

Can I upgrade a DHO914S to 250 MHz later?+

Rigol's DHO900 data sheet lists the DHO914S and DHO924S as separate order numbers and doesn't list a bandwidth upgrade option. Assume the bandwidth you buy is the bandwidth you keep, and choose based on the fastest signals you expect in the next few years.

Why does the DHO924S come with different probes?+

Rigol lists 350 MHz passive probes as standard with the DHO924 and DHO924S and 150 MHz probes with the DHO914 and DHO914S. A probe's bandwidth adds to the scope's, so a 150 MHz probe on a 250 MHz scope would throw away much of the extra speed.

What signals actually need 250 MHz?+

Edges faster than about 3 ns, clocks above roughly 40 MHz, and overshoot or ringing on fast logic. Microcontroller GPIO, high-speed SPI and modern logic families can switch that fast. Power supplies, audio, sensors, I2C and UART don't come close, and 125 MHz shows them as well as 250 MHz does.
