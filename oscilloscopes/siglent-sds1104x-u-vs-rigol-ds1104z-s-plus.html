Siglent SDS1104X-U vs Rigol DS1104Z-S Plus: Key Differences

Both are four-channel, 100 MHz scopes with 1 GSa/s single-channel sampling, but they diverge sharply in what else they offer: the SDS1104X-U documents a fast, clear waveform capture rate and standard serial decode, while the DS1104Z-S Plus bundles a genuinely useful two-channel signal generator — at the cost of some conflicting documentation from Rigol itself.

Spec by spec

With bandwidth and channel count tied, this comparison comes down to capture rate and documentation clarity on one side, against a built-in generator on the other.

A built-in dual-channel generator is a meaningful capability the SDS1104X-U simply doesn't have — there's no substitute for it if your work needs to drive two independent test signals. But the SDS1104X-U's faster, clearly documented capture rate and standard serial decode are real advantages of their own, especially for digital-bus debugging.

Why does a capture-rate number matter? After each acquisition a digital scope spends time processing and drawing before it can trigger again, and anything that happens during that gap never reaches the screen. A glitch that appears once every few seconds can sit in a gap many times before it finally lands inside a window. With up to 100,000 waveforms per second the Siglent re-arms more than three times as often as the Rigol's 30,000, so on the same time-base setting the blind gaps are shorter and an intermittent fault tends to show up sooner. The exact share of time each scope sees depends on the time base and the features switched on, so treat the published figures as maxima rather than everyday numbers.

Siglent SDS1104X-U vs Rigol DS1104Z-S Plus: key differences

These two serve overlapping but distinct needs despite matching on bandwidth and channel count.

If you need to drive two test signals: the DS1104Z-S Plus

Testing a circuit with two independent inputs — a differential pair, a mixer, a dual-channel filter — is far easier with two built-in generator channels than with a separate instrument or none at all. The optional logic-analyzer upgrade path is a bonus if your needs grow later.

If you want clean documentation and fast capture: the SDS1104X-U

Standard serial decode and a capture rate more than 3x faster make the SDS1104X-U the stronger choice for digital-bus debugging and intermittent-fault hunting, without the internal documentation conflicts that surround some of the DS1104Z-S Plus's specs.

Check Rigol's current documentation before relying on a spec

Rigol's own datasheet and series page disagree on the DS1104Z-S Plus's standard memory depth, and the series page lists serial decoding as an option rather than standard. If either of these matters to your purchase decision, verify directly against Rigol's current documentation rather than assuming either figure.

The bottom line

The Siglent SDS1104X-U and Rigol DS1104Z-S Plus tie on bandwidth and channel count but diverge sharply elsewhere: the DS1104Z-S Plus's built-in two-channel generator is a real capability the SDS1104X-U lacks, while the SDS1104X-U counters with a far faster documented capture rate, confirmed standard serial decode, and cleaner documentation overall.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Siglent SDS1104X-U have a built-in signal generator?+

No — this site's sourcing lists no built-in generator for the SDS1104X-U. For a built-in generator in this bandwidth/channel class, the Rigol DS1104Z-S Plus (two channels, 25 MHz) is the better fit.

Why does Rigol's documentation conflict on the DS1104Z-S Plus?+

Rigol's own datasheet states 24 Mpts of standard memory, while its series page lists 12 Mpts standard with 24 Mpts as an option. The series page also lists serial decoding as optional rather than standard. Verify current documentation directly if these specifics matter to your purchase.

Which has the faster waveform capture rate?+

The Siglent SDS1104X-U, by a wide margin: up to 100,000 wfm/s in normal mode and 400,000 in sequence mode, against the Rigol DS1104Z-S Plus's documented 30,000 wfm/s.

Can the DS1104Z-S Plus be upgraded with a logic analyzer?+

Yes — Rigol offers the separately sold RPL1116 kit to add 16 logic channels. The SDS1104X-U has no equivalent logic-analyzer upgrade path in this site's sourcing.

What does waveform capture rate mean on an oscilloscope?+

It's how many separate acquisitions the scope can trigger, process and display each second. Between acquisitions there is dead time in which the scope is blind. A higher rate, such as the SDS1104X-U's 100,000 wfm/s against 30,000 on the DS1104Z-S Plus, shortens those blind gaps, which raises the odds of catching a rare glitch or runt pulse.
