How to Solder a JST Connector Without Damaging the Plastic Housing

The metal pins of a JST connector shrug off soldering heat; the small plastic shell holding them in line does not. To solder a JST connector without damaging the plastic housing, you need to know which part of the connector is actually meant to be soldered, hold the pins in their true positions while the plastic is warm, and keep each pin's contact time to a couple of seconds. This guide covers all three, from pitch identification to spotting a pin that has quietly drifted.

PH, XH and SH: know your pitch first

"JST" is used loosely for a whole family of small wire-to-board connectors, and the series matters because pitch decides how close your iron sits to the plastic. PH is 2.0mm pitch and very common on LiPo balance leads and small boards, XH is 2.5mm and shows up on battery packs and 3D printer electronics, and SH is a tiny 1.0mm surface-mount part. Measure the center-to-center distance across several pins with calipers to confirm which one you have.

Crimp or solder?

The cable side of a JST connector is a crimp system: contacts are crimped onto the wire and clicked into the housing, with no solder involved. Soldering is the right method for the board side, the through-hole or SMD header that lives on the PCB. If you are tempted to solder a wire onto a crimp contact because you lack the crimper, pre-crimped JST leads are cheap and give a more reliable result than a soldered terminal that will not latch.

1Set the temperature and pick a tip

Counter-intuitively, a slightly hotter iron is kinder to the housing than a cool one, because the joint wets faster and the plastic sees heat for less time. Around 330-350°C for leaded solder and 350-370°C for lead-free are sensible starting points. Use a small chisel or bevel tip that touches the pad and pin together; a needle-sharp conical tip transfers heat slowly and makes you linger. Our tip types guide explains why contact area matters so much.

2Insert the mating plug to hold the pins true

Before the header goes into the board, push a mating cable housing (with or without wires) fully onto it. While the plastic around a pin is soft, the plug keeps every pin at its correct spacing and angle, so nothing can lean or sink. It also adds a little thermal mass to the top of the pins. Leave it in place until the last joint has cooled.

3Tin the pads, then place the header

For SMD headers like SH, put a thin layer of solder on one mounting tab pad and a dab of flux on the signal pads before placing the part, then reflow that tab to anchor the body. For through-hole PH or XH headers, the pads should be clean and fluxed; check the header sits flat against the board and tape it down if gravity will pull it away when you flip the board over.

4Solder one pin at a time, and skip around

Touch the tip to pad and pin together, feed solder into the joint on the far side, and lift away as soon as a concave fillet forms, ideally inside two seconds. Then move to a pin at the other end rather than the neighbor you just heated, so the plastic between adjacent pins never receives two heat doses back to back. Do outer pins first, then fill in the middle.

Dwell time vs housing damage

Housing damage is driven far more by how long heat sits on a pin than by the exact setpoint. The scale below is a rule of thumb for small nylon-bodied through-hole headers, not a measured limit; heat-resistant SMD housings tolerate more, thin-walled ones less.

Signs of a melted or shifted housing

What you seeLikely causeWhat to do
Pin sits lower than its neighborsPin pushed back while plastic was softReheat briefly with the mating plug fitted to re-seat it
Pins lean or splay at the topNo jig, or the tip levered the pinFit a plug before reheating; replace if plastic is cracked
Plug needs force or won't latchHousing walls distortedReplace the header; it will loosen in service
Glossy, rippled or brown plasticLong dwell or tip contact on the shellCosmetic if fit is fine; otherwise replace
Dull, grainy fillet on a straight pinLifted too early to protect the plasticAdd flux and reheat for one short touch

Safety: fumes, burns and lead

Work with a fan or extractor pulling flux smoke away from your face, keep the iron in its stand between joints, and wash your hands after handling leaded solder. Small connectors invite holding parts with fingers near the tip; use tweezers or tape instead.

Irons that make short dwell times easier

Protecting a JST housing comes down to getting heat into the pin quickly and then leaving. A temperature-controlled iron that recovers fast after touching the joint lets you work at a moderate setting with a short touch, instead of cranking the dial up and hoping. A fine conical or small chisel tip that matches the pitch keeps the tip away from the plastic walls. For tip shapes, see our soldering iron tip types guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can I solder wires straight onto the crimp terminals of a JST plug?

It is possible but rarely a good idea. Crimp terminals are shaped to grip the conductor and insulation mechanically, and solder wicking up the barrel makes the wire stiff right where it flexes, so it tends to crack there later. Solder also stops the terminal seating and latching properly in the housing. Use the correct crimp tool, or buy pre-crimped leads in the length you need.

Why did the pins of my JST header lean after soldering?

The plastic around each pin softened while it was hot, and something pushed on the pin before the plastic cooled: the iron tip, the weight of the board, or a neighboring pin being reheated. Plugging in a mating connector before soldering holds every pin at the correct spacing and angle until the housing has set again.

What temperature should I use for a JST PH header?

A common starting point is roughly 330-350°C for leaded solder and 350-370°C for lead-free, with a small chisel or bevel tip. The aim is a setpoint hot enough to wet each pin in about two seconds. If you need much longer than that, the tip is too small or dirty rather than the setting too low.

Is it safe to reflow a JST SH header with a soldering iron?

Yes, with a fine tip, plenty of flux and very short touches, but SH parts are 1.0mm pitch surface-mount headers, so treat them like fine-pitch SMD work. Tack one mounting tab first to hold the body, then do the signal pins, and inspect for bridges with magnification.

My housing has a small dark mark but the pins look straight. Is it ruined?

Probably not. Light surface marking from a brief tip touch is cosmetic. Check that a mating plug still inserts with normal force and latches, and that no pin has pushed back into the housing. If both are fine, the connector is usable.
