HANMATEK DOS1102 vs OWON SDS1102

HANMATEK DOS1102 vs OWON SDS1102 is the closest pairing in our oscilloscope list. Both makers publish the same 301 × 152 × 70 mm body, about 1.1 kg, a 7-inch 800 × 480 screen, a 10K record length, edge and video triggers and 30 automatic measurements. So the choice rests on a handful of differences: the HANMATEK runs from 5 V and adds a frequency counter and current scaling, while the OWON zooms further into small signals at 5 mV/div and plugs into mains. Here is which difference matters for which bench.

Identical published dimensions, weight, screen, memory and trigger set are unusual for two brands. We can't confirm from maker sources whether they share hardware, so we treat each spec sheet on its own and compare only what each maker or listing states.

HANMATEK DOS1102 vs OWON SDS1102 on small signals

The spec that changes daily use most is the bottom of the vertical range. Take 20 mV of ripple on a 5 V rail, a common size for a linear regulator or a well-filtered switcher. On the SDS1102 at 5 mV/div, AC coupled, that ripple fills four of the eight divisions, so its shape and spikes are easy to read. On the DOS1102 at 20 mV/div it fills one division, and the switching spikes riding on it shrink to a small fraction of a division.

There is no easy workaround on the DOS1102: a x1 probe is already its most sensitive setting, so no probe change brings the trace up. For audio preamps, sensor outputs in the millivolt range and ripple checks, the OWON's range is the practical win. For logic levels, PWM and anything above about 100 mV, both read the same. If ripple is your main job, our guide to measuring ripple voltage with an oscilloscope covers probe technique that matters as much as the scope.

Where the DOS1102 pulls back ahead

The HANMATEK listing adds a 6-digit hardware frequency counter from 2 Hz to 20 MHz, which beats a period measurement for checking a crystal or an inverter's switching frequency. Its current scaling from 100.0 mA/V to 1 kA/V turns a clamp probe's voltage straight into amps on screen. Its timebase reaches 2 ns/div, two and a half times faster than the OWON's 5 ns/div, for looking at a single edge. And it runs from 5 V, so a single adapter powers it and there is no mains cord on the bench.

10K points is the shared limit

At 500 MS/s, 10K points hold 20 µs; at 1 GSa/s, 10 µs. Show a 10 ms window and either scope must slow to about 1 MSa/s. That is fine for repetitive signals and painful for one-off events. If you need long captures, neither is the right buy: see HANMATEK DOS1102 vs Hantek DSO2C10, where 8 Mpts changes that.

What both can and can't do

With the three-to-five-times rule, both show signals up to about 20–35 MHz at close to true size. Edge and video triggers cover steady waveforms and TV-style sync, but neither offers pulse-width, runt or serial triggers, so catching one odd pulse in a stream is hard. Neither has a generator; HANMATEK sells the DOS1102S with a 10 MHz source, which we haven't checked as a pick. Neither decodes I2C, UART or SPI.

Mains-powered vs 5 V: the ground clip is still earthed

The OWON's mains cord ties every probe ground clip to earth. The HANMATEK on its adapter may float, but the moment it connects to a grounded PC over USB, its clips are earthed too. Clipping a ground lead onto a live mains conductor shorts it through the scope. HANMATEK's 400 V is an input limit, not a CAT rating, and neither listing gives one. Use a differential probe on mains circuits, and call an electrician for building wiring.

The trust question: photos and copied specs

Each listing has a flaw worth knowing. The SDS1102's Amazon photos mostly show an SDS1022, a different OWON model, and its item weight (2.2 lb) differs slightly from OWON's 1.1 kg. The DOS1102's maker FAQ carries figures from another model (250 MSa/s, 12,000 points, 3.5-inch), though its manual is consistent with the Amazon listing. Read the model number on the unit when it arrives, and check the box against the listing.

  • ✓You want a stated two-channel rate: 500 MS/s on each channel
  • ✓You want a 6-digit hardware frequency counter and clamp-probe current scaling
  • ✓You zoom into fast edges: its timebase reaches 2 ns/div

Choose the OWON SDS1102 if…

  • ✓You look at small signals such as supply ripple: its range starts at 5 mV/div
  • ✓You want a GND coupling position to find the zero line quickly
  • ✓You prefer a long-running model; Amazon first listed it in December 2017
  • ✓A mains-powered scope suits your bench

Neither if…

You need to capture long one-off events, decode serial buses or generate a test signal. Both have 10K points, edge and video triggers only, no decoding and no generator.

The bottom line

On paper these are near twins: same published size, weight, screen, 10K memory, triggers and 30 measurements. Buy the OWON SDS1102 if you look at small signals, because 5 mV/div shows ripple four times larger. Buy the HANMATEK DOS1102 if you want 5 V power, a 6-digit frequency counter, clamp-probe current scaling and a clearly stated 500 MS/s per channel.

Frequently asked questions

Are the HANMATEK DOS1102 and OWON SDS1102 the same scope?+

Their published dimensions (301 × 152 × 70 mm), weight (about 1.1 kg), screen, 10K record length, triggers and measurement count match. But their listed power, vertical range, timebase and extras differ, and neither maker says they share hardware. Compare them as two products and buy on the specs that differ.

Which is better for measuring power supply ripple?+

The OWON SDS1102. Its Amazon listing gives a most sensitive range of 5 mV/div against 20 mV/div on the DOS1102, so a 20 mV ripple fills four divisions instead of one. Use AC coupling and a short ground spring on the probe on either scope.

Does the OWON SDS1102 run on 5 V like the DOS1102?+

No. The SDS1102 Amazon listing specifies a 2 A, 250 V fuse, consistent with a mains-powered scope, and our other OWON pages treat it as mains powered. The HANMATEK DOS1102 runs from a DC 5 V, 2 A supply, per its manual.

Can either scope decode I2C or UART?+

No. HANMATEK's product FAQ says the DOS1102 has no serial decoding, and OWON's specs for the SDS1102 list none. Both trigger on edges and video only. For decoding at a similar budget, the Hantek DSO2C10 decodes UART, I2C, SPI, CAN and LIN.

Why do the SDS1102 photos on Amazon show a different model?+

The listing's main and most gallery images show an SDS1022, a lower-bandwidth OWON from the same family. We use a gallery photo with a blank model badge. The specs we quote come from OWON's own SDS1000 pages and the listing text, not the photos. Check the model badge on delivery.
