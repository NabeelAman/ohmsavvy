FNIRSI 1013D vs DPOS350P: Which Tablet Scope?

FNIRSI 1013D vs DPOS350P is a choice between two FNIRSI tablets that look alike on the bench: both have a 7-inch capacitive touchscreen, two channels, 1 GSa/s and a built-in battery. The 1013D Plus kit is the plain scope at 100 MHz, with a 6000 mAh battery FNIRSI rates for about 4 hours and a 100X probe for high voltage. The DPOS350P raises the bandwidth to 350 MHz and adds a 50 MHz generator, a spectrum analyzer and a frequency response analyzer, at the cost of a shorter 3-hour run time and a 12 V fast charger.

What 350 MHz on 1 GSa/s really gets you

Both tablets sample at 1 GSa/s, one point per nanosecond. On the 1013D that's 10 points per cycle at its 100 MHz limit, a comfortable margin. On the DPOS350P a 350 MHz sine gets 1,000 ÷ 350 ≈ 2.9 points per cycle. That clears the Nyquist limit of 2, so the information is there, but the scope has to rebuild the shape with sin(x)/x interpolation to draw a smooth sine. FNIRSI's listing doesn't say whether it does.

In practice the DPOS350P's extra bandwidth pays off most on edges, not on 300 MHz sines. Its listed 1 ns rise time against the 1013D's "under 3 ns" means a fast logic edge looks steep instead of rounded, and ringing after the edge shows up. FNIRSI doesn't say what either scope samples per channel with both channels on. If the DPOS350P splits its 1 GSa/s between two channels, the margin at 350 MHz gets thinner still.

FNIRSI 1013D vs DPOS350P away from the bench

Both are tablets, so the field matters. The battery numbers check out against each other. The DPOS350P's pack is 3.7 V × 8 Ah = 29.6 Wh, and at its listed 10 W that's 29.6 ÷ 10 ≈ 3 hours, which is what FNIRSI claims. FNIRSI doesn't give the 1013D's cell voltage or power draw, only 6000 mAh and about 4 hours.

Charging is where they part ways. The 1013D takes 5 V over USB-C at 2 A, 3 A or 4 A and keeps running while it charges, so a car USB-C port or a power bank can top it up. The DPOS350P specifies Quick Charge 18 W at 12 V/1.5 A and ships with that charger; FNIRSI doesn't say whether it runs while charging. The listing also contradicts itself on charge time: about 5 hours in the spec table, "just 3 hours" in a feature panel.

For a mechanic, the 1013D Plus kit is the better field tool. Its P4100 100X probe is rated by FNIRSI to measure up to 4000 V, ten times the 400 V FNIRSI gives for a 10X probe, and 100 MHz is far more than car sensor and injector signals need. The DPOS350P's two 350 MHz probes suit fast electronics, not high voltage, and FNIRSI lists 400 V overvoltage protection on its inputs.

Four instruments, one battery

The DPOS350P's generator, spectrum analyzer and response analyzer are the real reason to pay more. The response analyzer sweeps 100 Hz to 50 MHz at up to 500 points and plots gain and phase, the job you'd otherwise do point by point with a generator and a scope. If you'll never plot a Bode chart or look at a spectrum, the 1013D gives you the same tablet format with a longer run time.

The gaps in FNIRSI's figures

Neither tablet is fully documented, and the gaps are different. For the 1013D, FNIRSI doesn't give the ADC resolution, the sample rate with both channels on, or what "240 Kbit" means in sample points. Its vertical range is printed as "50mV-500V" with no "per division", so read it as an input range, not a sensitivity setting. For the DPOS350P, FNIRSI doesn't state memory depth or trigger types, and its own product page shows its specs only as images, so the figures here come from the official Amazon listing. The DPOS350P has been listed since April 2025, against October 2022 for the 1013D Plus.

Battery tablets and mains voltage

Running on battery with the charger unplugged, neither tablet is tied to earth, so a probe ground doesn't short through the mains wiring the way a bench scope's does. That doesn't make the case safe to touch while you probe a live circuit, and FNIRSI gives neither tablet a CAT rating. Use the 100X probe or a differential probe for mains-level work, unplug the charger first, and leave building wiring to an electrician.

Choose the FNIRSI 1013D Plus if…

  • ✓Your signals stay under about 20–30 MHz: car sensors, PWM, audio, power supplies
  • ✓You want the longer run time: about 4 hours from 6000 mAh, and it works while charging
  • ✓You measure high voltage and want the 100X P4100 probe included
  • ✓You'd rather charge from an ordinary 5 V USB-C supply

Choose the FNIRSI DPOS350P if…

  • ✓You need bandwidth above 100 MHz, or faster edges: FNIRSI lists a 1 ns rise time
  • ✓You want a 50 MHz generator in the same tablet, with 0–5 Vpp and ±2.5 V offset
  • ✓You'll plot filter or amplifier response with the 100 Hz–50 MHz frequency response analyzer
  • ✓You chase intermittent glitches: FNIRSI lists 50,000 waveforms per second

Neither if…

You need serial protocol decoding or more than two channels. Neither tablet lists decoding; a four-channel bench scope such as the Rigol DHO804 covers both.

The bottom line

Buy the FNIRSI 1013D Plus for field work on signals below a few tens of MHz: car diagnostics, power supplies, PWM. It runs longer, charges from 5 V USB-C while you use it, and the kit's 100X probe handles high voltage. Buy the FNIRSI DPOS350P if you'll use the generator, spectrum analyzer and response analyzer, or need 350 MHz and a 1 ns rise time, and you're fine with 3 hours of battery and its own 12 V charger.

Frequently asked questions

Is the FNIRSI 1013D Plus different from the 1013D?+

Only in the kit. FNIRSI sells the 1013D in two styles, "1013D Std." and "1013D Plus", and the Plus adds a P4100 100X high-voltage probe. The tablet itself is the same: 100 MHz, two channels, 1 GSa/s, a 7-inch touchscreen and a 6000 mAh battery.

Is 1 GSa/s enough for a 350 MHz scope?+

It's tight. 1 GSa/s gives about 2.9 samples per cycle at 350 MHz, above the Nyquist minimum of 2 but low enough that the scope must interpolate to draw a smooth sine. FNIRSI doesn't state the DPOS350P's two-channel sample rate or interpolation method. Most users will see the gain in sharper edges rather than in 300 MHz sines.

Which FNIRSI tablet has the longer battery life?+

The 1013D: FNIRSI rates it about 4 hours from 6000 mAh, and it can run while charging over 5 V USB-C. The DPOS350P is rated about 3 hours from a 3.7 V 8000 mAh pack, since it draws 10 W and has a cooling fan. It charges with an 18 W Quick Charge adapter at 12 V, included in the box.

Does the FNIRSI 1013D have a signal generator?+

No. The 1013D is a scope only. If you want a generator in a FNIRSI tablet, the DPOS350P has one with sine to 50 MHz, square to 10 MHz, 0–5 Vpp and ±2.5 V offset. FNIRSI's benchtop 1014D also has one, at 2.5 Vpp.

Which is better for car diagnostics?+

The 1013D Plus. Car sensor, injector and CAN signals sit well under its 100 MHz, the Plus kit includes a P4100 100X probe that FNIRSI rates for up to 4000 V, and the 4-hour battery charges from a 5 V USB-C source. The DPOS350P's extra bandwidth and analyzers add little under the hood.

How many captures can each tablet save?+

FNIRSI gives the 1013D 1 GB of storage for up to 1,000 screenshots and 1,000 waveforms. The DPOS350P's listing says it stores 500 waveforms and 90 screenshots. Both export over USB to a computer, and the DPOS350P also takes firmware updates as .iso files.
