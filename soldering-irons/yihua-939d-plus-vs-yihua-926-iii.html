YIHUA 939D+ vs 926 III

YIHUA 939D+ vs 926 comes down to what you want on the bench. The 939D+ is a transformer-based station, ESD safe, with a metal front panel, a detachable iron and a separate metal stand, covering 200 to 480 °C. The 926 III is a compact station with the holder built in, a wider 90 to 480 °C range and an adjustable sleep timer, sold as a kit with helping hands, six tips and solder. Both use PID control and YIHUA's 900M tips, and both listings give power figures that conflict with other YIHUA sources.

What you get for your bench

Lead with the box contents, because they decide this for many buyers. The 926 III arrives as a 12-in-1 kit: the station, two helping hands, six tips, a 35 g roll of lead-free solder, a solder sucker, ESD-safe tweezers, a solder dispenser and a sponge. The 939D+ arrives as a station, iron and stand, with tips sold separately in YIHUA's store. If you have nothing yet, the 926 III gets you soldering the day it arrives.

On the bench, the layouts differ. The 939D+ control box is 5.4 × 4.4 in, and the iron rests in a separate metal stand you can put wherever suits your hand. The 926 III is deeper at 7.3 in, but its holder is built in, with tip slots in the rear guard. Weight goes strongly to the 939D+: 2.16 kg against 1.8 lb, about 0.82 kg. A heavy base doesn't slide when you pull the iron cable, which matters more than it sounds on a cramped desk.

Temperature range and sleep

Both listings top out at 480 °C (896 °F). At the low end, the 926 III goes down to 90 °C, the 939D+ to 200 °C. Few solder jobs run below 200 °C, since common tin-lead solder melts at 183 °C and lead-free alloys higher, but a low setting is useful for warming heat-shrink, softening adhesive or keeping the iron idling cool between joints.

Sleep is the clearer advantage. The 926 III drops into sleep after 5, 10 or 30 minutes idle, which YIHUA's listing says reduces wear on the tip and heating element. The 939D+ listing describes automatic detection of the handle's status but doesn't state a sleep mode for the US model. YIHUA's site lists a 939D+-III variant with a 5 to 30 minute sleep, but the Amazon unit doesn't claim to be that variant, so we don't assume it. If you leave an iron on for hours, sleep is worth having; our page on soldering iron tip care covers why idle heat oxidizes tips.

A 480 °C tip and a light station

Both irons reach 480 °C. Park the iron in its holder every time, keep the cord clear of the tip, and unplug the station when you leave the bench. The 926 III's light body is easier to drag by the cable, so place it where the cable can't catch. If a station smells of burning plastic or the cable is damaged, stop using it.

ESD safety and the transformer

The 939D+ listing title says "ESD Safe", and its Pick photo shows "ESD SAFE" printed on the station. The 926 III listing makes no ESD claim for the station; only the tweezers in its kit are described as ESD safe. For through-hole parts, wires and connectors that rarely matters. For bare MOSFETs, CMOS chips and RF parts, an ESD-safe station with a grounded tip is the safer choice.

ESD safe means a grounded path, not a feature switch

An ESD-safe station is built to keep the tip at ground potential, so the tip doesn't carry a static charge or leakage voltage into a sensitive pin. That only works if the outlet's ground is good. Use a properly grounded three-prong outlet, and add a wrist strap and mat if you handle static-sensitive boards often.

Both stations use PID control, which adjusts heating power to hold the set temperature. The 939D+ adds error detection and a detachable handle. The 926 III adds a UL standard conformity statement in its listing. Both take YIHUA's 900M-series tips, so a tip collection moves between them. See soldering iron tip types for which shapes to buy.

The trust question: power figures

Neither listing gives a power figure you can take at face value. The 939D+ title says "75W equivalent", its spec table says 120 W, and a related 939D+-III variant on YIHUA's site says 32 W with 120 W maximum. The 926 III listing says 60 W, while YIHUA's own 926LED-III page says 130 W maximum and 18 W rated. A rated figure usually describes steady-state draw and a maximum the heat-up surge, so the numbers may not contradict each other as much as they look, but YIHUA doesn't explain them.

What that means for you: don't buy either one on wattage. Neither states a heat-up time. If you solder heavy wire, large ground planes or thick connectors often, a station with clearly documented power and recovery, such as the Hakko FX-888D, is the better-documented choice. The bottom line

The YIHUA 926 III is the better starter buy: a compact PID station with a 90 to 480 °C range, an adjustable sleep timer and a kit that includes helping hands, six tips and solder. The YIHUA 939D+ is the better choice for static-sensitive repair work, with an ESD-safe transformer station, a metal front panel and a separate stand. Treat both listings' power figures with caution.

Frequently asked questions

Is the YIHUA 939D+ better than the 926?+

For static-sensitive work, yes: its listing calls the station ESD safe, it is transformer-based and its iron sits in a separate metal stand. The 926 III is better value for a beginner, because it adds an adjustable sleep timer, a lower 90 °C minimum and a full kit with helping hands, six tips and solder.

Do the YIHUA 939D+ and 926 III use the same tips?+

Both listings name YIHUA's 900M-series tips, and the 926 III kit describes its six tips as #1200/900M series. In practice that means a stock of 900M tips works with either station. Check each tip's description before buying, as YIHUA sells several tip families.

Does the YIHUA 939D+ have a sleep mode?+

The US Amazon listing doesn't state one. It mentions automatic detection of the handle's status, temperature calibration and error detection. YIHUA's website lists a 939D+-III variant with a 5 to 30 minute sleep, but the Amazon unit doesn't claim to be that version. The 926 III has an adjustable 5, 10 or 30 minute sleep.

How many watts is the YIHUA 926 III?+

The Amazon listing says 60 W. YIHUA's own 926LED-III page gives 130 W maximum and 18 W rated. The figures may describe peak and steady draw, but YIHUA doesn't explain them, so treat the station as a light-duty hobby unit rather than one for heavy wire.

Is the YIHUA 926 III ESD safe?+

The listing doesn't say the station is ESD safe. It describes the kit's tweezers as ESD safe and says the station conforms to the UL standard. If you work on static-sensitive chips, the 939D+, whose listing calls the station ESD safe, is the better match.

Which YIHUA station takes less space?+

The 939D+ control box is smaller at 5.4 by 4.4 inches, but it needs a separate iron stand beside it. The 926 III is 7.3 by 4.5 inches with the holder built in, so its total footprint can end up the smaller of the two. It is also far lighter at 1.8 lb against 2.16 kg.
