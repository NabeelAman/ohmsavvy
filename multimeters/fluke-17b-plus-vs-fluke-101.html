Fluke 17B+ vs Fluke 101

Fluke 17B+ vs Fluke 101 is less a contest than a question of scope. The 101 has only two input jacks and cannot measure current at all, so it is a voltage, resistance and frequency checker; the 17B+ is a full meter that adds current from microamps to 10A, temperature and MIN MAX. Fluke calls neither True RMS.

  • →The 17B+ adds temperature, MIN MAX, relative mode and a 30V hazardous-voltage LED.
  • →Both read frequency to 100 kHz and duty cycle; the 101 does it in a body roughly a third of the 17B+'s weight.

What the 101 leaves out

Fluke built the 101 as a minimal tester. Its two-jack layout is the giveaway: there is nowhere to plug a lead for current. These are the 17B+ functions it doesn't have.

Current

No amps, milliamps or microamps on the 101.

Temperature

The 17B+ includes a Type K probe.

MIN MAX

The 17B+ records highs and lows.

Relative

The 17B+ can zero a reference reading.

1000V range

The 101 stops at 600V.

Warning LED

The 17B+ lights at 30V or more.

Fluke 17B+ vs 101 spec by spec

For the functions both meters have, Fluke publishes the same accuracy: ±(0.5% + 3) on DC volts and the same three resistance bands, from ±(0.5% + 3) on the 400 Ω range to ±(1.5% + 3) on 40 MΩ. The 101's 6,000-count display moves its range breaks to 6 V and 60 V, so on a 5 V rail it shows one more digit.

Why two jacks means no current

A meter reads voltage in parallel with a circuit but has to sit in series to read current: you open the circuit and let the current flow through the meter's fused shunt. The 17B+ has dedicated current jacks fused at 440 mA and 11 A for that. The 101 has only COM and a combined volts and ohms input, so there is no path that could carry load current safely.

That design is also a safety feature: you can't accidentally short an outlet by leaving a lead in an amps jack. If you like that idea but need occasional current, a clamp meter covers it without breaking the circuit; see measuring current without breaking the circuit.

The import question

The 17B+ is a current Fluke US product with a one-year warranty. The 101 is sold by Fluke in markets such as India and Malaysia, and the Amazon US listing comes from a third-party importer, with a one-year warranty. Its own Fluke page also contradicts itself on capacitance: the range table runs to 1,000 µF while the general specs say 100 µF. Treat 100 µF as the safe ceiling until Fluke clears it up.

Who the 101 is really for

Someone who wants a Fluke-built voltage and continuity checker that weighs less than a phone and can't be misused on an amps jack. If you might ever measure current or temperature, the 17B+ is the better first meter, and our best multimeter for beginners guide covers other options.

Safety on household circuits

Both meters are rated CAT III 600V, suitable for outlets, fixtures and branch circuits but not the service entrance. Neither is True RMS, so readings on dimmers and variable-speed drives can be off. Use the live-dead-live habit: confirm the meter on a known live outlet before and after you check that a circuit is dead. For anything inside the panel, call a licensed electrician.

The bottom line

For almost everyone the 17B+ is the right buy: it does everything the 101 does except fit in the smallest pocket, and adds current, temperature and logging. The 101 only makes sense as a deliberately simple voltage and continuity tester, and its US listing is an import with a one-year warranty.

Frequently asked questions

Can the Fluke 101 measure amps?+

No. The 101 has only a COM jack and one shared input, and Fluke's datasheet lists no current function. To read amps a meter must sit in series through a fused current jack. Use the 17B+, which measures from 400 µA to 10A AC and DC on inputs fused at 440 mA and 11 A.

Is the Fluke 101 sold in the US?+

Not by Fluke US: its product page isn't on fluke.com US, and the model is listed on Fluke's India and Malaysia sites. The Amazon listing is fulfilled by Amazon but sold by a third-party importer, so warranty support may run through the seller. The 17B+ is a regular Fluke US product.

What capacitance can the Fluke 101 measure?+

Fluke's own documents disagree: the datasheet gives a 100 µF maximum, while the users manual and the range table on Fluke's 101 page go to 1,000 µF. Plan around 100 µF to be safe. The 17B+ is consistently listed at 1,000 µF, with ±(5% + 5) accuracy on the microfarad ranges.

Do both meters read frequency and duty cycle?+

Yes. Both read frequency from 10 Hz to 100 kHz. Fluke lists duty cycle from 1% to 99% on the 17B+ and from 0.1% to 99.9% on its 101 page. For checking a PWM fan, a dimmer or a small generator's output, the 101 keeps up with the 17B+.

Do the 17B+ and 101 have the same warranty?+

Yes, on paper: the 15B+/17B+/18B+ users manual gives one year, and the 101's manual, datasheet and Fluke India page also give one year. The difference is support. Because the 101 reaches the US through an importer, check the Amazon seller's return and warranty terms before ordering, since Fluke US doesn't list the model.
