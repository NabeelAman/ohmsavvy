Rigol DS1102Z-E vs DS1202Z-E

Rigol DS1102Z-E vs DS1202Z-E is a pure bandwidth decision. Rigol covers both models in one DS1000Z-E datasheet, and every row is shared except three: bandwidth (100 MHz against 200 MHz), calculated rise time (3.5 ns against 1.75 ns) and the standard probes (150 MHz PVP3150s against 350 MHz PVP2350s). Memory, sample rate, decoding, triggers, screen, size and the three-year warranty are identical. The probes turn out to matter almost as much as the scope, so this page works through the whole signal chain.

Spec by spec

Only three datasheet rows differ. The charts show them, plus the samples-per-cycle figure that limits how much of the DS1202Z-E's extra bandwidth you get with both channels running.

The probe is part of the bandwidth

A scope's rated bandwidth is measured at the BNC. Your signal also passes through a probe, and the two rise times combine. The standard approximation: rise time ≈ 0.35 ÷ bandwidth for each part, then system rise time ≈ √(probe² + scope²). Running Rigol's numbers through it gives a result most buyers never see on a listing.

Worked example for the first row: 0.35 ÷ 150 MHz = 2.33 ns for the probe; √(2.33² + 3.5²) = √17.7 = 4.2 ns; 0.35 ÷ 4.2 ns ≈ 83 MHz. The real figures depend on the ground lead and probe tip, and a long ground clip can cut them further. Treat these as a guide to scale, not measurements.

What the extra headroom shows

With the five-times rule for square waves, the DS1102Z-E keeps the 5th harmonic of clocks to about 17 MHz with its bundled probes (83 ÷ 5), and the DS1202Z-E to about 35 MHz (173 ÷ 5). On edges, the DS1202Z-E's 2 ns system rise time reads a 10 ns edge as about 10.2 ns; the DS1102Z-E's 4.2 ns reads it as about 10.8 ns. Both calculated the same way.

What each one can and can't measure

Rigol DS1102Z-E: 100 MHz is enough for most benches

With its bundled probes, the DS1102Z-E covers clocks to roughly 17 MHz cleanly and edges down to about 13 to 21 ns with good accuracy (three to five times its 4.2 ns system rise). That spans most hobby, repair and student work. Its 24 Mpts memory, decoders and 500 µV/div range are exactly those of its sibling.

Rigol DS1202Z-E: headroom with a sampling caveat

The DS1202Z-E doubles the clock ceiling and halves the edge floor. The caveat is sampling: with both channels on, 500 MSa/s gives 2.5 samples per cycle at 200 MHz, barely above the Nyquist limit of 2. Repetitive signals still reconstruct with sin(x)/x interpolation, but for single-shot work near the top of its band, turn the second channel off and get 1 GSa/s back.

Rated inputs, not isolation

Rigol rates both models CAT I 300 Vrms and CAT II 100 Vrms at the input, and both ground their probe returns to mains earth. Don't probe mains-side circuits with a standard probe and ground clip; use a differential probe, and leave household wiring to an electrician.

The trust question: what ships in the box

The DS1202Z-E's value depends partly on its 350 MHz probes. Rigol's datasheet lists them as standard, but the Amazon listing in our Pick is sold through a reseller, and listings sometimes bundle different accessories. Check the listing's "included components" before buying. If it ships with 150 MHz probes, the calculated system bandwidth falls to about 120 MHz, which still beats the DS1102Z-E but by less.

The DS1202Z-E's other matchups are Rigol DS1202Z-E vs Siglent SDS1202X-E, Rigol DS1054Z vs DS1202Z-E and Rigol DS1202Z-E vs DHO802. For the DS1102Z-E, see Rigol DS1102Z-E vs DS1054Z and Rigol DS1102Z-E vs Hantek DSO2D15. Our probe compensation guide helps you get the bandwidth you paid for.

Choose the Rigol DS1102Z-E if…

  • ✓Your clocks stay under about 15 to 20 MHz and your edges are slower than 10 ns
  • ✓You work mostly with audio, power, PWM, UART, I2C and SPI
  • ✓You want the same memory, decode and warranty for less money
  • ✓You plan to probe at 1X often, where probe bandwidth limits both scopes

Choose the Rigol DS1202Z-E if…

  • ✓You look at clocks from 20 to 40 MHz and want them drawn as squares
  • ✓You measure edges in the 3 to 10 ns range or check ringing on fast lines
  • ✓You want Rigol's 350 MHz PVP2350 probes, standard with this model per the datasheet
  • ✓You'd rather have headroom now than replace a 100 MHz scope later

Neither if…

You need more than two inputs or finer vertical detail. Both are two-channel, 8-bit scopes; a four-channel Rigol DS1054Z or a 12-bit Rigol DHO802 suits those jobs better.

The bottom line

Buy the Rigol DS1102Z-E unless you know you need more than about 17 MHz of clean clock or edges faster than about 13 ns: it shares every other spec with its sibling. Buy the Rigol DS1202Z-E for fast digital work, where its 200 MHz and Rigol's 350 MHz standard probes roughly double the usable system bandwidth, as long as the listing includes those probes.

Frequently asked questions

Is the DS1202Z-E the same scope as the DS1102Z-E?+

Rigol describes both in one DS1000Z-E datasheet, and only bandwidth, calculated rise time and standard probes differ. Sample rate, 24 Mpts memory, 30,000 wfms/s, triggers, decoding, screen, size, weight and the three-year warranty are listed identically. The DS1202Z-E is rated 200 MHz, the DS1102Z-E 100 MHz.

Do I need the 350 MHz probes with the DS1202Z-E?+

To get most of its bandwidth, yes. By the standard rise-time calculation, the DS1202Z-E reaches about 173 MHz with 350 MHz probes but only about 120 MHz with 150 MHz probes. Rigol lists PVP2350 350 MHz probes as standard for the DS1202Z-E; confirm the Amazon listing includes them.

Is 200 MHz too much for 1 GSa/s?+

On one channel, 1 GSa/s gives 5 samples per cycle at 200 MHz, a workable ratio with sin(x)/x interpolation. With both channels on, each gets 500 MSa/s, or 2.5 samples per cycle at 200 MHz, which is tight for single-shot captures. Repeating signals are fine either way.

Which one should a student buy?+

The DS1102Z-E for most students. Lab signals in electronics courses, from RC circuits to microcontroller buses, sit well inside its roughly 83 MHz system bandwidth with the bundled probes. It has the same memory, decoders, triggers and warranty as the DS1202Z-E, so nothing else is lost.

Can I use the DS1102Z-E's probes on the DS1202Z-E?+

Yes, they share the same BNC inputs and Rigol lists its passive probes as compatible across its scopes. The trade-off is bandwidth: the calculated system figure with 150 MHz PVP3150 probes is about 120 MHz on the DS1202Z-E, against about 173 MHz with the 350 MHz PVP2350 probes.
