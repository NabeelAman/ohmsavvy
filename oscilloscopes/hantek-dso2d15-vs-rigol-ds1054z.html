Hantek DSO2D15 vs Rigol DS1054Z

Hantek DSO2D15 vs Rigol DS1054Z is a trade between speed and inputs. The DSO2D15 is a two-channel, 150 MHz scope with a 2.4 ns rise time, a 25 MHz generator and CAN and LIN decoding. The DS1054Z has four channels at 50 MHz, a calculated 7 ns rise time and up to 24 Mpts of memory, and it is one of the scopes our owner has tested hands-on. This page works out how much each scope's own rise time distorts the edges you measure, then sorts the jobs where four inputs matter more.

Spec by spec

The Hantek wins the speed rows and the Rigol wins the capacity rows. Neither scope leads across the board, which is why the right pick depends on the circuit you're probing.

How much each scope slows the edges you measure

Every scope has its own rise time, and it adds to the signal's. The standard approximation is root sum of squares: displayed rise time ≈ √(signal² + scope²). Probes add a term too, but with a decent 10X probe the scope dominates. Plug in each scope's figure and the gap gets concrete.

Calculated, not measured. Worked example for the 5 ns row: √(5² + 7²) = √74 = 8.6 ns on the Rigol, and √(5² + 2.4²) = √30.8 = 5.5 ns on the Hantek. The usual target is a scope three to five times faster than the edge. That puts the DS1054Z's comfortable floor near 20 to 35 ns and the DSO2D15's near 7 to 12 ns.

Slow edges level the field

Most hobby signals, from Arduino GPIO and I2C to motor PWM and supply ripple, have edges well above 20 ns. At 50 ns the two scopes disagree by half a nanosecond. The DSO2D15's speed only pays off when you actually work with fast logic, gate drive, ringing or clocks above about 10 MHz.

What each one can and can't measure

Hantek DSO2D15: fast edges, two at a time

The DSO2D15 handles edge timing, ringing and clocks into the tens of MHz, and its generator and five decoders make it a compact embedded-debug bench. Its limits: two inputs, so you can't see a full SPI transaction at once, and 500 MSa/s per channel with both on, which is 3.3 samples per cycle at 150 MHz. It's 8-bit, so small signals on big offsets show coarse steps, and its high-res mode trades speed for resolution.

Rigol DS1054Z: more inputs, slower edges

The DS1054Z suits multi-signal timing: bus lines, sequenced supplies, multi-phase PWM. Rigol lists up to 30,000 waveforms per second and a 1 mV/div range, half the Hantek's smallest setting. The 7 ns rise time makes it a poor tool for measuring edges faster than about 20 ns; it will show that they happen, and when, but will report them slower than they are. With three or four channels on, each samples at 250 MSa/s.

Both are earth-referenced mains instruments

Neither scope is isolated. Hantek rates the DSO2D15's inputs at 300 V CAT II; Rigol's DS1000Z datasheet lists CAT I 300 Vrms and CAT II 100 Vrms. For a car this is fine on the 12 V side; for mains-side circuits, use a differential probe and don't clip a ground lead to a live node. Leave household wiring to an electrician.

Where the DS1054Z's paperwork gets messy

Rigol's DS1000Z datasheet lists 24 Mpts and parallel, UART, I2C and SPI decoding with no option label. Its product page lists 12 Mpts standard, 24 Mpts optional, and calls bus trigger and decode an option. If deep memory or decoding is why you're choosing the Rigol, confirm what your unit ships with. Hantek's spec table for the DSO2D15 has no such split; its decoding and 8 Mpts are listed as standard.

The DS1054Z's other matchups are Rigol DS1054Z vs Hantek DSO2D10, which looks at channels against the generator, Rigol DS1054Z vs DS1202Z-E and Rigol DS1102Z-E vs DS1054Z. For the Hantek, see Hantek DSO2D15 vs DSO2D10 and Hantek DSO2D15 vs FNIRSI 1014D. Our Rigol DS1054Z review covers that scope on its own, and measuring rise time with cursors shows the technique.

Choose the Hantek DSO2D15 if…

  • ✓You time edges faster than about 20 ns, such as fast logic or MOSFET gate drive
  • ✓You look at clocks above 10 MHz and want them drawn as squares
  • ✓You want a built-in signal source and CAN or LIN decoding
  • ✓Two channels cover what you probe at once

Choose the Rigol DS1054Z if…

  • ✓You watch SPI clock, chip select and both data lines together
  • ✓You want deep memory, up to 24 Mpts on one channel per Rigol's datasheet
  • ✓You want a scope our owner has tested hands-on
  • ✓Your signals stay under about 10 MHz, which covers most hobby and repair work

Neither if…

You need four channels and fast edges at once. A four-channel 100 MHz scope such as the Siglent SDS1104X-E covers both better than either of these, at a higher price.

The bottom line

Pick the Rigol DS1054Z if you need to see three or four signals together or want deep memory, and your edges are slower than about 20 ns, which covers most hobby and repair benches. Pick the Hantek DSO2D15 if you measure fast edges or clocks above 10 MHz, or want a built-in generator and CAN or LIN decoding, and two inputs at a time are enough.

Frequently asked questions

Is 150 MHz overkill compared to the DS1054Z's 50 MHz?+

For most hobby work, yes. Arduino, I2C, UART and PWM edges are slow enough that 50 MHz reads them within a few percent. The extra bandwidth matters for edges under about 20 ns, clocks above 10 MHz, and ringing on fast switching nodes, where the DS1054Z's 7 ns rise time inflates what you see.

Can the Hantek DSO2D15 replace a four-channel scope for SPI?+

Not fully. With two channels you can watch clock and one data line, or chip select and clock, and decode SPI from those, but not see all four lines in the same capture. The DS1054Z shows CLK, CS, MOSI and MISO together, which is how most SPI timing faults are found.

Has OhmSavvy tested the Hantek DSO2D15?+

No. The Rigol DS1054Z is one of the scopes our owner has bought and tested hands-on, and it carries our Hands-on Tested badge. The DSO2D15 assessment is desk research from Hantek's published spec table, and the rise-time figures on this page are calculated, not measured.

Does the DS1054Z have a signal generator?+

No. In Rigol's DS1000Z range, the built-in two-channel 25 MHz source is on the S models such as the DS1104Z-S Plus. The Hantek DSO2D15 has a one-channel generator with 25 MHz sine and 10 MHz square and arbitrary waves, up to 7 Vpp into high impedance.

Which one is easier to carry around?+

The Hantek DSO2D15. Hantek lists it at 1900 g and 318 × 110 × 150 mm, while Rigol's datasheet gives the DS1054Z 3.2 kg and 313.1 × 160.8 × 122.4 mm. Both run on mains power, so neither is a field instrument, but the Hantek is the lighter bench scope to move around.
