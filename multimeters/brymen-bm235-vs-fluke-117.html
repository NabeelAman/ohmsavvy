Brymen BM235 vs Fluke 117

Brymen BM235 vs Fluke 117 is the enthusiast's favorite against the electrician's standard. On the core electrician features they match: True RMS, 6,000 counts, a low-impedance voltage mode and non-contact voltage detection. The BM235 piles on a VFD mode, Type-K temperature, relative zero and µA/mA ranges; the 117 answers with a bar graph, a three-year warranty and Fluke's US support network.

Brymen BM235 vs Fluke 117 spec by spec

Brymen publishes a full accuracy table for the BM230 Series in its catalog, and Fluke publishes one for the 110 Series. Set side by side, the Brymen is the tighter instrument on paper for DC volts and line-frequency AC volts, and it resolves far smaller currents. The Fluke's advantages are elsewhere: the analog-style bar graph, a lower continuity threshold that only beeps on a near-dead short, and the paperwork behind the brand.

What those numbers mean on the job: for checking a 120 V receptacle, either meter is far more accurate than the question needs. A 0.5% error on 120 V is 0.6 V. The gap matters on a 0-10 V control signal or a 4-20 mA loop, where the BM235's mA ranges read loop current directly at 0.01 mA resolution and the 117 can only show whole milliamps on its 6 A range. For furnace flame-sensor current in microamps, only the Brymen has a range at all.

Two kinds of low-impedance input

Both meters fight ghost voltage, the phantom reading a disconnected wire picks up from a live neighbor in the same conduit, by loading the circuit with a low input impedance. They do it differently. Fluke gives one figure for the 117's AutoVolt position: about 3 kΩ. Brymen publishes a curve. Below about 8 V the BM235's AutoV input stays above 8 MΩ, so it doesn't load a low-voltage control circuit. Between 9 V and 50 V it falls to about 2.6 kΩ. Push well past 50 V and it starts near 2.1 kΩ, then climbs within a fraction of a second to about 12 kΩ at 100 V and 240 kΩ at 600 V.

Why the rise matters is our arithmetic, not a Brymen statement: at 600 V a 240 kΩ load dissipates about 1.5 W (600² ÷ 240,000), while a fixed 2.1 kΩ would have to absorb more than 170 W. A ghost voltage never gets that far, because the coupled source can't hold its voltage up once the meter loads it, so it collapses while the BM235 is still in its low-impedance region. A real supply holds its voltage and drives the meter up the curve. For a walk-through of the check itself, see why a multimeter shows voltage on a disconnected circuit.

Reading the BM235's safety rating

Practical meaning

For household panels and building wiring, both meters carry the CAT III 600V rating you need. The BM235's extra CAT IV 300V rating suits low-voltage service points; neither is a CAT IV 600V meter. Brymen protects the BM235's current inputs with 0.4 A and 11 A, 1000 V HBC fuses rated to interrupt 30 kA and 20 kA.

Who each meter suits

The BM235 is the better instrument for anyone who works across trades. An HVAC technician gets temperature, microamps for flame rectification and capacitance up to 10 mF for run and start capacitors in one meter. A maintenance technician on drives gets a VFD mode with a low-pass filter that reads the fundamental voltage of a PWM motor output, specified at ±(1.0% + 3) from 10 Hz to 100 Hz, a job where an ordinary True RMS input reads the switching noise too.

The 117 is the safer purchase for a working electrician who wants the meter the inspector, the employer and the rental crib already recognize. Its bar graph tracks a drifting reading better than digits alone, its 9V battery lasts about 400 hours, and Fluke's three-year warranty is stated plainly. We have owner-tested the 117; the BM235 assessment here is desk research against Brymen's catalog. Our Fluke 117 review covers the Fluke in depth.

Safety: a beep is not a voltage test

Neither the BM235's EF detection nor the 117's VoltAlert proves a conductor is dead: shielding, distance and DC all hide voltage from a non-contact sensor. Confirm with the test leads on a known live source before and after you test the circuit (live-dead-live). Keep both meters to CAT III 600V work, which covers panels and branch circuits; at the service entrance or meter base, use a CAT IV 600V meter or call a licensed electrician.

Frequently asked questions

Do both the Brymen BM235 and Fluke 117 have LoZ and NCV?+

Yes. Brymen lists AutoV LoZ and Hi/Lo EF non-contact detection for the BM235, and Fluke lists AutoVolt with a low-impedance input of about 3 kΩ plus VoltAlert for the 117. The Brymen's input impedance changes with the applied voltage, while Fluke quotes a single figure, but both are built to collapse ghost voltage on disconnected wires.

What is the Brymen BM235's CAT rating?+

Brymen's BM230 Series catalog and the meter's own markings give CAT III 600V and CAT IV 300V. Its BM235 product sheet and web page list CAT II 1kV and CAT III 600V instead. Either way it covers CAT III 600V household and panel work, the same as the Fluke 117, and neither is rated CAT IV 600V for service entrances.

Why is the BM235 sold as an EEVblog meter?+

The US Amazon listing we link is titled EEVblog Brymen BM235. It is the Brymen meter sold through that channel, which is how most US buyers get one. Support and warranty run through Brymen's distributors rather than a US service network, so check the seller, the contents and the return terms on the listing before you buy.

Can the Fluke 117 measure a VFD output?+

It can read the True RMS voltage, but Fluke doesn't list a VFD mode or low-pass filter for the 117, so the switching noise on a PWM drive output can inflate the reading. The BM235's VFD mode filters that out and reads the fundamental voltage and frequency, specified at ±(1.0% + 3) from 10 Hz to 100 Hz.

Which meter is better for HVAC work?+

The BM235, for most technicians. It reads Type-K temperature from -40 °C to 400 °C with an included bead probe, has a 600.0 µA range for flame-sensor current and measures capacitance up to 10 mF. Fluke's datasheet puts temperature and microamps on the Fluke 116, not the 117, so the 117 would need extra tools for those checks.
