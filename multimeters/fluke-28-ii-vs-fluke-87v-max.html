Fluke 28 II vs Fluke 87V MAX: Which Is Better for Harsh Environments?

For mines and other MSHA-regulated sites, pick the Fluke 28 II: Fluke lists it as MSHA approved and doesn't list that approval for the 87V MAX. For every other harsh environment, the 87V MAX is the stronger choice, rated for a 4 m drop against the 28 II's 3 m, with tighter DC current accuracy and published accuracy for resistance, capacitance and frequency. The two meters share a lot: IP67 sealing, the same cold rating (−15 to 55 °C, down to −40 °C for 20 minutes), three AA cells for about 800 hours, a 698.5 g holstered weight, CAT IV 600V / CAT III 1000V and a limited lifetime warranty.

Two sealed meters, one real fork in the road

Put the two spec tabs side by side and the surprise is how little separates them. Both cases are IP67, both weigh exactly 698.5 g in the holster, both run three AA cells for about 800 hours, and both carry the same cold rating. The choice turns on two lines: who has approved the meter, and how far it is rated to fall.

28 II: MSHA

The only one of the two with a listed mining approval.

One meter (about 3 ft) more than the 28 II's holstered rating.

87V MAX: DC amps

The DC amps gap is small in practice. Two extra counts add the value of two steps of the last digit to the error allowance, so on a reading such as 2.000 A, if the range resolves 0.001 A, the 28 II's window is wider by 0.002 A. That is irrelevant for motor and heater currents and only starts to matter on precise loop readings. The bigger practical gap is that Fluke doesn't publish the 28 II's resistance, capacitance or frequency accuracy, so a quality system that wants a stated tolerance for every function used will find it only on the MAX.

IP67 means the case is dust-tight (the 6) and protected against temporary immersion (the 7); the usual IEC 60529 test is about 1 m of water for 30 minutes. Neither is rated for continuous submersion, and both still need the battery door and jacks clean and closed. Our Fluke 87V vs 87V MAX comparison decodes the IP digits in more detail.

The cold rows are worth reading carefully. Both meters work continuously only down to −15 °C. The −40 °C figure is an excursion: Fluke words it as 20 minutes, and on the MAX adds "when taken from 20 °C", meaning a warm meter carried out of a heated cab. A technician who works a whole shift outdoors in a northern winter is outside both ratings, so keep the meter inside a jacket between readings.

What MSHA approval does, and doesn't, buy you

MSHA is the US Mine Safety and Health Administration. If your employer's electrical program for a mine specifies MSHA-approved instruments, the 28 II is the one of these two that Fluke lists, and that single line ends the comparison. Ask your safety department to confirm which approval conditions apply at your site.

Approval is not intrinsic safety. Fluke sells the 28 II EX as its intrinsically safe version for hazardous (explosive) locations, and neither the standard 28 II nor the 87V MAX is described that way. If your work is in a classified area at a refinery, grain elevator or gas plant, you need the EX model and a permit, not either meter on this page.

Daily-use details: leads, holster and calibration paperwork

The 87V MAX ships with TL175 TwistGuard leads, AC175 clips and a holster with built-in lead storage. It also lists Input Alert, which Fluke describes as an audible warning when the leads are in the wrong input jacks for the selected function. The 28 II ships with TL75 leads, AC175 clips and a reversible rubber holster; Fluke's page doesn't mention Input Alert.

On paperwork, the 28 II's Amazon listing says the unit is calibrated before shipment and comes with a NIST-traceable certificate with data. That is the seller's claim rather than part of Fluke's box contents, and the offer is from a third-party seller, so confirm it's still described when you order. If your plant needs a certificate before a meter goes into service, it can save a trip to a cal lab.

Same armor, different paperwork

The 28 II and 87V MAX share the IP67 seal, the cold rating, the battery and the weight. Buy the 28 II if a mining approval is on your checklist. Otherwise the 87V MAX is the better harsh-site meter: one more meter of drop rating, two fewer counts on DC amps, and a full set of published accuracy figures.

Safety: a sealed meter is not a sealed circuit

IP67 keeps water out of the meter, not out of the circuit you're measuring. Wet gloves, wet probes and standing water all shorten the path from a live conductor to you. Dry the probe tips, use leads rated for the CAT IV 600V / CAT III 1000V category you're working in, and follow your site's lockout/tagout and arc-flash PPE rules. Never take either meter into a classified explosive atmosphere. If a job is outside your training or permit, stop and call a qualified electrician.

The bottom line

Both meters are IP67, share the same cold rating, AA battery life, weight and CAT IV 600V / CAT III 1000V protection, so the choice is narrow. Buy the Fluke 28 II if your site needs MSHA-approved equipment or you want the listing's NIST-traceable certificate. For every other harsh environment, buy the Fluke 87V MAX: it's rated for a 4 m drop instead of 3 m, carries two fewer counts on DC amps, and Fluke publishes its resistance, capacitance and frequency accuracy.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Fluke 28 II the same meter as the 87V MAX?+

No, though they're close. Both are IP67, weigh 698.5 g holstered, run 800 hours on three AAs and share DC volts, AC volts and AC amps accuracy. The 28 II adds MSHA approval. The 87V MAX adds a 4 m drop rating, ±(0.2% + 2) DC amps against 0.2% + 4, TL175 leads and published accuracy for ohms, capacitance and frequency.

Is the Fluke 87V MAX MSHA approved?+

Fluke's 87V MAX page lists CE and CSA approvals and doesn't mention MSHA. The 28 II page lists MSHA approval. If your mine's electrical program specifies MSHA-approved instruments, buy the 28 II or confirm the 87V MAX's status with Fluke and your safety department before ordering.

Can the Fluke 28 II or 87V MAX go underwater?+

Briefly. IP67 covers temporary immersion, usually tested at about 1 m for 30 minutes under IEC 60529, so a drop into a puddle or sump shouldn't kill either meter. Neither is rated for continuous submersion. Dry it, open the battery door to check for moisture, and never take readings with a wet meter or wet probes.

Which is better in extreme cold, the 28 II or the 87V MAX?+

Neither has an edge. Fluke rates both for −15 to 55 °C continuously and down to −40 °C for up to 20 minutes; the MAX page adds that the excursion assumes the meter starts at 20 °C. For all-day work below −15 °C, both are outside their rating, so keep the meter warm between readings.

Do the Fluke 28 II and 87V MAX have a low-pass filter for VFDs?+

Yes, both do, and both list 250 µs peak capture and a −200 to 1090 °C temperature input with the 80BK-A probe. That makes either one usable on variable-frequency drive outputs in a wet or dusty plant. Fluke doesn't list a LoZ ghost-voltage mode for either meter.

Is the Fluke 28 II intrinsically safe?+

No. Fluke sells a separate model, the 28 II EX, as the intrinsically safe version for explosive atmospheres. The standard 28 II is MSHA approved and IP67, which is a different thing. The same applies to the 87V MAX: rugged and sealed, but not for use in a classified hazardous location.
