Fieldpiece SC260 vs Fluke 323: Which Clamp Meter Should You Buy?

Fieldpiece SC260 vs Fluke 323 is a brand-and-feature-set decision at a similar price, not a simple upgrade pick. The SC260 is an HVACR specialist's clamp: the same 400A AC current clamp as the Fluke, plus capacitance for run capacitors, a Type K temperature input and non-contact voltage, all on a CAT III 600V body. The Fluke 323 drops every one of those extras and adds the one thing the SC260 doesn't have: a CAT IV 300V rating, alongside Fluke's two-year warranty and published accuracy figures for its core functions. Neither meter has been bought and bench-tested by OhmSavvy; everything below comes from each maker's own pages and official manuals, cited as we go.

  • →Safety category: the Fluke 323 adds CAT IV 300V on top of CAT III 600V; Fieldpiece's SC260 manual states CAT III 600V only, with no CAT IV figure anywhere we found.
  • →Function count: the SC260 adds capacitance, Type K temperature and NCV that the Fluke 323 simply doesn't have — it measures only volts, amps and ohms/continuity.
  • →Published accuracy: Fluke states AC current accuracy (2% / 2.5% ± 5 digits) for the 323; neither Fieldpiece's product page nor the operator's manual pages we read state an AC current accuracy figure for the SC260, only the 400A clamp rating.

The real choice: a dial full of extras, or four positions

Both meters clamp around a conductor and read AC current the same way, so the decision isn't about current measurement at all — it's about what else lives on the dial. The SC260's rotary switch has seven stops; three of them (temperature, capacitance, NCV) don't exist on the Fluke 323's dial of four.

For a tech who carries one meter into a mechanical room, the SC260's extra stops mean checking a run capacitor, a duct temperature and a possibly-live junction box without switching tools. For someone who already owns a Fluke multimeter or an infrared thermometer, those three stops are functions they'll never touch on this meter, and the Fluke 323 is the simpler, more focused instrument.

Safety rating: the one spec that actually separates them

Strip away the HVACR extras and the single hard difference that matters for where you're allowed to work is CAT rating. Fluke states CAT III 600V and CAT IV 300V on the 323's own product page. Fieldpiece's SC260 operator's manual states CAT III 600V, Class II, for indoor use, and we found no CAT IV figure for it anywhere, including the manufacturer's own product page, which instead tells buyers to pair the meter with leads "rated CATIII 600V or above."

CAT IV applies to the service entrance, the meter base and the line side of the main disconnect — the locations with the highest transient energy. If any of your work touches those points, the Fluke 323 is rated for it (at 300V) and the SC260, as documented, is not. For everything downstream of the main panel, both meters' CAT III 600V rating applies equally. Our CAT III vs CAT IV guide and clamp meter buying guide cover why that distinction exists.

A published CAT rating only protects you if the leads match it

Fieldpiece explicitly tells SC260 buyers to use leads rated CAT III 600V or higher — a reminder that a meter's printed category is only half the story. On either meter, inspect leads for cracked insulation before every job, and never exceed the rating printed on the leads even if the meter body is rated higher.

What each one can and can't measure

Fieldpiece SC260: built around the HVACR service call

Past the shared 400A current clamp, the SC260 adds the three measurements an AC/R technician reaches for most: capacitance for a failing run or start capacitor, Type K temperature (-30°F to 752°F per the operator's manual) for a supply or return duct, and non-contact voltage for a quick first check on an unfamiliar junction box. True RMS is listed for the SC260 specifically — the otherwise similar SC220 and SC240 in the same family don't have it. What it can't do: there's no microamp DC range (that stayed on the SC220, for flame-rectification sensors), no manual ranging, and no AC current accuracy figure published anywhere we could find, which makes it hard to judge how tight a current reading really is next to a competitor that states its number.

Fluke 323: current, voltage and resistance, stated plainly

The Fluke 323 does less by design. Its four dial positions cover AC/DC voltage to 600V, AC current to 400.0A, and resistance with continuity, and Fluke publishes an accuracy figure for every one of them: 2% (or 2.5% above 65 Hz) on current, 1.5% on AC volts, 1.0% on DC volts and on resistance. What it can't do: no DC current, no capacitance, no frequency and no temperature — those functions start on the step-up 324 and 325 in the same family, not the 323.

A stated number beats an implied one

The SC260's 400A rating and the Fluke 323's 400.0A rating look identical on a spec sheet, but Fluke backs its figure with a tolerance you can compute against — 2% ± 5 digits is a number you can check a reading against. Fieldpiece's page gives the clamp a current limit, not an accuracy, so you know the SC260 won't read current above 400A reliably, but not how far off its reading can be below that limit.

Skip the SC260 if you'll never use capacitance, temperature or NCV — you'd be carrying functions you won't touch, without the CAT IV rating in return. Skip the Fluke 323 if you're an HVACR tech who wants one meter for the full service call; its simplicity means a second tool (a thermometer, a capacitance tester) for jobs the SC260 does alone. If you need both AC/R extras and CAT IV, neither meter here covers that combination — look at a model like the Fieldpiece SC480, which adds a CAT IV rating in Fieldpiece's own lineup.

The trust question with this pair

Fluke and Fieldpiece don't publish the same depth of documentation for these two meters. Fluke states an accuracy figure for current, both voltage functions and resistance, plus a display-adjacent spec sheet with ranges down to the digit. Fieldpiece confirms the display count (4000) and gives detailed voltage and temperature accuracy in its operator's manual, but neither its product page nor the manual pages we could read state an AC current accuracy or a CAT rating above CAT III. That doesn't make the SC260 less capable — Fieldpiece sells thousands of these to working AC/R techs — but it does mean a buyer comparing the two on paper has more to go on with the Fluke for the functions both meters share.

The bottom line

The Fieldpiece SC260 is the stronger single tool for an HVACR service call: capacitance, Type K temperature and non-contact voltage on top of the shared 400A current clamp, in a CAT III 600V body with a one-year warranty. The Fluke 323 drops every one of those extras in exchange for a CAT IV 300V rating, stated accuracy on every function it has, and a two-year warranty — the better pick if you work near panels or just want a dedicated, well-documented clamp. Frequently asked questions

Is the Fieldpiece SC260 CAT IV rated like the Fluke 323?+

No. Fluke states CAT III 600V and CAT IV 300V on the Fluke 323's own product page. Fieldpiece's SC260 operator's manual states CAT III 600V, Class II, for indoor use, and neither the manual pages nor the product page we checked list a CAT IV figure for the meter itself. Fieldpiece's page also tells buyers to pair the SC260 with leads rated CAT III 600V or above, which is a lead requirement, not a higher meter rating.

Does the Fluke 323 measure temperature or capacitance like the SC260?+

No. The Fluke 323's dial covers only AC/DC voltage, AC current and resistance/continuity. Temperature and capacitance don't appear until Fluke's 324 and 325 models in the same clamp-meter family. The Fieldpiece SC260 has both built in, plus non-contact voltage, which the Fluke 323 also lacks.

Which clamp meter has better accuracy, the SC260 or the Fluke 323?+

We can't fully compare them on current accuracy, because Fluke publishes a figure for the 323 (2% ± 5 digits from 45-65 Hz, 2.5% above that) and neither Fieldpiece's product page nor the operator's manual pages we read state a current accuracy for the SC260 — only its 400A clamp limit. On voltage, the SC260's manual lists ±(0.5% + 2) on DC and ±(1.2% to 1.5% + 8) on AC; the Fluke 323 lists ±(1.0% + 5 digits) on DC and ±(1.5% + 5 digits) on AC. The ranges aren't directly comparable because the digit conventions differ.

What's the jaw opening on each meter?+

Fluke states a maximum wire diameter of 30 mm (600 MCM) for the 323 on its own product page. Fieldpiece doesn't state a jaw size on the SC260 pages we checked; multiple independent retailer listings consistently give about 1.2 in (30 mm), which we treat as retailer-sourced rather than manufacturer-confirmed. In practice the two clamps appear to open to almost the same size.

Has OhmSavvy hands-on tested either the Fieldpiece SC260 or the Fluke 323?+

No. Neither meter carries this site's Hands-on Tested badge.
