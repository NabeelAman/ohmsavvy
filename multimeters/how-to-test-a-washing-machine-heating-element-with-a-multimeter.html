How to Test a Washing Machine Heating Element With a Multimeter

To test a washing machine heating element with a multimeter, unplug the machine, remove the back panel, pull the wires off the element's two terminals and measure resistance across them. On UK 230V machines a healthy element usually reads somewhere between about 20 Ω and 35 Ω depending on its wattage; OL means it has burnt out. Then check each terminal to the metal flange: anything other than OL means it is leaking to earth, the classic cause of an RCD tripping mid-wash.

Unplug it, don't just switch it off

  • Pull the plug out of the socket before removing any panel. Washing machines hold live parts behind the back cover even when the program dial is off.
  • Wait for the drum to cool if it has just run a hot wash, and have a towel ready for drips.
  • Resistance tests only work on a dead, disconnected part. Never run the machine with panels off to "see if it heats".

Symptoms of a failed heating element

  • Clothes come out cold on a 40 °C or 60 °C wash, and the glass door never warms up.
  • The RCD in the consumer unit trips part-way into a wash, usually soon after filling.
  • A wash cycle that runs far longer than normal, or stalls, while the machine waits for heat.
  • A heating or temperature fault code: the code list in the manual tells you which part it suspects.

1Unplug the machine and reach the element

Unplug the washer and pull it out far enough to work behind it, minding the hoses. On most front loaders the element sits low under the drum, behind the back panel; a few brands put it behind the front panel instead. Undo the panel screws and look for a flange with two spade terminals, a central nut and, often, a small sensor plugged into it.

2Measure resistance across the two terminals

Pull both supply wires off the element so nothing else in the machine is measured alongside it. Set the meter to Ω (on a manual meter, the 200 Ω range) and touch one probe to each terminal. Touch the probes together first and note the reading: subtract that lead resistance, since it matters at these low values.

3Check each terminal to earth

Set the meter to its highest resistance range. Put one probe on the earth stud or a clean, bare part of the flange, and the other on terminal A, then terminal B. Both should read OL. A reading in the kΩ or MΩ range means the heating wire is leaking through its insulation to the earthed sheath.

A multimeter is not an insulation tester

Your meter checks insulation with only a few volts. An element can pass this test cold and still leak at 230V when hot. If the RCD trips on heat and this test is clean, an insulation resistance test at 500V by an electrician or appliance engineer is the definitive check.

4Check the temperature sensor

Many machines clip an NTC thermistor into the element flange to tell the board the water temperature. If it fails, the machine may refuse to heat or show a temperature fault even with a perfect element. Unplug it and measure its resistance; most read several kΩ to tens of kΩ at room temperature and fall as they warm. The step-by-step method is in how to test a thermistor with a multimeter, and the service manual gives the exact value for your model.

Which multimeter you need

Both tests are resistance measurements on a dead appliance, so any working digital meter is enough. Auto-ranging saves switching between the low range for the element and the high range for the earth check. For a wider comparison, see our best multimeter for home use guide.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my washing machine trip the RCD when it starts heating?

The most common reason is an element whose sheath has cracked or corroded, letting current leak from the heating wire to the earthed drum. The machine runs normally on a cold rinse and trips only when the element is switched in. A multimeter reading of anything less than OL from either terminal to the earth stud confirms it, though a minor leak may only show on an insulation tester.

Can a washing machine heating element test fine and still be faulty?

Yes. A multimeter tests with a few volts, so an element can show normal resistance and OL to earth yet leak once it is hot or under 230V. If the RCD keeps tripping on heat and everything else checks out, have the element insulation-tested at 500V by an electrician or appliance engineer.

Where is the heating element on a front-loading washing machine?

On most front loaders it sits under the drum and is reached from the back panel, with its terminal block facing you. Some brands put it behind the front panel instead. The flange is held by a single nut that expands a rubber seal, which also has to be loosened to remove it.

Is it worth replacing a washing machine heating element myself?

On many machines it is a single-nut part and a reasonable DIY job once the machine is unplugged and the right replacement is matched by model number. Make sure the new seal seats properly and the earth connection is refitted, then run an empty hot wash and check for leaks before trusting it.

My element reads fine but the washer still won't heat. What next?

Check the NTC temperature sensor, which is often plugged into the element itself, and the wiring and connectors to the element. Beyond that the heater relay sits on the main control board, which is a job for an appliance engineer or a board-level repairer.
