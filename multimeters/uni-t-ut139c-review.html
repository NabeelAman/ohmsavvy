The verdict

UNI-T's UT139C packs True RMS accuracy, a 6,000-count display, and both auto and manual ranging into one of the more affordable meters here — a strong value pick if you don't need a specific brand name.

Consider it if

  • ✓You want True RMS and CAT III 600V without spending much
  • ✓You like the option to lock a range manually
  • ✓You want non-contact voltage detection included
  • ✓You'd use temperature, capacitance and duty cycle on one meter, with the thermocouple probe in the box
  • ✓You prefer AA cells you can buy anywhere over a 9 V battery

Skip it if

  • ✕Brand reputation and a long service record matter to you
  • ✕The meter will take heavy job-site punishment
  • ✕You need LoZ ghost-voltage rejection; UNI-T lists it on the UT139E/S variants, not the C
  • ✕You need a microamp range for flame sensors or sleep current

The trade-off: The UT139C covers features found on meters a tier or two up, and its published DC accuracy matches the Fluke 117's on paper. The savings show in build ruggedness, brand recognition and the electrician extras such as LoZ that the C version doesn't list.

Performance assessment

We haven't bench-tested this unit. This assessment is based on its published specifications and on what those specifications mean in practice. How we research.

Features for the price

True RMS, a 6,000-count display, auto and manual ranging, and non-contact voltage detection, with CAT III 600V protection. On paper, that's a feature set comparable to the AstroAI DM6000AR, which states no CAT rating. UNI-T also lists temperature from -40 °C to 1000 °C with a probe in the box, capacitance up to 99.99 mF, frequency from 10 Hz to 10 MHz and duty cycle, so the C covers HVAC and small-electronics checks that cheaper meters leave out.

Accuracy against the big names

UNI-T specifies DC volts at ±(0.5% + 2) and True RMS AC volts at ±(0.8% + 3) from 45 Hz to 1 kHz. The DC figure is the same as Fluke publishes for the 117, and the AC figure is slightly tighter on paper. Worked from the spec, a 12.00 V battery reading could be off by 0.06 V plus 0.02 V, about ±0.08 V, which is fine for judging a car battery's state of charge. What the paper can't tell you is how well that accuracy holds over years, temperature swings and drops, and that's where established brands justify their price.

Build

Build quality is adequate rather than rugged. For home and bench use that's fine; for a meter that lives in a work truck, the Klein Tools MM400 or Fluke 117 has a stronger durability reputation. At 170 × 80 × 48 mm and 354 g it's a full-size meter, close to the Fluke 117's footprint, so it doesn't save pocket space either.

Manual ranging, when auto-ranging alone isn't enough

Most budget meters, including several others in our guide, are auto-ranging only. The UT139C's manual option matters in a specific situation: when you're taking repeated readings in a known range and want to lock the display there, rather than waiting a beat each time for auto-ranging to settle. It's a small workflow improvement, but one that separates this meter from pure auto-ranging-only competitors at a similar price.

What the C version leaves out

UNI-T sells several UT139 variants on one product page, and features don't carry across all of them. LoZ, which suppresses ghost voltage on unused conductors, is listed for the UT139E and UT139S rather than the C. Current tops out at 10 A AC and DC with no separate microamp range in the table. The series page lists a 31-segment analog bar graph, but its flattened table doesn't make clear which variants carry it, so don't count on one.

Frequently asked questions

Is the UNI-T UT139C True RMS?+

Yes. UNI-T lists True RMS on AC voltage with a 45 Hz to 1 kHz bandwidth, alongside a 6,000-count display, auto and manual ranging and non-contact voltage detection. True RMS matters when you measure the distorted waveforms from LED drivers, dimmers and inverter appliances, where an average-responding meter can read noticeably low.

UNI-T UT139C or AstroAI DM6000AR?+

On paper they're close: both are True RMS, 6,000-count, with auto and manual ranging. The UT139C is rated CAT III 600V and includes non-contact voltage detection; AstroAI states no CAT rating for the DM6000AR. For any work on household mains, the UT139C is the safer choice of the two.

Can the UNI-T UT139C measure temperature?+

Yes. UNI-T lists a temperature function from -40 °C to 1000 °C, and the UT139C ships with a temperature probe as a standard accessory. The probe itself will have a narrower rating than the meter's input, so check the probe before measuring anything very hot, such as an exhaust manifold or a furnace heat exchanger.

What batteries does the UNI-T UT139C use?+

UNI-T lists two 1.5 V R6 cells, the common AA size. That's convenient compared with the 9 V battery many meters use, since AA cells are cheap and easy to find. UNI-T also lists auto power-off for the series, which helps when the meter is left switched on in a drawer between jobs.

Is it risky to buy a meter from a less internationally known brand like UNI-T?+

UNI-T is a large, established test-equipment manufacturer internationally, even if its US name recognition lags Fluke or Klein. The real trade-off is the decades of field reputation and documented durability that Fluke has built among US tradespeople. For home, bench and light-duty use, that gap matters far less than for a meter headed into a work truck daily.
