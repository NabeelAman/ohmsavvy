Rigol DHO802 vs Hantek DSO2D10

Rigol DHO802 vs Hantek DSO2D10 is a fair fight: both are compact two-channel scopes for a home bench. The difference is what each adds to those two channels. The Rigol adds 12-bit vertical resolution, a 1024 × 600 touchscreen, standard serial decoding and USB-C power. The Hantek adds 100 MHz of bandwidth (against 70 MHz) and a built-in one-channel waveform generator up to 25 MHz.

Spec by spec

With channels tied at two, the comparison comes down to speed, detail and extras. The Hantek has the higher bandwidth; the Rigol has the higher single-channel sample rate, the only stated two-channel rate, and the higher-resolution screen.

Rigol DHO802 vs Hantek DSO2D10 for a Home Lab

A home lab usually means one shared bench, a limited budget and a mix of projects: an Arduino or ESP32 sketch one weekend, a guitar pedal or a bench supply the next. That mix decides this comparison better than any single spec.

The Hantek's case rests on one fact: it is two instruments in one enclosure. A home bench without a signal generator can't easily test a filter, an amplifier or an input stage, because there is nothing to feed in. The DSO2D10's built-in output (one channel, up to 25 MHz) fills that gap with no extra box, power lead or desk space. If you would otherwise buy a separate generator, that is the strongest reason to choose it.

The Rigol's case is about what you see on the screen. 12-bit resolution means 4,096 vertical levels against the 256 of an 8-bit scope, so the small ripple on a 5 V rail or the output of a slow sensor stays smooth when you zoom in. Standard decoding of UART, I2C, SPI and CAN turns microcontroller traffic into readable bytes, which is most of what hobby debugging involves. HDMI output lets you put the trace on a monitor, LAN connects it to your network, and Rigol specifies a USB-C 15 V supply. It is the lower-priced two-channel sibling of the four-channel DHO804.

What each one can and can't measure

Rated bandwidth is the frequency at which a sine wave is shown about 3 dB low, roughly 70% of its true amplitude. The common 3–5x rule says a scope should have three to five times the bandwidth of the fastest signal you care about, which gives the DHO802 roughly 14–23 MHz and the DSO2D10 roughly 20–33 MHz. Both comfortably cover hobby work: 16 MHz microcontroller clocks are near the top of the DHO802's range, while UART, I2C, PWM, audio and typical switching supplies sit far below it. Square waves need more margin than sines because their edges carry higher harmonics, so the Hantek shows sharp digital edges a little more faithfully. See what oscilloscope bandwidth means for the full explanation.

On sample rate the Rigol holds up well with both channels running: 625 MSa/s each is nearly nine samples per cycle of a 70 MHz sine, well above the Nyquist minimum of two. The Hantek reaches 1 GSa/s, ten samples per cycle at 100 MHz, but our data doesn't state its rate with both channels on, so check Hantek's datasheet if you plan to run both near full bandwidth. Our explainer on what GSa/s means covers why that figure matters.

Both run out at the same point: a third signal. Two channels cover input against output, a PWM signal against its filtered result, or a clock against one data line. For an SPI bus with chip select and two data lines, or several supply rails powering up in order, you need four channels, which puts the Rigol DHO804 on the list instead. And on the Hantek, using the generator for a stimulus-response test normally takes both inputs, one for the signal going in and one for what comes out.

Ask what your bench is missing

If you have no signal generator, the Hantek fills two gaps for the price of one instrument. If you already have a source, or your projects are mostly microcontroller code and power rails, the Rigol's resolution, decoding and screen do more for you every day.

Mains and high-voltage circuits

A scope's probe ground is normally tied to earth through its supply or connected equipment, whether it runs from mains or USB-C. Never clip it to a mains-referenced node; use a differential probe for mains work.

The bottom line

For a home lab that already has a signal source, or works mostly on microcontroller and power projects, the Rigol DHO802 is the better two-channel scope: 12-bit resolution, standard serial decoding, a sharper touchscreen, HDMI and LAN. If your bench has no generator and you build audio circuits, filters or amplifiers, the Hantek DSO2D10's built-in 25 MHz generator and 100 MHz bandwidth make it the more complete single purchase.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better for a home lab, the Rigol DHO802 or the Hantek DSO2D10?+

It depends on what your bench already has. Without a signal generator, the Hantek's built-in 25 MHz output makes it the more complete single instrument. With a generator, or for mostly microcontroller and power-supply work, the DHO802's 12-bit resolution, standard decoding and 1024 × 600 touchscreen give it the edge.

Can the Hantek DSO2D10's generator replace a separate function generator?+

For many home-lab tasks, yes. It is a single output up to 25 MHz, enough to drive audio circuits, filters and sensor inputs while the scope watches the result. It won't give you two independent signals at once, and it doesn't add scope channels, so check Hantek's documentation for waveform options.

Should I buy the DHO802 or the four-channel DHO804?+

Both share 70 MHz, 12-bit resolution and the same touchscreen. The DHO802 is the lower-priced two-channel model and keeps 625 MSa/s per channel with both on. Pick the DHO804 if you expect to watch three or four signals together, such as an SPI bus or several supply rails.

Is two channels enough for Arduino and ESP32 projects?+

Usually. Two channels cover a clock against data on I2C, a PWM pin against its filtered output, or a trigger against a response. On the DHO802, standard UART, I2C and SPI decoding helps further. You need four when chip select and two SPI data lines must line up with the clock.

Does the Rigol DHO802 need mains power?+

Rigol specifies a USB-C 15 V supply for the DHO802 as its power input. Our data doesn't state battery operation for this model. Like any scope, its ground can connect to earth through the supply or connected equipment, so treat it like any bench scope around mains circuits.
