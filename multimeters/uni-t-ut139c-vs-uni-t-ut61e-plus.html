UNI-T UT139C vs UT61E+: Electrician's Meter or Bench Meter?

UNI-T UT139C vs UT61E+ is a choice between two UNI-T meters built for different people. The UT139C is a 6,000-count CAT III 600V meter that UNI-T describes as designed for electricians, with a temperature probe in the box. The UT61E+ is a 22,000-count CAT III 1000V / CAT IV 600V meter with a 220 µA range, USB and hFE, aimed at electronics. The UT61E+ reads finer on every range; the UT139C is the only one of the two that measures temperature.

Key differences

  • →Resolution: 22,000 counts on the UT61E+ against 6,000 on the UT139C, so the E+ shows one more digit on most signals.
  • →DC accuracy: ±(0.05% + 5) on the UT61E+'s 2.2 V and 22 V ranges against ±(0.5% + 2) on the UT139C, roughly a tenth of the error at 12.6 V.
  • →Safety: the UT61E+ claims CAT III 1000V / CAT IV 600V and reads to 1000 V; the UT139C is CAT III 600V and stops at 600 V.
  • →Temperature: the UT139C measures -40 to 1000 °C with a probe in the box; the UT61E+ has no temperature function at all.
  • →Current: the UT61E+ has a 220.00 µA range at 0.01 µA; UNI-T's UT139C table lists only the 10 A top range.
  • →AC bandwidth: 40 Hz to 10 kHz on the UT61E+ against 45 Hz to 1 kHz on the UT139C.
  • →LoZ: neither has it. UNI-T keeps LoZ for the UT139E/S and the UT61D+.

UNI-T UT139C vs UT61E+, range by range

More counts only help where the extra digit lands on your signal. The ladder below shows where each meter changes range and what its last digit is worth. At 3.3 V both resolve 1 mV. At 1.8 V and 12.6 V the UT61E+ resolves ten times finer, because its 2.2 V and 22 V ranges sit just above those signals.

Resolution is only half of it. Accuracy decides whether that last digit means anything. Here is the error band on three common voltages, worked from UNI-T's own figures. On a 12.6 V battery the UT139C's ±(0.5% + 2) on the 60.00 V range gives 0.063 V plus 0.02 V, so ±0.083 V. The UT61E+ on its 22.000 V range gives 0.0063 V plus 0.005 V, so ±0.011 V. On a 3.3 V rail it is ±0.0185 V against ±0.0067 V.

For a car battery, ±0.083 V is fine: the difference between 12.6 V and 12.2 V is a charged battery against a half-flat one, and the UT139C sees it easily. For a 3.3 V regulator whose spec allows ±2%, or a voltage reference, the UT61E+'s tighter band is the one you want. Our guide to measuring a voltage reference works through why.

What the UT139C does that the UT61E+ can't

Temperature. UNI-T ships the UT139C with a thermocouple probe and lists -40 to 1000 °C at ±(1% + 3). The UT61E+ has no temperature position, so HVAC techs checking supply air, or anyone checking a heatsink, would need a second tool. UNI-T puts temperature on the UT61D+ instead.

The UT139C is also smaller, 170 × 80 × 48 mm against 190 × 90 × 50 mm, and runs on two AA cells. UNI-T sells an optional AC/DC 40 A current sensor for it. It lists VFC, which the Amazon listing describes as measuring signals with varied frequency, useful on motor drive outputs; the UT61E+ answers that job with its own low-pass filter mode. Neither meter has LoZ, so neither can tell a ghost voltage from a real one. If that matters, see Fluke 117 vs UNI-T UT139C.

Which should you buy?

Buy the UT139C if most of your readings are on household wiring, appliances and HVAC equipment, where you want temperature and don't need a fourth digit. Buy the UT61E+ if you spend more time with a soldering iron than a screwdriver: microamps, logic rails, frequency and a PC link all point that way. The UT61E+ also covers higher voltages on paper, 1000 V and CAT IV 600V, which helps on solar and battery systems.

Buy for the signal you measure most

A 22,000-count meter on a 120 V outlet shows 120.04 V where a 6,000-count meter shows 120.0 V. Nobody needs the extra digit there. On a 1.8 V rail it is the difference between 1.800 V and 1.8003 V, and on a 150 µA sleep current it is the difference between a reading and none.

The fuse fine print on the UT61E+

UNI-T's manual lists 240 V fuses for the UT61E+'s 1 A and 10 A inputs, below its 1000 V and CAT IV ratings. Keep the leads in the voltage jacks for mains work, and don't open a service panel with either meter unless you are trained to; call a licensed electrician. Our CAT III vs CAT IV guide explains what the ratings cover.

The trust question: UNI-T's own paperwork

Both meters come from one maker, so the paperwork gaps are UNI-T's. The UT139 datasheet's flattened table prints some rows differently from the web table, DC volts for one, so we used the web table. The UT139A/B/C manual on UNI-T's site is a scanned PDF, which is why we can't state the UT139C's lowest current range or its warranty term. For the UT61E+, the web page says 350 g while the manual says 400 g, and the warranty in the manual is one year.

The UT139C has its own review and comparisons with the Fluke 115, Extech EX330 and KAIWEETS KM200s. The UT61E+ goes up against a Fluke in UNI-T UT61E+ vs Fluke 117, and both appear in our best multimeters under $100.

Choose the UNI-T UT139C if…

  • ✓You check outlets, HVAC controls and appliances, where 0.1 V at 120 V is plenty
  • ✓You want a K-type temperature probe included
  • ✓You'd rather feed it two AA cells than four AAA

Choose the UNI-T UT61E+ if…

  • ✓You work on circuit boards and want 0.1 mV steps on 1.8 V logic rails
  • ✓You measure sleep or standby current in microamps
  • ✓You want USB logging to a PC, hFE and frequency to 220 MHz

Neither if…

You prove circuits dead for a living. Neither has LoZ, so both can show ghost voltage on a disconnected wire; UNI-T's UT139E/S or an electrician's meter like the Fluke 117 has it.

The bottom line

The UNI-T UT61E+ is the better instrument on paper: 22,000 counts, ±(0.05% + 5) DC on its middle ranges, a 220 µA range, 1000 V and CAT IV 600V, USB and a 10 kHz AC bandwidth. The UNI-T UT139C is the better fit for electrical and HVAC checks: smaller, CAT III 600V, and the only one of the two with a temperature probe.

Frequently asked questions

Is the UNI-T UT61E+ better than the UT139C?+

For electronics, yes: 22,000 counts against 6,000, DC accuracy of ±(0.05% + 5) on its 2.2 V and 22 V ranges against ±(0.5% + 2), a 220 µA range and a PC link. For HVAC and household checks the UT139C holds its own, because it measures temperature, which the UT61E+ cannot, and its 6,000 counts are enough at 120 V.

Does the UNI-T UT61E+ measure temperature?+

No. UNI-T's UT61+ datasheet lists temperature only for the UT61D+, which ships with a probe. The UT61E+ trades that for hFE, a low-pass filter, AC+DC mode and its 22,000-count display. If you want UNI-T resolution and temperature in one meter, look at the UT181A, which reads two thermocouples.

Can the UT139C measure microamps?+

UNI-T's searchable spec table for the UT139 series lists current only up to its 10 A top range, and the UT139C manual on UNI-T's site is a scanned document, so we can't confirm its lowest range or resolution. For sleep-current work, pick a meter whose maker publishes a µA range, such as the UT61E+ at 220.00 µA with 0.01 µA steps.

Which UNI-T meter is better for car diagnostics?+

Both read a 12.6 V battery to 0.01 V, which is all most car work needs. The UT61E+ is better for parasitic drain, since its 22 mA and 220 mA ranges show small currents finely. The UT139C adds temperature, handy for checking coolant sensor readings against the real engine temperature. Neither is a specialist automotive meter.

What battery does each UNI-T meter use?+

The UT139C runs on two 1.5 V R6 cells, the AA size, and the UT61E+ on four 1.5 V AAA cells. UNI-T doesn't publish a runtime for either meter. AA cells hold more charge than AAA as a rule, but without a stated runtime we can't say which meter lasts longer between changes.
