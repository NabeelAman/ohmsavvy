Rigol DHO914 vs Rigol DHO914S

Rigol DHO914 vs DHO914S is a one-feature decision: both are the same 125MHz, 12-bit, four-channel scope from Rigol's DHO900 series, with the same 1.25 GSa/s sample rate, 50 Mpts of memory and 7-inch touchscreen. The DHO914S adds a single-channel, 25MHz arbitrary/function generator and the Bode-plot sweep that runs on it, for $110 more at Rigol's own direct-sale price. Everything else — bandwidth, channels, memory, screen, optional 16-channel logic probe — is identical.

What $110 actually buys

Because the scope side is identical, the only numbers worth charting are the generator itself, the sticker price, and what the two scopes cost once you add the logic probe that turns either one into a mixed-signal instrument.

The generator isn't a serious bench AWG — single channel, 25MHz sine at best, and 10MHz for arbitrary waveforms at 156 MSa/s — but it's enough to drive a filter, feed a reference clock, or run the scope's own Bode-plot sweep without a second instrument on the bench. If you're also weighing bandwidth against sample rate on either model, our oscilloscope bandwidth and what GSa/s means explainers cover the scope side, which is where these two are the same.

What each one can and can't do

Rigol DHO914: a complete 12-bit scope, nothing to generate signals with

On the capture side the DHO914 does everything the DHO914S does: four 12-bit channels at 125MHz, up to 1.25 GSa/s, 50 Mpts of memory on one channel, standard UART/I2C/SPI/CAN/LIN decoding, and 16 digital channels if you add the PLA2216 probe. What it can't do is produce a signal of its own. There's no GEN OUT port, no arbitrary waveform output and no Bode-plot menu — if you need to drive a circuit under test, that has to come from something else on the bench.

Rigol DHO914S: the same capture, plus a modest signal source

The DHO914S's generator is useful but limited: one channel, sine to 25MHz, square/pulse to 15MHz, ramp to 150kHz, and arbitrary waveforms to 10MHz at 156 MSa/s and 14-bit vertical resolution. It won't replace a dedicated multi-channel AWG for serious signal work, but it's enough to feed a test tone into an amplifier, clock a digital input, or — paired with the scope's own channels — run the built-in Bode-plot sweep against a filter or a switching supply's feedback loop, which is the one job the DHO914 genuinely can't do at all, with or without an outside generator wired in.

The generator is a convenience, not a lab AWG

25MHz sine and a single channel put the DHO914S's generator well below a dedicated bench AWG. Treat it as a built-in test-tone and sweep source that saves a cable and a second box for light duty — reference clocks, filter checks, quick continuity-style signal injection — not as a replacement for a real signal generator if your work depends on one.

The PLA2216 probe costs more than the price gap between the two scopes

Rigol's PLA2216 16-channel logic probe is $399 at rigolna.com — more than three times the $110 difference between the DHO914 and DHO914S. If logic channels are the real goal, that purchase dwarfs whichever scope you start from; the generator decision is the smaller one.

The bottom line

The Rigol DHO914 and DHO914S are the same 125MHz, 12-bit, four-channel scope. Buy the DHO914S if you want a single-channel 25MHz generator and the built-in Bode-plot sweep for $110 more at Rigol's own price; buy the plain DHO914 if you already have a signal source or don't run swept frequency-response tests, and save that $110 — a gap that stays $110 even after adding the optional PLA2216 logic probe to either one.

Frequently asked questions

What's the actual difference between the Rigol DHO914 and DHO914S?+

One thing: a built-in signal generator. The DHO914S adds a single-channel, 25MHz arbitrary/function generator and the Bode-plot sweep that runs on it. Bandwidth, sample rate, memory, the touchscreen and the optional 16-channel logic probe are identical on both, per Rigol's own DHO900 datasheet.

Is the DHO914S's built-in generator good enough to replace a dedicated signal generator?+

For light duty, yes; for serious signal work, no. It's a single channel maxing out at 25MHz sine, 15MHz square/pulse, 150kHz ramp, or 10MHz arbitrary waveforms at 156 MSa/s and 14-bit. That covers test tones, reference clocks and driving a filter for a quick sweep, but it won't match a dedicated bench AWG's channel count, frequency range or modulation options.

Can the Rigol DHO914 run a Bode plot if I wire up an external generator?+

Not the automated version. Rigol's own product page states the Bode-plot feature belongs to the "S" models, meaning the sweep-and-plot function is tied to the internal generator rather than simply needing any signal source present. On the plain DHO914 you'd have to sweep an external generator manually and read gain and phase off the screen yourself.

Does the extra $110 for the DHO914S buy anything besides the generator?+

Not based on Rigol's published specs. Bandwidth, sample rate, memory depth, the 7-inch 1024×600 touchscreen, decoding protocols and the optional PLA2216 logic probe are the same figures on both models. The $110 premium is specifically for the generator and the Bode-plot feature it enables.

Which one should I buy if I might add the PLA2216 logic probe later?+

The PLA2216 is $399 on either scope, so the $110 gap between the DHO914 and DHO914S doesn't change once you add it: $1,058 total for the DHO914, $1,168 for the DHO914S. Decide on the generator and Bode plot on their own merits — the logic probe's cost is the same either way.
