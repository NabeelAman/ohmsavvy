Rigol DHO804 vs Rigol DS1054Z

Rigol DHO804 vs Rigol DS1054Z is old favorite against new generation. Both are four-channel Rigol scopes for hobbyists and students. The DHO804 moves to 12-bit vertical resolution, 70 MHz, a 1024 × 600 touchscreen and USB-C power; the DS1054Z keeps the familiar 8-bit, 50 MHz platform with optional serial decoding and a large community behind it.

Rigol DHO804 vs DS1054Z spec by spec

Both scopes share one sample-rate budget across their channels, so the figure that matters for four-channel work is the per-channel rate with everything switched on. Rigol's datasheets put the DHO804 ahead on every axis it publishes, from bandwidth to recording depth, with the normal-mode waveform rate the one tie.

Record time follows from memory divided by sample rate. With one channel on, the DHO804 holds 25 Mpts at 1.25 GSa/s, which is 20 ms of signal at full speed; a standard DS1054Z holds 12 Mpts at 1 GSa/s, or 12 ms. With all four channels on, the DHO804 keeps 5 Mpts at 312.5 MSa/s, which is 16 ms per channel.

What 12 bits buys you

An oscilloscope's converter splits the screen's vertical span into equal steps. Eight bits gives 256 steps, twelve gives 4,096. At 10 mV/div across eight divisions, the screen spans 80 mV, so the DS1054Z resolves about 0.31 mV per step and the DHO804 about 0.02 mV. That is the arithmetic; real noise sets the floor you can actually see.

When the extra bits help

Sixteen times as many levels means you can zoom into ripple, noise or a small sensor signal without the trace breaking into visible steps. For square-wave logic work the difference is far smaller. Use cases in practice

For Arduino and Raspberry Pi work, the deciding factor is usually serial decoding. Watching a UART or I2C bus as text rather than counting edges saves time, and on the DHO804 it costs nothing extra. Our guide to diagnosing a UART problem with an oscilloscope shows the workflow.

For audio and analog circuits, 12 bits and the extra rise-time margin make the DHO804 the cleaner tool. A 5 ns rise time against 7 ns matters little on audio itself, but helps on the switching edges of class-D amplifiers and buck converters. The DS1054Z still does the job; it just draws a coarser trace when you zoom in.

The DS1054Z's real advantage is familiarity. Its menus, screenshots and quirks fill a decade of forum threads and course notes, which helps if you learn by following along. Read our Rigol DS1054Z review for the full picture, or compare the two-channel DHO802 against the DS1054Z.

Safety: neither scope is for direct mains measurement

Both datasheets give a maximum input of CAT I 300 Vrms on the 1 MΩ input, which rules out probing mains wiring directly. The probe's ground clip is tied to earth through the power cord on the DS1054Z, so clipping it to a live point creates a short. Use a rated differential probe for mains or switch-mode primaries, and leave panel work to a licensed electrician.

The bottom line

For a new purchase, the DHO804 is the stronger scope: 12-bit resolution, more bandwidth, a faster four-channel sample rate, standard decoding and a modern touchscreen in a lighter body. The DS1054Z still makes sense if you want the most documented beginner scope and 50 MHz covers your work.

Frequently asked questions

Is the DHO804 a replacement for the DS1054Z?+

Rigol sells both. The DHO804 is a newer platform with 12-bit resolution, a 1024 × 600 touchscreen and standard decoding, while the DS1054Z remains in stock on Rigol's North American site. Treat the DHO804 as the modern alternative rather than a direct successor; the DS1054Z is still a capable four-channel scope.

Does the DHO804 have a built-in signal generator?+

No. Rigol's DHO800 specifications list no built-in generator or digital channels, and neither does the DS1054Z. If you need a generator in the same box, look at a model with one, or pair either scope with a separate function generator for filter and amplifier testing.

Which has more memory, the DHO804 or the DS1054Z?+

With one channel on, the DHO804 reaches 25 Mpts against the DS1054Z's 12 Mpts standard (24 Mpts optional). With all four channels on, the DHO804 drops to 5 Mpts, which still holds 16 ms at its 312.5 MSa/s four-channel rate. Rigol's 1000Z page doesn't break down the standard memory per channel count.

Can the DHO804 run from a battery?+

Rigol says it takes USB-C power at 15 V and 3 A, with a 45 W maximum, so it can run from a portable power source that supplies that profile. The DS1054Z is a conventional mains-powered benchtop. Check that a power bank supports 15 V output before relying on it in the field.

Is the DS1054Z 8-bit or 12-bit?+

8-bit. Rigol's DS1000Z datasheet lists 8 bits of vertical resolution for the analog channels, and a High Resolution acquisition mode that averages samples to reach up to 12 bits on repetitive signals. The DHO804 is 12-bit natively, which helps on single-shot captures and small signals where averaging isn't possible.
