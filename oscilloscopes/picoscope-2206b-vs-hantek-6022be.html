PicoScope 2206B vs Hantek 6022BE

PicoScope 2206B vs Hantek 6022BE sets a well-specified USB oscilloscope against the cheapest PC scope in our roundup. Both are two-channel, 8-bit and powered over USB with no screen of their own. The PicoScope adds 50 MHz, 500 MS/s, a 32 MS buffer, a built-in generator and Pico's PicoScope 7 software; the Hantek offers 20 MHz and 48 MS/s on Windows for very little money.

Spec by spec

Both devices are small boxes with two BNC inputs and a USB cable, so the difference is entirely in what sits behind those inputs. The figures below are from Pico's 2000 Series datasheet and Hantek's 6022BE spec table. On every number both makers publish, the PicoScope is ahead by a wide margin.

Sample rate and bandwidth work together. With one channel on, the PicoScope takes ten samples per cycle at its 50 MHz limit, enough to draw a recognizable sine; with both channels on, five. The Hantek's 48 MS/s gives 2.4 samples per cycle at 20 MHz, barely above the Nyquist minimum of two, so the 6022BE's usable range ends well before its rated bandwidth. For repetitive signals Pico also lists equivalent-time sampling (ETS) at 5 GS/s, which builds a waveform from many triggers. More on these specs in our bandwidth explainer and what GSa/s means.

The spacing between dots is simply one divided by the sample rate: about 20.8 ns on the Hantek and 4 ns on the PicoScope with both channels running (2 ns with one). A 20 MHz sine completes a cycle every 50 ns, so the Hantek lands only two or three points on each cycle and the software has to guess the rest. That is why the 6022BE can confirm a 20 MHz signal exists but can't tell you whether it is clean, clipped or ringing, while the 2206B still draws a usable shape at the same frequency.

What you can measure with each

Apply the 3–5× rule and the PicoScope covers signals up to roughly 10–17 MHz with good fidelity, while the Hantek's 20 MHz suggests 4–7 MHz. Because of its sample rate, the 6022BE is realistically a tool for signals in the low megahertz and below.

Jobs the Hantek 6022BE handles

Audio signals, 555 timers, PWM for fans and LEDs, the 50 Hz frame of a hobby servo signal and slow sensor outputs are all well inside its range. You can check whether a UART line is toggling and count its bits at 9600 baud with cursors. What you can't do is read the bytes as text: Hantek lists no decoding, only an edge trigger, and no AC coupling, so a small ripple on a 12 V rail needs an external blocking capacitor before the 20 mV/div range can show it.

Jobs where the PicoScope 2206B is worth the step up

An I2C or SPI bus running at a few megahertz, a CAN bus in a car project, or a UART exchange you want decoded into bytes: PicoScope 7 decodes these alongside the waveform. The 32 MS buffer matters for bursty traffic. At 500 MS/s on one channel it holds 64 ms of full-rate data, and shared across two channels at 250 MS/s it still covers 64 ms, long enough to capture a whole sensor transaction or a power-up sequence without dropping to a slow sample rate. The built-in AWG lets you inject a test tone into a filter or amplifier and watch the result on the second channel, without buying a separate generator, though at 1 MHz it is a low-frequency source.

Why memory depth decides your real sample rate

Capture time equals memory divided by sample rate. A scope with a short buffer must slow its sample rate to show a long time window, and fine detail disappears. With 32 MS, the PicoScope can keep sampling at hundreds of megasamples per second across tens of milliseconds. Hantek doesn't clearly state the 6022BE's buffer, so we can't calculate the same figure for it.

Both share your computer's ground

Neither maker's spec sheet we checked lists USB isolation, so treat each probe's ground clip as connected to your computer, and through a desktop PC or earthed charger to mains earth. Pico rates the 2206B inputs at ±20 V full scale with ±100 V overvoltage protection; Hantek's range tops out at 5 V/div. Never probe mains directly with either. Use an isolated, rated differential probe.

Choose the PicoScope 2206B if…

  • ✓You debug serial buses and want them decoded on screen (Pico lists CAN, I2C, SPI, UART, LIN and more)
  • ✓You want a signal source built in: a function generator and AWG up to 1 MHz
  • ✓You need long captures: 32 MS of buffer memory
  • ✓You work on macOS or Linux as well as Windows

Choose the Hantek 6022BE if…

  • ✓Your budget is the deciding factor and you only want to learn the basics
  • ✓You have a Windows PC and look at audio, timers and slow PWM
  • ✓You are happy to read waveforms and bits manually with cursors
  • ✓You want a spare scope you won't mind leaving in a toolbox

The bottom line

The PicoScope 2206B is the far more capable instrument: 50 MHz, up to 500 MS/s, a 32 MS buffer, a built-in generator, serial decoding and software for Windows, macOS and Linux, backed by a 5-year warranty. The Hantek 6022BE is a learning tool for slow signals on a Windows PC, and its very low price is the reason to buy it. If you already know you'll debug microcontroller buses, skip the stepping stone and buy the PicoScope.

Frequently asked questions

Is the PicoScope 2206B worth it over the Hantek 6022BE?+

If you'll use it beyond basic learning, yes. The 2206B has 2.5 times the bandwidth, over ten times the sample rate, a 32 MS buffer, a function generator and AWG, and serial decoding in PicoScope 7. The Hantek 6022BE suits someone who wants to see audio, timer and PWM waveforms on a Windows PC for the lowest possible outlay.

Can the Hantek 6022BE decode I2C, SPI or UART?+

Hantek lists no protocol decoding for the 6022BE: its software offers an edge trigger, FFT, basic math, X-Y mode and cursors. You can still see the bus waveforms and count bits manually at slow speeds. Pico's datasheet lists decoding for the 2206B, including CAN, I2C, SPI, UART, LIN and FlexRay.

Does the PicoScope 2206B have a signal generator?+

Yes. Pico lists a built-in function generator with sine, square, triangle, ramp, noise and other shapes from 0.03 Hz to 1 MHz, plus an arbitrary waveform generator with a 12-bit, 20 MS/s DAC and ±2 V output on a front-panel BNC. The Hantek 6022BE has no generator.

Which of these USB scopes works on a Mac?+

The PicoScope 2206B. Pico's PicoScope 7 software runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. Hantek lists only Windows versions (10, 8, 7, Vista and XP) for the 6022BE, so a Mac or Linux user would need a Windows machine or another scope.

Are probes included with the PicoScope 2206B?+

Pico's datasheet lists two x1/x10 probes, USB cables and a quick start guide in the kit. The Amazon listing we link doesn't spell out the box contents, so confirm with the seller before ordering if you don't already own probes.
