Rigol DS1102Z-E vs Hantek DSO5102P

Rigol DS1102Z-E vs Hantek DSO5102P looks like a tie on the listing: two channels, 100 MHz, 1 GSa/s, a 3.5 ns rise time and a 7-inch 800 × 480 screen on both. The spec sheets diverge on two things that matter for real measurements. Rigol's vertical scale goes down to 500 µV/div against Hantek's 2 mV/div, and its memory reaches 24 Mpts against 40K points. This page focuses on the small-signal side: ripple, noise and sensor outputs, where those extra ranges show up on screen.

  • →Memory: Rigol's datasheet lists 24 Mpts on one channel (its series page says 12M standard); Hantek lists 40K on its product page and 24K in its datasheet.
  • →Decoding and triggers: Rigol lists parallel, UART, I2C and SPI decoding plus runt, window, Nth-edge and setup/hold triggers. Hantek lists six trigger types and no decoding.

Spec by spec

Bandwidth, sample rate and screen are identical, so they aren't charted. These are the rows where the two 100 MHz benches differ in a way you'll notice.

Reading millivolt signals on each scope

An 8-bit scope splits the screen height into 256 levels. A trace that only fills a quarter of the screen uses about 64 of them, so it looks steppy and noisy. Filling more of the screen gives a small signal more levels, and that depends on how far down the vertical scale goes.

The 10X probe catch

Both makers quote their smallest range with a 1X probe. Switch to 10X, which you need for full bandwidth, and the floor rises tenfold: 5 mV/div on the Rigol, 20 mV/div on the Hantek. For a 20 mVpp ripple at 10X, that is the difference between four divisions and one. For ripple work at 10X, the DS1102Z-E is simply the easier scope to read.

What each one can and can't measure

Rigol DS1102Z-E: the better small-signal and debug bench

Its 500 µV/div floor, 24 Mpts memory and listed triggers (runt, window, Nth edge, setup/hold, plus RS232/UART, I2C and SPI) make it a capable two-channel debug tool. Rigol's math menu includes low-pass, high-pass, band-pass and band-stop filters, which help pull ripple out of noise. Its limits are two channels, an 8-bit converter (12 bits only in High Resolution mode, which averages) and a fan you will hear in a quiet room.

Hantek DSO5102P: fine at volts, weak at millivolts

On logic, PWM and audio at line level the DSO5102P draws the same 100 MHz picture as the Rigol, with a 1024-point FFT and 32 automatic measurements. Below about 10 mV it runs out of vertical range, especially at 10X, and its 40K record limits long captures. Hantek lists no decoding and no runt or window triggers.

Ripple on mains-powered supplies

Measure ripple on the low-voltage output side only. Both scopes ground the probe return to mains earth, so clipping it to the primary side of a switch-mode supply shorts that node to earth. Rigol rates the DS1102Z-E's inputs at CAT I 300 Vrms and CAT II 100 Vrms; Hantek lists 300 Vrms through a 10X probe. Use a differential probe on anything mains-referenced.

The trust question: memory figures on both sides

Both makers contradict themselves on memory. Rigol's DS1000Z-E datasheet lists 24 Mpts standard, while the series page our Pick records says 12M standard. Hantek's DSO5000P product page lists 40K and its datasheet 24K. Even taking the lower Rigol figure and the higher Hantek figure, the DS1102Z-E holds 300 times more points. Rigol also states a three-year warranty on the mainframe; we found no warranty term in the Hantek documents we used.

The DS1102Z-E's other pairings are Rigol DS1102Z-E vs DS1202Z-E, Rigol DS1102Z-E vs Hantek DSO2D15, Rigol DS1102Z-E vs DS1054Z and Rigol DS1102Z-E vs Siglent SDS1102CML+. For the Hantek, see Hantek DSO5102P vs DSO2D10 and Hantek DSO5102P vs DSO2D15. Our guide to measuring ripple voltage covers probe setup for this kind of work.

Choose the Rigol DS1102Z-E if…

  • ✓You measure supply ripple, noise or sensor signals in the low millivolts
  • ✓You want UART, I2C, SPI or parallel decoding without an add-on
  • ✓You hunt glitches and want runt, window, Nth-edge and setup/hold triggers
  • ✓You want up to 60,000 frames of waveform recording to replay later

Choose the Hantek DSO5102P if…

  • ✓Your signals are volts, not millivolts: logic, PWM, audio line levels
  • ✓You want a fanless bench (Hantek lists convection cooling)
  • ✓You trigger from the AC line or need the EXT/5 input
  • ✓You want the lighter scope: 2.08 kg against 2.9 kg

Neither if…

You need to resolve microvolt-level detail on top of a larger signal. Both are 8-bit; a 12-bit scope such as the Rigol DHO802 gives 16 times finer steps across the same screen.

The bottom line

Buy the Rigol DS1102Z-E if you measure anything in millivolts or debug serial buses: 500 µV/div, up to 24 Mpts, standard UART, I2C, SPI and parallel decoding, and a three-year warranty. The Hantek DSO5102P is a reasonable volt-level bench scope that is lighter, fanless and syncs to the AC line, but it lists no decoding and its 2 mV/div floor limits ripple and sensor work.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better for measuring power supply ripple?+

The Rigol DS1102Z-E. Its smallest range is 500 µV/div with a 1X probe and 5 mV/div at 10X, against 2 mV/div and 20 mV/div on the Hantek DSO5102P. A typical few-millivolt ripple fills four times more of the Rigol's screen, which makes peak-to-peak readings and noise far easier to judge.

Do both scopes have 100 MHz bandwidth?+

Yes. Rigol lists the DS1102Z-E at 100 MHz with a calculated 3.5 ns rise time, and Hantek lists the DSO5102P at 100 MHz with 3.5 ns typical at the BNC. Both sample at up to 1 GSa/s. Bandwidth won't decide this comparison; sensitivity, memory and decoding do.

Is the Hantek DSO5102P quieter than the Rigol?+

On paper, yes. Hantek lists convection cooling for the DSO5000P series, which means no fan, while Rigol's DS1000Z-E datasheet lists fan cooling. We haven't measured either, so we can't give noise levels, but a convection-cooled scope makes no fan noise by design.

Does the DS1102Z-E come with probes?+

Rigol's datasheet lists two PVP3150 passive probes as standard accessories for the DS1102Z-E, rated 150 MHz. The 200 MHz DS1202Z-E ships with 350 MHz PVP2350 probes instead. Check the Amazon listing's box contents before you buy, since resellers sometimes bundle differently.

Can the DSO5102P decode serial data?+

Hantek lists no serial triggering or decoding for the DSO5102P. The Rigol DS1102Z-E's datasheet lists triggers for RS232/UART, I2C and SPI and decoding of those three plus parallel buses, on up to two buses at once. If you work with microcontrollers, that alone points to the Rigol.
