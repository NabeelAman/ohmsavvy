Siglent SDS1104X-U vs Rigol DHO804: Which Four-Channel Scope?

Siglent SDS1104X-U vs Rigol DHO804 pits a 100 MHz, 8-bit mains bench scope against a 70 MHz, 12-bit USB-C touchscreen scope, both with four channels. The surprise is in the four-channel numbers: the lower-bandwidth DHO804 samples faster with all four on, 312.5 MSa/s against 250 MSa/s. The Siglent answers with 30 MHz more bandwidth, a faster normal update rate and LIN decoding.

Spec by spec

Bandwidth and update rate go to the Siglent. Resolution and sample rate go to the Rigol.

With four channels on, the SDS1104X-U samples a 30 MHz signal about 8 times per cycle and the DHO804 about 10. Both are adequate. The Siglent's extra bandwidth shows on that 30 MHz signal as more of its true amplitude, since 30 MHz is under a third of 100 MHz but nearly half of 70 MHz. Pick by the signals you actually measure, and use the oscilloscope buying guide if you aren't sure yet.

What each one can and can't measure

Siglent SDS1104X-U: speed headroom on a classic layout

The X-U is comfortable up to roughly 20 to 33 MHz by the three-to-five-times rule, and its 100,000 wfm/s normal update rate makes persistence displays fill quickly when you look for rare events. Siglent includes CAN and LIN decode as standard. Its 8-bit converter and 1 mV/div floor make millivolt signals coarse: at 10 mV/div each step is about 0.31 mV. Siglent's application note also confirms it has a single converter for four channels, so four channels share 1 GSa/s.

Rigol DHO804: the small-signal four-channel scope

At 70 MHz the DHO804 is comfortable to about 14 to 23 MHz, which covers most hobby clocks and buses. Below that it shows more: 12-bit steps of about 0.02 mV at 10 mV/div, a magnified 500 µV/div scale, and 312.5 MSa/s per channel with all four on. Rigol's 500,000-frame recording helps replay intermittent events, and the 1024 × 600 touchscreen and HDMI output make it easy to show a waveform to someone else. It runs from a 15 V USB-C supply.

Count how often you'll use all four channels

If you plan to run four channels most of the time, the DHO804's 312.5 MSa/s per channel beats the Siglent's 250, and its 12 bits help on every channel. If you mostly use one or two channels on fast signals, the Siglent's bandwidth is the bigger asset.

USB-C power doesn't mean isolated inputs

The DHO804's probe grounds share the ground of whatever powers it, and Rigol rates its inputs CAT I. The SDS1104X-U ties its grounds to mains earth. Neither is safe for direct mains measurement; use a differential probe, and get an electrician for anything inside a breaker panel.

The question with the DHO804: 70 MHz for the long run

The DHO804 is the scope our owner tested, and nothing here contradicts the badge; this page uses Rigol's published numbers only. Its long-term risk is bandwidth. 70 MHz covers today's hobby buses, but if your projects move toward faster microcontrollers or high-speed SPI, a 25 MHz clock sits past its comfortable range. The same 12-bit platform comes in 100 MHz as the DHO814 if you want both.

See Rigol DHO814 vs DHO804 for that step, and Rigol DHO804 vs SDS1104X-E for the DHO804 against Siglent's two-converter model. The X-U's place in Siglent's range is on Siglent SDS1104X-U vs SDS1104X-E, and the two-channel DHO is on Rigol DHO804 vs DHO802.

  • ✓Your clocks and edges sit between about 15 and 30 MHz
  • ✓You want 100,000 wfm/s in normal mode for glitch hunting
  • ✓You work with LIN as well as CAN
  • ✓You want physical buttons and a fixed mains bench

Neither if…

You need a built-in signal generator or logic channels. Neither has them; the Rigol DHO914S adds both on Rigol's 12-bit platform.

The bottom line

Buy the Siglent SDS1104X-U if your signals run between about 15 and 30 MHz, you hunt glitches with persistence, or you need LIN: it has 100 MHz, 100,000 wfm/s and CAN and LIN decode as standard. Buy the Rigol DHO804 if you measure small signals or run all four channels often: 12-bit resolution, 312.5 MSa/s per channel with four on, a touchscreen and a 1.78 kg USB-C body, and it is the one our owner has tested.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the 70 MHz DHO804 sample faster than the 100 MHz SDS1104X-U?+

Bandwidth and sample rate are separate specs. Rigol lists 1.25 GSa/s for the DHO804, dropping to 312.5 MSa/s with four channels on. Siglent lists 1 GSa/s for the X-U and, in its application note, one converter shared by four channels, which gives 250 MSa/s each. The Siglent's front end passes faster signals; the Rigol samples more often.

Is 8-bit a problem on the SDS1104X-U?+

Not for timing, logic and most general work. It shows on small signals: at 10 mV/div each step is about 0.31 mV, so a 1 mV ripple is drawn in a few levels. If supply ripple, audio noise or sensor outputs are a big part of your work, the DHO804's 12 bits help.

Which one decodes more serial buses?+

They overlap. Siglent lists I2C, SPI, UART, CAN and LIN as standard on the SDS1104X-U. Rigol lists parallel, RS232/UART, I2C, SPI and CAN as standard on the DHO804. Choose the Siglent for LIN and the Rigol for parallel bus decoding.

Can the DHO804 run from a power bank?+

Rigol lists a USB Type-C power input at 15 V and 3 A, 45 W maximum. A USB-C PD power bank that supplies a 15 V profile at 3 A can run it. Many smaller banks top out at 9 V or lower current, so check the output label first. The SDS1104X-U needs 100 to 240 V AC.

Which is better for a first oscilloscope?+

Both suit a first scope. The DHO804's touchscreen, 12-bit traces and small case make it easy to live with, and our owner has tested it. The SDS1104X-U is the better pick if you already know your projects involve clocks above about 15 MHz, or you prefer knobs and buttons.
