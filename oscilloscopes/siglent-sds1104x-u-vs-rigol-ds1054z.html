Siglent SDS1104X-U vs Rigol DS1054Z: Budget Four-Channel Scopes

Siglent SDS1104X-U vs Rigol DS1054Z is the budget four-channel question. Both are 8-bit, both have a 7-inch 800 × 480 screen, and both drop to exactly 250 MSa/s per channel with all four on. The SDS1104X-U brings 100 MHz instead of 50 MHz, a 100,000 wfm/s update rate and CAN and LIN decoding as standard. The DS1054Z brings deeper memory on one or two channels, a huge base of learning material and our owner's hands-on time.

Same converter resolution, same screen, same sample rates. The charts show where they part.

The update-rate chart matters more than it looks. A scope is blind between captures, and at 100,000 waveforms per second it looks at the signal over three times as often as at 30,000. For a glitch that happens once in thousands of cycles, that raises the odds of seeing it with persistence on. See how to use persistence to find glitches.

What each one can and can't measure

Siglent SDS1104X-U: more headroom, fewer extras

100 MHz is comfortable to roughly 20 to 33 MHz by the three-to-five-times rule, so faster SPI and modern microcontroller signals stay recognizable. Siglent includes I2C, SPI, UART, CAN and LIN trigger and decode, a hardware pass/fail test at up to 40,000 decisions a second and 80,000-segment sequence mode. Its limit is that one converter serves all four channels, per Siglent's own application note, so four channels share 1 GSa/s as 250 MSa/s each. Siglent also lists no logic option, web control or Bode plot for the X-U.

Rigol DS1054Z: the 50 MHz classic

50 MHz is comfortable up to about 10 to 17 MHz. That covers UART, I2C, PWM, servo signals, audio and an Arduino clock at 16 MHz with its edges softened. With one channel on, Rigol's data sheet gives it 24 Mpts, the deepest one-channel memory of the two. Its 30,000 wfms/s update rate is a third of the Siglent's, and Rigol's own pages disagree on whether serial decode is standard. It is also the heavier of the two: 3.2 kg against 2.6 kg.

With four channels on, bandwidth past 50 MHz has little to work with

At 250 MSa/s a 100 MHz signal gets 2.5 samples per cycle, too few to draw it well. So with all four channels on, the SDS1104X-U's practical gain is in the 15 to 40 MHz range, where its front end passes signals the DS1054Z shrinks and 250 MSa/s still gives six or more samples per cycle. Turn channels off to use the rest.

Both are earthed bench scopes

Siglent lists 400 Vpk maximum input and Rigol CAT I 300 Vrms. Neither is a mains-measurement tool through a standard probe, and both tie probe grounds to earth. Use a differential probe for mains-referenced circuits.

The question with the SDS1104X-U: what was cut

Siglent is open about it. Its application note lists what the X-U drops against the SDS1104X-E: one converter instead of two (250 against 500 MSa/s with four on), one memory module instead of two, a 1 mV/div floor instead of 500 µV/div, no Bode plot, no logic option, no web control and a 128 kpts FFT instead of 1 Mpt. None of that makes it worse than the DS1054Z, which shares its four-channel sample rate and 1 mV/div floor. It does mean the SDS1104X-E is the Siglent to buy if any of those matter to you.

Choose the Siglent SDS1104X-U if…

  • ✓Your clocks reach 15 to 30 MHz
  • ✓You hunt intermittent glitches and want a fast update rate
  • ✓You want CAN and LIN decode included, not optional
  • ✓You want a 2.6 kg scope rather than 3.2 kg

Choose the Rigol DS1054Z if…

  • ✓Your signals stay under about 10 MHz
  • ✓You want the most tutorials, courses and forum help for your model
  • ✓You capture long records on one or two channels (24 or 12 Mpts per Rigol's data sheet)
  • ✓You want the scope our owner has tested

Neither if…

You need fine detail on small signals such as millivolt ripple. Both are 8-bit; a 12-bit scope such as the Rigol DHO804 or Siglent SDS814X HD resolves 16 times finer steps.

The bottom line

Buy the Siglent SDS1104X-U if your signals reach beyond 10 MHz or you hunt rare glitches: 100 MHz, 100,000 wfm/s and CAN and LIN decode as standard, in a 2.6 kg case. Buy the Rigol DS1054Z if your work is Arduino-class logic, PWM and audio and you value deep one-channel memory and the largest pool of tutorials; it matches the X-U's 250 MSa/s with four channels on, and it is the one our owner has tested.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Siglent SDS1104X-U better than the Rigol DS1054Z?+

On bandwidth, update rate and standard decoding, yes: 100 MHz against 50 MHz, 100,000 against 30,000 waveforms per second, and CAN and LIN included. The DS1054Z has deeper memory with one or two channels on and far more learning material. With four channels on they sample at the same 250 MSa/s.

Can the DS1054Z be upgraded to 100 MHz?+

Rigol's DS1054Z product page lists no bandwidth option, and we don't cover unofficial modifications. Rigol sells 100 MHz four-channel models in the same family, such as the DS1104Z-S Plus. If you need 100 MHz, buy a scope rated for it.

Why do both drop to 250 MSa/s with four channels?+

Siglent's application note says the SDS1104X-U has one analog-to-digital converter shared by all four channels, so 1 GSa/s becomes 250 MSa/s each. Rigol's DS1000Z data sheet lists the same 1 GSa/s, 500 MSa/s and 250 MSa/s steps for the DS1054Z. Turning off unused channels restores the higher rates on both.

Which one has better serial decoding?+

The SDS1104X-U, on paper. Siglent lists I2C, SPI, UART, CAN and LIN trigger and decode as standard. Rigol's DS1000Z data sheet lists parallel, RS232/UART, I2C and SPI, while Rigol's product page calls bus decoding an option. Check the options screen on any DS1054Z you buy.

Are the screens the same?+

On paper, yes: both makers list a 7-inch TFT LCD at 800 × 480 with intensity grading. Neither is a touchscreen. Siglent's has 10 one-button shortcut keys; the DS1054Z uses Rigol's menu and soft-key layout. Choose on the other differences rather than the display.
