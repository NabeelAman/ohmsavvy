Fluke 1587 FC vs Fluke 87V: Which Tester Do You Need?

You need the Fluke 1587 FC if you test insulation: it applies 50 to 1000 V DC to a de-energized motor, cable or transformer winding, reads up to 2 GΩ, and runs PI and DAR ratio tests logged to your phone. You need the Fluke 87V if you want the more precise everyday multimeter: ±(0.05% + 1) DC volts against ±(0.09% + 2), True RMS AC, a 10 A current input, 200 kHz frequency and 250 µs peak capture. The 1587 FC has no 10 A input at all, and the 87V cannot test insulation. Many motor technicians end up carrying both.

Two different jobs

These two Flukes rarely compete. A multimeter asks "what is the circuit doing right now?" with the power on. An insulation tester asks "will this winding or cable hold up when the power comes back?" with the power off. The 1587 FC answers the second question and keeps a capable meter on board; the 87V answers the first question more precisely and doesn't touch the second.

How an insulation test works

Good winding insulation still leaks a tiny current. Apply a known DC voltage between the copper and the frame, measure that leakage, and Ohm's law gives the insulation resistance. The 1587 FC sources 1 mA when the load is at the bottom of each range (for example 1 mA into 1 MΩ at 1000 V), and far less through healthy insulation. Moisture, carbon dust, oil and cracked varnish all give the current an easier path, so the megohm reading falls.

Why can't the 87V do this? Its ohms function drives a small test current at a low voltage, chosen so it won't damage components. Insulation that reads "OL" at a few volts can still break down at the 480 V a motor sees in service, plus the spikes riding on it. Only a test at or above working voltage reveals that weakness, which is why the 1587 FC offers 500 V and 1000 V test levels.

PI and DAR: why the trend matters more than one number

A single megohm reading moves with temperature and humidity, so it's hard to judge on its own. Ratio tests fix that. Clean, dry insulation keeps absorbing charge for minutes, so its resistance climbs over the test; wet or contaminated insulation leaks steadily, so the reading stays flat. Safety: insulation testing puts high voltage on purpose

An insulation tester generates up to 1000 V DC on its own, so treat the leads as live the whole time the test runs. Before testing, de-energize the equipment, lock and tag it out, and prove it dead with a meter on a known live source before and after. Disconnect the motor or cable at both ends so the test voltage can't reach drives, capacitors, surge protectors or electronics, which it can destroy. Keep everyone clear of the far end of a cable under test. Afterward, let the 1587 FC discharge the circuit (Fluke quotes under 0.5 s for 1 µF or less; long cables can hold more) and confirm zero volts before touching conductors. The 30 V lockout is a backstop, not a substitute for lockout/tagout. If the equipment is part of a service or utility feed, call a qualified electrician.

Frequently asked questions

Can the Fluke 87V test insulation resistance?+

No. The 87V's resistance function tops out at 50 MΩ and uses a low test voltage, so it can't stress insulation the way a megohmmeter does. Weak insulation can read open at a few volts and still fail at 480 V. For winding and cable insulation tests you need a tester like the 1587 FC, which applies 50 to 1000 V DC.

Can the Fluke 1587 FC measure amps?+

Only small currents. Fluke lists 60 mA and 400 mA ranges, AC and DC, protected by a 0.44 A fuse, with a 600 mA overload allowed for two minutes. There is no 10 A input, so it can't read a motor's running current in series. Use a clamp meter for that, or carry an 87V, which reads to 10 A.

What test voltage should I use on a 480 V motor?+

Common practice, based on IEEE 43 guidance, is a 500 V DC test for motors rated up to about 1000 V, with 1000 V DC used for some higher-rated equipment. The 1587 FC offers 50, 100, 250, 500 and 1000 V. Always check the motor maker's instructions first, and never test with drives or electronics still connected.

What is a good PI reading?+

A polarization index of 2.0 or higher is the guideline commonly cited from IEEE 43 for most modern motor insulation, while readings near 1.0 suggest moisture or contamination. PI is the 10-minute reading divided by the 1-minute reading. Trends matter more than one result, which is why the 1587 FC's Fluke Connect logging is useful.

Is the Fluke 1587 FC accurate enough as an everyday multimeter?+

For most field work, yes. Fluke lists ±(0.09% + 2) on DC volts, ±(1% + 3) on AC volts at 50–60 Hz, a low-pass filter, capacitance to 9,999 µF and temperature to 537 °C. The 87V is tighter, at ±(0.05% + 1) DC and ±(0.7% + 2) AC, and adds a hi-res mode and 10 A input.

Does the Fluke 1587 FC need Fluke Connect to work?+

No. Insulation tests and meter functions run on the instrument itself. Fluke Connect adds phone features: saving readings in the app, TrendIt graphs of PI and DAR tests, temperature compensation and sharing results. Fluke lists asset history and trending as Fluke Connect Assets, sold separately, so check what your plan needs before relying on it.
